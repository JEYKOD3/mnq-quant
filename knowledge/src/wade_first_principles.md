## 1. The whole method in one paragraph

Institutions move the market; retail follows their footprints. Markets move in pairs of twos because of human behaviour: traders try something once, fail, try again, and give up after the second failure ("90% chance they will completely abandon their decision", LCm3VvhkQxI). So the one setup is the **second entry of a two-legged pullback, with the trend, at a key entry point**, triggered one tick beyond a good signal bar, stop one tick beyond the other side of that bar, never moved, target about 1:1 (a scalp of 8 to 10 ticks on ES), with a runner left on at breakeven and trailed under each swing. Every other rule exists to decide whether that setup is allowed right now. (core)

Four-step filter, in order, every time: **bias (market structure) -> key entry point -> high probability setup -> signal bar quality and no congestion**. "You just decide if rules are being met and either take the setup or don't." Restated as "three factors" in MJTge-KU9_o and RpdD-Tifcok: (1) overall market structure = current bias, (2) is there a proper key entry point, (3) what is the behaviour at the key entry point (is there a two-legged pullback). And as the order of confirmation in sat588vKgJw: "market context, key entry point, price action pattern, and the candlestick pattern is the last piece of confirmation." (core)

"The biggest breakthrough in my trading career was not just focusing on the patterns themselves but trying to identify the current market structure" (oYeSn3HMnkI). "We are not pattern traders, we rely on the overall market structure" (L6SD9duXkww).

## 2. The five price action rules (video 1)

1. **Trendline rule.** (core) After price closes outside a trendline or channel, expect a correction and then, "8 or 9 times out of 10" / "90%" / "9 out of 10 times", a retest and new extreme of the old trend. A break is not a reversal ("buying this breakout will just sucker people in", dMkdZVtU-9o). Only after the new extreme do you look for a correction or reversal. Applies to the big channel and to every short-term correction channel, down to the tiniest micro trendline. After the new extreme: "either market can go into correction for let's say like 30 minutes at least or market can straight up reverse" (O1sIYq9Vxl8). The attempt at a new extreme counts when a key entry point blocks the full new extreme: "close is close enough in price action" (OQNuBnPOqlk, LCm3VvhkQxI, qo0ebM_yyZY, ayMhgoErg0A, v5HqLD8kcRc, MJTge-KU9_o) (core). A new low "just by one single tick" counts (v5HqLD8kcRc). A double top or double bottom can be the attempt: "sometimes you will just get a double top or double bottom as the attempt by traders to make a new extreme and a lot of times that will be enough" (xYI4QLdhsAU, mbzdZ-JH64k, 7l4ruYYgp-c).
2. **No counter-trend trading.** (core) No tops or bottoms, no matter how good the bar looks ("avoid counter-trending regardless how good the signal bar is", sat588vKgJw). Trade the other way only after a proven reversal pattern (section 6). "With-trend pullbacks are designed to look weak, counter-trend entries are designed to look strong." He himself does not take counter-trend scalps: "I always follow the main bias, I don't want to over complicate my trading" (viVjkkB0TuE).
3. **Trading range rule.** (core) Most breakouts fail: "~90%", "one breakout out of 10 will eventually work", "at least temporarily", "prices will pull back into trading range 90% of the time". Never trade the breakout; trade the breakout pullback or fade the breakout. In a range, buy low, sell high, avoid the middle ("no man's land"), stay out of congestion. Watch for bullish or bearish imbalance inside the range to guess which breakout will work.
4. **High probability setup rule.** (core) The only setups: (a) second entry with the trend at a key entry point, (b) failed second entry against the trend, (c) failed breakout, (d) higher-low / lower-high confirmation, (e) double top / double bottom, (f) "double trap" (trapped traders at two key entry points), (g) breakout pullback only when it is also a second entry at a key entry point. A setup away from a key entry point is not a setup: "you cannot have a high probability setup far away from key entry points at the very top of the moves" (OQNuBnPOqlk).
5. **Signal bar rule.** (core) Longs only above a bullish bar, shorts only below a bearish bar ("you can't enter below this bullish bar, you have to enter below this bearish bar", xYI4QLdhsAU, RpdD-Tifcok, Pf9T9ZGauLY, Q4UFvxWB0AU, Ajq4FELRa1s). The bar must show direction and momentum; never a doji; wait for the bar to close ("never sneak in early"; "focus on the close of the bars, not to trade when the bar is in the middle", Ajq4FELRa1s). Stronger context = less perfect bar acceptable (section 13).

## 3. Counting entries (the mechanical core)

This is the part that becomes code with no interpretation.

- **Where the count starts.** (core) For longs: at the highest high of the most recent move. For shorts: at the lowest low. "Where the trend is starting, that's when we start counting." "I'm always going to reset the count at each new low" and also at "another significant low" (Pf9T9ZGauLY). In ranges: "if you have small uptrend in a trading range, you're going to reset the count at the high looking for a pullback to go long; if you have a small downtrend, you're going to reset the count at the low" (Pf9T9ZGauLY).
- **Trigger.** (core) "Entry is when the bar breaks the previous bar's high just by one tick" (longs), or the low by one tick (shorts). The first entry triggers on whichever bar is actually broken by one tick, "we didn't trigger above this bullish bar, we triggered above this bearish bar one tick" (IdgvwfhWhHU). Technical vs practical: a first entry short can technically trigger below a bullish bar, "but as a trader since the market is down, I want to sell below bearish bar" (Pf9T9ZGauLY).
- **Leg.** A bullish leg is a run of bars making higher highs and higher lows, "or even one bullish bar"; bar colour inside it does not matter. It ends the moment a bar trades one tick below the low of the last bar of the leg. Mirror for bearish legs.
- **First entry.** Counting from the extreme, the first bar whose high is taken by one tick (looking for longs) is the first entry long. He does not trade it: "first entries are 50/50", "first entries are not reliable" (core). While a correction works higher and no bar gets a break below its low, there is no entry yet (Pf9T9ZGauLY). If a first entry keeps working with no pullback, there is no second entry; wait for the next count (Mu8XCswoe1Q, Pf9T9ZGauLY).
- **Pullback between entries.** "After first entry short, a bar that is going to get a break above its high is going to create pullback followed by another bar that gets a break below its low. It's creating second attempt" (Pf9T9ZGauLY). The first entry fails when price breaks the opposite side of the first-entry bar.
- **Second entry.** (core) After the first entry fails (price makes a lower push without breaking the start of the count), the next bar whose high is taken by one tick is the second entry long. That is the two-legged pullback. This is the trade. "Hidden" legs: "sometimes the two legs are going to be clearly visible, sometimes they're going to be hidden, but you can sell either way if you are at the key entry point, you're in a downtrend and you have good signal bar" (Pf9T9ZGauLY). First leg may be longer and the second smaller, "it doesn't matter."
- **Reset rules.** (core) The count restarts (a) at every new extreme, even by one tick ("Does the count reset? Yes", TCeNTRKpe9s); (b) when a swing breaks the previous pivot's high (uptrend) or low (downtrend); (c) at double tops and double bottoms, even to the tick ("micro double top resets the count", MJTge-KU9_o, Pf9T9ZGauLY, NNzCegosXu8, L7G19gc2C9Y, Mu8XCswoe1Q, Juyy-5cLfBk); (d) after a "significant event" such as a first or second entry on the other side playing out; (e) at matching highs when counting counter-trend failures ("I started a count over from this bar because all these highs are matching", ayMhgoErg0A).
- **Pivot definition.** A pivot exists only when a bullish move is followed by a bearish move that triggers one tick below a prior bar's low. No tick below, no pivot, no restart.
- **Trend end.** "It remains a downtrend until the bar or the move breaks the previous pivot's high."
- **Nesting.** Two-legged moves sit inside bigger two-legged moves ("two legged pullbacks inside of two legged pullbacks", v5HqLD8kcRc). He counts only where he expects a setup: at a key entry point, in the direction of the bias. "I'm not looking for two-legged moves all over the chart."
- **Measured legs.** (core) Sometimes leg 2 equals leg 1 in size, sometimes not; "the legs I guarantee you they're almost measured" (mbzdZ-JH64k); "second leg up to the very tick" (v5HqLD8kcRc). He measures leg 1 (swing high to swing low) and projects it from the end of the correction to anticipate where the setup will appear, where the second leg will end, and where a runner is likely to go (vHaDDLX9lOc). Only measure legs in the direction you intend to fade. A measured overshoot gives "my first target" (9y2hp3sMRHg).
- **Edge cases he states.** A first entry can be stopped and the second entry trigger on the same bar. An inside bar in the second leg is itself a key entry point (stop one tick below the entire swing). A high "so close" to the prior high may not be accepted as a new extreme; "the attempt is often enough to count as the new high" when it happens at a key entry point. Counting can be done "a little bit differently" and still give a second entry (v5HqLD8kcRc); an alternative count from a matching-highs area is "advanced" (Juyy-5cLfBk).
- **Third entries do not exist.** "A failed third entry long, that's really not a thing, not a reliable setup." Exception stated once: a technically third entry short at two key entry points with a beautiful reversal bar after a micro break and new high "is a form of a second entry"; "if it works once, very similar pattern, it's most likely going to work twice" (Ajq4FELRa1s).
- **Dual counting at double tops/bottoms.** (core) The same bar can be read as a failed second entry long and a second entry short (count from the previous low as a second entry, or from the new low as a first entry). "Two high quality setups in one." He trades it when both readings agree with the bias (rmmLXeYivk8, ebULiDW6O70, Pf9T9ZGauLY, MJTge-KU9_o, mS1920Y-o3Q).
- **Counting counter-trend to catch traps.** (core) In a downtrend, reset the long count at the highest point of each correction near the key entry point; new high -> first entry long -> pullback -> second entry long; when it fails (break below the second-entry-long bar) it is the failed second entry long and the short entry ("I want to use trapped bulls to gain advantage", Pf9T9ZGauLY). He wants that failure to happen before price exceeds the low he is using for the short count. A lower high is a failed first entry long, "a little bit more advanced trade."
- **Two legs to a new extreme.** After a trendline break, one leg to a new extreme may still carry momentum; after two legs to the new extreme "there is at least a correction or maybe a straight-up reversal", so stop trading in that direction.
- **Hidden moves (advanced).** A two-legged pullback hidden inside what counts technically as a first entry (the intermediate bars never triggered; a lower timeframe would show two entries). Use only when every other factor is met: trend, key entry point, prior correction played out, good bar. "95% of your trading, even more, is going to be nice clear second entries"; proper second entries "are going to be there 98% of the time" (_YuPbI4FE2M, d0aud04jpag).
- **First entry exceptions.** (core) He takes a first entry only when: it is a lower high / higher low at two key entry points with a strong reversal bar while the trendline rule still expects a new extreme (LCm3VvhkQxI); the trendline has been bounced many times in a very strong trend ("you cannot ignore how many times you already bounced the trendline, you have to take a chance", DVNSeqUS3Lc); a very strong "crawling" trend gives no clean second entries ("you will be forced to take any pullback you can and it's going to be first entry", _YuPbI4FE2M); a clearly defined triple-top resistance plus a gigantic leg down plus EMA holding to the T ("a little bit aggressive, you may skip it", mbzdZ-JH64k); a strictly bullish structure at two key entry points (hWjUp_Df_t0); a two-leg pullback to the spike-and-channel line plus EMA that is "technically just a first entry" because of count resets (TCeNTRKpe9s, "beginners don't listen to me"). Otherwise first entries are ignored by rule: "I cannot break the rule. Even though this worked, this is just a first entry" (9y2hp3sMRHg).

## 4. Key entry points

- (core) Trendline or channel line, support/resistance (prior swing highs/lows, range edges, prior day levels, prior session close, high and low of the day), and the **21-bar EMA**. "Two key entry points" (EMA plus trendline, or EMA plus level) is the best case; "the more key entry points you have the better" (Mu8XCswoe1Q, TCeNTRKpe9s, sat588vKgJw). Three coinciding (trendline + EMA + old resistance as support) turns a breakout pullback from "50/50" into "80 or 90% probability" (vHaDDLX9lOc).
- "The biggest trend line is the strongest key entry point and takes precedence" (OQNuBnPOqlk).
- **EMA is a supportive tool only** (core): direction of slope = bias; "as long as prices stay below EMA, you're thinking bearish, and once prices push above EMA, something is changing" (9y2hp3sMRHg). Price far from the EMA ("multiple points") snaps back ("EMA acts as a magnet", core), so entries far from the EMA are skipped ("you're risking without reason"; "this is why I use EMA, it helps me to measure distance", MJTge-KU9_o; "next time you take it so far from EMA you will get burned", Juyy-5cLfBk). EMA flat through the bodies of stacked bars = congestion = no trade. In a strong trend every touch of the EMA bounces ("hard mode", more aggressive entries allowed).
- **How close is close.** A second entry that "hasn't quite touched the EMA", "not fully at the EMA", "still inside the channel", or where only the tail touched the EMA ("I would like to touch with the body", d0aud04jpag; "body far from EMA, only tail touched: you cannot enter", MJTge-KU9_o) is skipped. "A little bit stacked, three or four bars stacked, still above EMA, decent bar" is still valid at the key entry point (9y2hp3sMRHg). Selling above the EMA is allowed only when a trendline confirms it or the trend is very strong (Pf9T9ZGauLY). Prefer the second entry close to the EMA "so you have room to get out before reaching this swing high" (MJTge-KU9_o). Having room to the prior swing is a condition for a failed-second-entry trade.
- **Prior session close and OHL** (Ajq4FELRa1s): the previous session's close is a key level that repeatedly produces bounces and failed breaks; prior-day high is useful on volatile days "so you don't go long into the high of the day"; "a few ticks" off is not important; do not trade the level blindly. After an overshoot, the open of the day or the close of yesterday is "a good key level where prices will give you opportunity" (sIsbARcnv3s). Yesterday's close may be drawn slightly off the actual close to pick up multiple touches (KH4VvrFD8ag).
- **High and low of the day.** "As intraday scalpers the only key level you need to pay attention to is the one you would find during your pre-market analysis, you mark your low for the day and you mark your high for the day"; price retests these areas during the day; "it is not a single line, it is an area" (OQNuBnPOqlk, 7l4ruYYgp-c, aEYG2Kptufk, GD6D3r2cwZQ). Forex traders who "mark 20 key levels" wonder why trades fail. Pick "the one that is probably the strongest one", e.g. three matching lows at the prior close (oYeSn3HMnkI).
- **Key level drawing rules for ranges** (kf-gs1kfJEE): (1) draw support/resistance off bodies and closes, not wicks; sacrifice a few points off the extreme to get more touches; use the highest high / lowest low only when there is no close for confirmation (triple top at the highest high); (2) use tight short-term trendlines, not wide or flat ones; (3) as the range narrows, keep the main levels static and add an inner level if needed; a breakout above a line drawn too high "is not really a failed breakout yet because the proper key entry point is down here". Pick the side with the most touches, put the other side "where I have a double confirmation" (7l4ruYYgp-c). Levels "can move with more price action, it's completely normal."
- Levels must be drawn, not only trendlines: not drawing the double/triple top and multiple bottoms was "my cardinal sin today" (vHaDDLX9lOc).

## 5. Stop loss (the only rigid rule)

(core) One tick beyond the signal bar: below its low for longs, above its high for shorts. On every instrument and timeframe. Never moved ("never move your stop loss, never exit early", MJTge-KU9_o). Never placed in the middle of the bar. If the stop is too large: fewer contracts, a limit order deeper inside the bar, micros instead of minis, or skip the trade (but mark it and track it); "if the bar is too big you simply cannot enter because it violates your money management rules" (sat588vKgJw). "You can move your target, you cannot move your stop." A stop below a whole swing (inside-bar entry, double-bar entry) is "not good risk to reward" when the swing is large (O1sIYq9Vxl8).

Sizing the typical stop (bDhJn3ux30s): "backtest the last 30 trades, put your stop loss one tick below the signal bar and average the amount of it; in this market it's about eight ticks, sometimes more sometimes less depending on volatility." Elsewhere: "most of the stop loss I take are around the two point area" on ES (LCm3VvhkQxI).

Why traders move stops (IdgvwfhWhHU): they never accepted the risk and judge themselves on one trade. Fix: put the limit order, the stop and the target "where they belong", then alt-tab away for 15 minutes. "Losing trades are like business expenses." Judge performance at the week or month, not the trade or the day. "If you find yourself closing trades prematurely you haven't accepted the idea that the trade may be a losing trade" (MJTge-KU9_o).

## 6. Targets, exits, trade management (summary; detail in section 14)

- **Target is a scalp of about 1:1, slightly less.** (core) ES: "8 to 10 ticks" (JGULPdDOWbw, zOhx-tuXR4k, aEYG2Kptufk, bDhJn3ux30s), "10 ticks which is two and a half points" (qo0ebM_yyZY, LCm3VvhkQxI, rmmLXeYivk8), "I'm going for eight ticks" (oYeSn3HMnkI), "8 ticks is such a tiny little move" and a trade exited at exactly 2 points, 4425 to 4450 (d0aud04jpag), TCeNTRKpe9s checks a trade for 3319.75 to 3317.75 = 8 ticks. NQ (1500-tick chart): 10, 12, 14 points. CL 500-tick: 10, 12, 14 ticks. Procedure for any instrument: measure the signal-bar size of high probability setups over many days (or use ATR), set target = average stop, then slightly below.
- Minimum scalp he will accept: ES 1 point, stocks 10 cents, forex 10 pips, but he argues against small targets: at a 1-point target one 12-tick loss wipes three 4-tick wins after commissions. Full argument in section 14.
- **Runner.** (core) Once the scalp fills, the remaining contract goes to breakeven and is trailed below each swing. "Scalp is the main portion, runner is gravy." Split: start with 1 contract; later 2/3 scalp, 1/3 runner. Fade-the-breakout trades and range extremes must carry a runner: "you can catch a low of the day or high of the day majority of the times" (aEYG2Kptufk, GD6D3r2cwZQ, rmmLXeYivk8). On a major reversal "you would still be holding runner to this very end of the day" (dMkdZVtU-9o).
- **Early exit.** Beginners never exit early. Advanced: scratch at breakeven only after about one hour of congestion with no direction ("very long congestion", MJTge-KU9_o).
- **Missed fill.** If price reaches the scalp target without filling the entry, cancel the order and wait for the next setup. "You cannot enter this late, your scalp portion is already fulfilled" (GD6D3r2cwZQ).
- **Orders.** (core) He uses limit orders placed after the trigger prints, at the close of the signal bar or 1 to 2 ticks back. Stop orders never miss but can slip 1-2 ticks; he uses stop orders only when demonstrating live.
- **No chasing.** (core) After the scalp, no new entries at the highs; wait for the next pullback to a key entry point. "Market can go higher 20, 50, 100 points, I don't care." "You're supposed to be buying in the pullback phase as close to the key entry point as you can", not in the exhaustion phase (9y2hp3sMRHg). While holding a runner, no new entries (Mu8XCswoe1Q); one position at a time implied (GD6D3r2cwZQ skipped a valid setup while holding a runner).
- **Claimed statistics (his, unverified, they vary):** 95%+ of high probability setups reach 8-10 ticks (zOhx-tuXR4k); "98% of those trades would work easily for two points or more", 100 days backtested (bDhJn3ux30s); taking every with-trend second entry blindly gives ~70% wins (LCm3VvhkQxI, ObLZtYJ_sAg) or "like 65% win percentage" trading second entries and traps alone (ayMhgoErg0A); waiting for the retest of the new extreme gives ~90% (LCm3VvhkQxI); he was "60 to 70%", "65%", at a 4-tick target and still break-even (bDhJn3ux30s); "80% of the time you will almost always get a nice failed second entry long" in a downtrend after a new extreme (viVjkkB0TuE); "on every single candle there's a 50% chance" (Juyy-5cLfBk, sIsbARcnv3s); rules "nine out of ten times are going to work" (vHaDDLX9lOc).

## 7. Risk, money management, session rules

- Risk capital only; prove it on sim for a few months first. Start ES with $2,500-3,000 (20-25 trades of runway), $10,000 ideal; risk 1-2% per trade; never so big that one loss gives "jitters".
- **Daily rules:** two losing trades in a row -> 15-minute break; third losing trade -> done for the day; two or three winners in a row -> stop for the day (except easy trend days). "Take your one winning trade, take your two winning trades, go home, next day stack another two" (7l4ruYYgp-c); "two easy scalps, I'm out of here" (RpdD-Tifcok); "I took two setups and I called it a day" (d0aud04jpag). If 30 trades on $3,000 do not make money -> back to sim. "Trading is a probabilities game, you just follow the rules and the odds will work" (O1sIYq9Vxl8).
- **Trade frequency he describes:** "I only took two trades today" (Ajq4FELRa1s, vHaDDLX9lOc); "there's always going to be one or two good trades a day worth waiting on" (Mu8XCswoe1Q); zero trades in four hours with two misses (xYI4QLdhsAU); "high probability setups are quite rare; if every five minutes there was a high probability setup we would all be rich"; strong trend days are "one or two" per period and "you can make a month worth of profits on a day like this" (DVNSeqUS3Lc, L7G19gc2C9Y).
- Pre-market: analyse the lows and highs and identify the market structure "every single time before I start my live trading" (aEYG2Kptufk); identify the pattern and bias from the prior session; unclear bias -> wait for the open. Pre-market setups are "too early" and first entries there are not taken (Ajq4FELRa1s, IdgvwfhWhHU). If late or upset: no trades for the first minutes. "Not making money is better than losing money; it's like putting money in the bank" (xYI4QLdhsAU). Print the chart at the end of the day and mark every setup; study charts over the weekend and journal. "Not taking a trade is a trading error as well." "Stick to your edge, take all of these trades; what you don't want to do is miss all these trades and then take one trade and that will be a loser" (GD6D3r2cwZQ).
- Psychology: markets are neutral, random per trade, not personal (sIsbARcnv3s); after a loss "take a break, get up and walk away for five minutes"; revenge trading and paralysis are the two failure modes; "trading is only mental"; the hand-on-the-stove mechanism makes you ignore downticks when you keep buying a grinding downtrend (rmmLXeYivk8). "Scared traders do not make money in this business" (oYeSn3HMnkI). "Nothing is 100% in price action."

## 8. Trendlines and channels (how he draws them)

- (core) Two swings set the line, the third touch confirms it ("first two swings will set the trend line and third swing generally confirms the channel"). Copy the line to the other side to form the channel. A valid line fits "from the highs as well as from the lows". "Using the first two swings is always the right way to find the trendline" (oYeSn3HMnkI). "From the first two swings off the start of the day a lot of times this will give you an idea where the potential trend line is going to be" (Mu8XCswoe1Q).
- **Find it off the opposite side** (a7wKNVCPx1A, ayMhgoErg0A, OQNuBnPOqlk, IdgvwfhWhHU): look for the trendline off the highs in an uptrend, then put it off the lows; it is more robust and gives earlier setups; "always confirm it off the other side"; "you can find the trend lines before everybody else."
- (core) Draw short-term trendlines on every correction, including corrections inside the main trend, "micro, tight, steep" (OQNuBnPOqlk, 7l4ruYYgp-c, O1sIYq9Vxl8, kf-gs1kfJEE). The trendline rule applies to them: a correction is "played out" only after its own break and new extreme (or a clear attempt at one). Until then, no with-trend entries even on a perfect second entry. Short-term lines are "the least subjective and the most objective of them all" and "your best and only reliable tool" in ranges. They tell you where the high probability setup will occur; "you don't draw these trend lines just for the sake of it" (O1sIYq9Vxl8). Short-term lines with the direction of the trend are "redundant" (OQNuBnPOqlk).
- **The oldest line is the right one** (a7wKNVCPx1A, kf-gs1kfJEE): "the first trend line you would see watching the chart live is always the proper one"; do not widen or flatten it to justify late entries; a widened line hides a completed break and new extreme and gets you trapped.
- **Bigger patterns take time** (a7wKNVCPx1A, s2ivxFOJl0U, d0aud04jpag, MJTge-KU9_o): do not look for the bigger channel right after the shortened trendline plays out; "let it breathe"; "you don't have to find your channel right away." A trendline posted with three touches "came into play later in the day."
- **Bigger pattern takes precedence** (core): when two lines conflict, "seven or eight times out of ten the bigger pattern will prevail" (a7wKNVCPx1A); "the bigger pattern in price action takes precedence, on a balance of probabilities the bigger channel will overpower the smaller one" (viVjkkB0TuE, RpdD-Tifcok, mS1920Y-o3Q, qo0ebM_yyZY). Sitting on your hands until the short line plays out is acceptable. Seven consecutive bullish bars are "meaningless compared to this big overall downtrend."
- Break = close outside the line on the counter-trend side. **Overshoot** (core) = break on the trend side, far from the EMA; "unnatural", "oversold territory, you don't need no Bollinger bands or RSI"; "the rules of overshoots are 95% legit": leads to a trendline break or a straight reversal ("you can have straight up reversal without break and a new extreme", OQNuBnPOqlk); "one out of 10 times" the trend simply continues (TCeNTRKpe9s). After an overshoot, do not take the second entry at the key level ("after overshoot, I need to wait for a higher low", 9y2hp3sMRHg); wait for the lower-high / higher-low confirmation. An overshoot of a short-term line can be handled by making it a two-tier channel (IdgvwfhWhHU, 7l4ruYYgp-c); measure the overshoot for a target on the other side (viVjkkB0TuE, 9y2hp3sMRHg). Breaks in the overnight session "a lot of times will not count" (TCeNTRKpe9s).
- Widen the channel when the trend keeps making new extremes against a line ("five legs to a new extreme" means the line is wrong; "the bigger the move, the wider the pattern", RpdD-Tifcok; keep the tight line when "these two legs are tiny"). Widen also when price breaks to a new high but "is barely correcting" (MJTge-KU9_o). Do not widen after repeated overshoots with "no prior point of reference; I need at least one confirming swing and it should be the second" (OQNuBnPOqlk). Losing momentum = price stops reaching the channel line, barely reaching the middle; "each bullish push phase is smaller and smaller." Delete a wider channel when the trend fails to reach its far line (Juyy-5cLfBk).
- Draw wider "when it's unsure what the pattern is and when we expect a reversal"; after a reversal pattern "extend the trend line and make it a little bit wider" (OQNuBnPOqlk, d0aud04jpag). "Trends are not supposed to be flat, they're supposed to be steep"; a too-flat big line "may even be a brand new downtrend." Healthy trend = about 45 degrees, small bars getting smaller; a 90-degree move "won't last long" (DVNSeqUS3Lc, _YuPbI4FE2M).
- Spike and channel: strong spike (e.g. four big bullish bars), congestion at the EMA, then an orderly channel with the EMA holding; a break of it still expects a new extreme; it "will eventually break after a significant move", so find the trades "as the market is establishing this channel" (a7wKNVCPx1A). Two-tier channel trick: measure one tier and copy it to project the second tier, which catches the higher low (oYeSn3HMnkI). "Pyramid pattern": a narrowing channel; after its break expect support at the EMA (Ajq4FELRa1s).
- Fit strictness: big channels may be approximate ("close is enough, tweak during the day"); short-term lines must fit "neatly, hugging" the swings, and the copied parallel must fit the other side too, otherwise the line is wrong and gets deleted. Confirm the big channel on a higher timeframe chart (10,000-tick) if needed (viVjkkB0TuE).
- Short-term lines are trusted over an uncertain bigger pattern: "they'll give you more trades than the bigger patterns." When the big picture is unreadable, trade only the short-term trendline rule.
- Channel mechanics: push phase ends at the channel line, correction phase ends at the key entry point; on reaching the far channel line "expect a little bit stronger correction, you're still looking for shorts" (viVjkkB0TuE).
- Wedges: never trade inside a wedge ("tempting to go short and tempting to go long = no trade"; "I don't like taking trades in the wedge", OQNuBnPOqlk; a wedge is "so much empty room at the top portion of the trend channel, pushes to the upside are smaller, smaller, and smaller", 9y2hp3sMRHg). Trade only its resolution, usually the failed breakout back to the prior support/resistance. General rule: take a trade only when "there's only one possibility that can happen"; two conflicting beliefs = "watch pattern" = sit on your hands (RpdD-Tifcok, d0aud04jpag, TCeNTRKpe9s).

## 9. Market reading: trend, range, reversal

- **Bias from three factors** (9y2hp3sMRHg): (1) structure: higher highs/higher lows, lower highs/lower lows, or sideways; (2) the 21 EMA: direction, which side price is on, and whether it holds or rejects price; (3) the trendline rule state (has the current line broken, has the new extreme formed). Short-term bias can differ from the main bias; "always try to focus on the bigger pattern."
- **Trend:** (core) EMA pointing up, higher highs and higher lows, trendline fitting -> longs only (second entry long at key entry point, failed second entry short). Mirror for downtrends. "Once I see this bullish bar we broke above the EMA I'm switching into longs-only mindset" (Mu8XCswoe1Q). Strong trend look: "a lot of tiny bullish bars, a lot of dojis, a lot of tails, crawling their way higher"; big 90-degree bars get rejected (_YuPbI4FE2M). "The deeper the pullback is, the lower you buy, the safer it is"; "you have to buy the pullbacks when it's uncertain, because when you wait after the market has proven it's resuming it's already too late" (DVNSeqUS3Lc, _YuPbI4FE2M). A massive spike to a new high that never touched the EMA "a lot of times results into additional bullish continuation" (MJTge-KU9_o).
- **Range:** (core) series of highs and lows, price swinging through a flat EMA; confirmed by "a move down, a move up, a move down". "Six or seven days out of ten are some form of trading range" (ayMhgoErg0A); "the majority of days" (GD6D3r2cwZQ). "The situation where you're not sure what's going to happen, you cannot find the best setups, that is some form of trading range" (ayMhgoErg0A). Range highs/lows come from the prior day's levels, the pre-market high and low, and the first bounces. Buy the lows, sell the highs, skip the middle ("the only exception is a reversal pattern", 7l4ruYYgp-c), fade breakouts of small and medium ranges (a failed break back inside the range is "the safest setup of the day"; the entry trigger is still 1 tick beyond a proper signal bar, not the breakout bar). Once the range is big enough to contain real trends (his example: a 60-point downtrend inside it), stop fading and trade the internal trends with second entries ("this is now a trend and you want to buy the second entry with the direction of a trend"; "switch the rules, abandon the trading range and stick with the trend", 7l4ruYYgp-c). Direction of the move INTO the range hints at the break direction. Do not buy the lows blindly: the short-term down-trendline must break and retest first. Healthy range: "price goes from one extreme to the other, no imbalance." Warning sign (core): a selloff that stops short of support (a "gap" to the level), or bounces "in the middle or the lower third" twice, means buyers are early; the next second entry short will fail; switch sides (qo0ebM_yyZY, GD6D3r2cwZQ, ayMhgoErg0A, Ajq4FELRa1s). Four EMA rejections in a row = imbalance, stop fading that side (7l4ruYYgp-c). A bullish imbalance near resistance: wait for the break higher and then take second entry longs (kf-gs1kfJEE). Inside a bullish day fade only downside breaks of a small range; inside a bearish day only upside breaks (9y2hp3sMRHg, DVNSeqUS3Lc). "The further away you're from EMA especially in trading ranges the higher likelihood there is you're going to pull back."
- **Buying the low, the pattern** (KH4VvrFD8ag): only in a range; the downtrend must have played out (break + new low); prefer a break below a key level because most breakouts fail; strong bearish move -> depletion candle (doji) -> indecision -> a second entry short that traps late shorts -> go long on a nice bar. "The stronger the move the better, because most people are going to get trapped."
- **Traps** (s2ivxFOJl0U): a trap looks exactly like a second entry at the EMA; what makes it a trap is context: a range, a range extreme just broken, or a two-leg move already completed with break and new extreme. "Second entry with trend, and counter-trend high quality setup is a failed second entry." A first entry in a downtrend under the EMA is never a trap "because nobody's going to go long" there.
- **Skip rules he repeats on every chart:** (core) no shorts into the low of the day / longs into the high ("you need a deeper correction"); no entries while the counter-trend correction's trendline is intact; after a break and new extreme of the main trend, be "leery" of continuation entries ("I'm no longer looking for longs, you have to respect the trend lines", d0aud04jpag; "taking a second entry is risky after a new extreme, I need a lower high or a failed second entry long", viVjkkB0TuE); no setups when the EMA is flat and price chops around a level; no entry "directly into" a trendline, resistance or support ("I cannot go short directly into this trend line, no room", RpdD-Tifcok; "going long exactly into this resistance is a sure way to get trapped", v5HqLD8kcRc); no setup "too close to resistance, you can get barely out before market reversing" (9y2hp3sMRHg); no second entry in a very strong trend "if this was a very strong uptrend I could potentially take additional second entries even if the trend played out" is the only exception (ayMhgoErg0A).
- **Regime shift:** two big legs through the EMA with volume, tails and dojis on the far side, failure to reach the old resistance/support, and price struggling to get back across the EMA = the range is no longer normal, bias has flipped. "Pushed massively below the EMA" = bias shifting (sIsbARcnv3s). A "hockey stick" move that never touches the EMA from resistance to support means the breakout will work (Juyy-5cLfBk). Steepness rule: big steep corrections are safe to trade with the trend; a grinding move with small corrections is not. A breakout that gets "way too far above the initial trading range" is a new structure (9y2hp3sMRHg).
- **Reversal (three conditions, all required):** (core) (1) trendline break then new extreme (trendline rule satisfied); (2) two failed attempts in the old direction (failed first and failed second entry); (3) price spikes through the 21 EMA and the EMA then holds as a "stepping stone" (bars closing through it); "the true reversal pattern closes above the EMA" (dMkdZVtU-9o); "the best reversal pattern is the one that you're touching the EMA nicely" and "needs to be at the EMA, much closer" (d0aud04jpag, vHaDDLX9lOc, RpdD-Tifcok, v5HqLD8kcRc). EMA rejecting = no reversal, sit on your hands; if price pushes way below the EMA after the push up, abandon the reversal idea. A double top / double bottom retest "adds more fuel". Top/bottom picking is allowed only inside trading ranges, and only for experienced traders. The reversal pattern works on short-term trends inside ranges too. In a big range with strong internal trends a reversal pattern at the top is "too early to pick a top" (Juyy-5cLfBk).
- **Lower highs / higher lows** (viVjkkB0TuE): "lower highs are generally after second entries. If it's a clear trading range without imbalance, lower highs are great. If it's a strong downtrend without significant bullish push you can take lower high but not when you can see the stronger bullish momentum", then wait for the failed second entry long. "Higher low is always safe confirmation setup despite the fact that we have news" (MJTge-KU9_o).
- **Congestion:** (core) overlapping bars stacked side by side, EMA flat through the bodies (his counts: 4, 7, 8, 10, 14-15 bars) -> no trades; "the only way you can trade congestion and not have a losing trade is you fade the breakout" (dMkdZVtU-9o, lgIrZDR6uxs, TCeNTRKpe9s, DVNSeqUS3Lc, oYeSn3HMnkI); in an uptrend only fade the downside break. Congestion vs channel: congestion lows are static; a healthy channel has gaps between bars and each low and high higher than the previous (oYeSn3HMnkI).
- **Depletion:** very large bars at the end of a long trend = exhaustion; expect a larger correction. "Strong bearish move followed by a depletion candle" starts the bottoming pattern (KH4VvrFD8ag). Bearish depletion: massive bearish bar with no continuation, many wicks, bullish bars (sat588vKgJw).
- **Correction length:** "long corrections are only for big patterns; you're not going to get a new extreme after such a pullback" on a medium uptrend (7l4ruYYgp-c); "bigger patterns may get longer corrections, you don't want to be going long too quickly" (9y2hp3sMRHg).

## 10. Charts, instruments, time

- Avoid anything under 1 minute. 1-minute is the optimal time chart for scalping; tick charts are preferred because they are more symmetrical and "filter these congestions, there will be only one two or three bars" (aEYG2Kptufk). His picks: ES 2000-tick ("very similar to three minute or one minute chart depending on volatility", _YuPbI4FE2M), NQ 1500-tick, CL 500-tick, GC 500-tick. Micros are matched to the mini's chart, not to 1 minute: **MNQ 2500-tick ~ NQ 1500-tick**, MES 950-tick ~ ES 2000-tick. He took one trade "from a 2500-tick chart because the signal bar here is really bad, it looked much better there" (9y2hp3sMRHg).
- Rule for picking a chart: too few bars or setups -> lower the tick count; too many fakeouts -> raise it; watch and backtest several days before trading it. Master one market, one timeframe: "I take all my trades and make all my analysis on one single chart" (aEYG2Kptufk).
- **5-minute chart as a filter only** (aEYG2Kptufk, L6SD9duXkww, O1sIYq9Vxl8): (1) check whether the bigger trend has already played out (break + new extreme on 5m) before a with-trend entry at the day's high; (2) distance from the 5m EMA, a 90-degree move snaps back; (3) a bearish bar at a 5m trendline justifies a tick-chart second entry slightly away from the EMA for an 8-10 tick scalp; (4) a 5m signal bar can be used for a hidden first entry / higher low.
- **Session times:** he trades the US open (15:30 his clock, Europe) onward; "US open session is opening soon, where a lot of professional traders start" (Mu8XCswoe1Q); pre-market is slow and early; "I recommend traders not to be trading like the two hours before the close at least" (Ajq4FELRa1s); no trading "this late regardless" (viVjkkB0TuE). His clock readings in videos: 16:30, 16:37, 17:13, trades at 13:00 and 16:15.
- **Day types:** Fridays "tend to be slow", the Friday before a long weekend "sit on your hands or even close the shop and go home" (IdgvwfhWhHU, RpdD-Tifcok, 7l4ruYYgp-c); rollover day "generally the second day of the rollover is pretty decent" but rollover plus Friday = skip (bDhJn3ux30s); FOMC days: no trades before the release, "before a big FOMC the market will usually go into consolidation" (kf-gs1kfJEE, mbzdZ-JH64k).
- Holidays: the prior day's price action carries into the next day (he does not otherwise use the prior day's structure, apart from the prior close level and prior-day OHL). His scalp on ES is stated as 10 ticks = 2.5 points; bars of 6-7 points are "unenterable" for that target, so on volatile days many setups are skipped.

## 11. Where this comes from, and what the outside world says

- **Al Brooks lineage.** Wade cites Brooks' logic for second entries. Every element here (H2/L2 second entries, two-legged pullbacks, trendline break then test of the extreme, breakout pullbacks, failed second entry as a with-trend setup, signal bar rule, "most breakouts fail", 20-bar EMA) is Brooks' Trading Price Action framework with a 21 EMA and tick charts. Brooks' books are the primary source if we want the complete and numeric version.
- **NexusFi / Brooks community rules** (from earlier web research): entry on a stop one tick beyond the signal bar; stop beyond the leg-2 extreme (2-4 ticks on NQ); measured-move target with a minimum of 2:1 for swing portions; skip in ranges, flat EMA, no key level, bad signal bar. Note the difference: Wade targets ~1:1 scalps; the Brooks swing variant targets 2:1 measured moves.
- **Academic.** Intraday momentum exists in equity index futures: the first-half-hour return predicts the last half hour; gamma hedging by option dealers is the main explanation. NDX futures show both momentum and reversal depending on conditions. This supports Wade's "trade with the day's bias, not against it" but says nothing about pullback counting.
- **Institutions.** Portfolio managers and quant desks do not trade trendlines. They work orders against VWAP over time, which is why pullbacks to VWAP/EMA get bought in trend days and why price "snaps back" to the average. Wade's "EMA as a magnet" is the retail-visible version of that behaviour.

## 12. Translation into numbers (proposal to test, not yet tested)

Definitions for the engine, on 1-minute MNQ bars (his 2500-tick MNQ chart is close to 1-minute in RTH) with a 5-minute cross-check. Items from version 1 are kept; new numeric candidates from batches 3-5 are added.

- **Bias, three factors:** (1) structure: last two confirmed swing highs and lows rising (bull), falling (bear), else range; (2) 21 EMA: slope sign of EMA[t] - EMA[t-3] and side of price (close above/below) over the last 5 bars; (3) trendline-rule state: has the active short-term line broken (close beyond it) and has the new extreme (or an attempt within 2 ticks at a key entry point) printed. Bias = bull only when all three agree; bear mirror; otherwise range. Prior-session close relative to its open stays as a tie-breaker.
- Extreme and count start: highest high (for longs) since the last confirmed pivot; pivot = a bar whose low is taken by 1 tick after an up move. In ranges, restart the count at the extreme of each internal micro-trend.
- Leg state machine: track legs by the 1-tick-break rule; first entry = first bar whose high is taken by 1 tick after the extreme; second entry = the next bar whose high is taken after the first entry fails without a new extreme; reset on new extreme, pivot break, double top/bottom within 1 tick, matching highs within 1 tick when counting counter-trend. Third entries are not trades.
- **Key entry point test at the signal bar:** distance from 21 EMA <= X ticks measured from the body (not the wick), X from signal-bar statistics, start with 1 median bar range; or touching a prior swing / prior session close / prior-day high-low / pre-market high-low within Y ticks (treat levels as zones: start Y = 2 ticks); or touching a channel line (channel = 2 swings + 3rd touch within Z ticks; start Z = 2 ticks). Score = number of key entry points coinciding (1, 2, 3); test the rule "2+ required" against "1 required".
- **Key level selection for ranges (kf-gs1kfJEE):** build levels from closes, not wicks: cluster bar closes within 2 ticks; a level needs >= 2 touches; prefer the close-based level over the extreme unless no close-based level exists (triple top at the highest high); keep the level fixed once set; add an inner level only as a second zone.
- **Signal bar score (candlestick tiers, section 13):** body = |close - open|, range = high - low, for longs: upper tail ratio = (high - close)/range. Tier 1: body >= 60% of range, close in top 10%, upper tail <= 10%. Tier 2: body >= 40%, close in top 25%. Tier 3: body >= 1 tick bullish or a one-tick bearish body with lower tail >= 50% of range and close above the EMA. Tier 4: body <= 1 tick with tails both sides (doji) or bearish body > 2 ticks: no long. Allow tier 3 only when key entry points >= 2 and the correction has played out. Excessively big bar: range > 1.5x the median signal-bar range -> skip (or limit order variant below).
- Congestion filter: no trade if the last K bars (test K = 4 and K = 7) overlap (each bar's range inside the prior bar's range by more than 70%) with the EMA inside their bodies.
- Correction played out: the correction's own 2-swing trendline must be broken by a close, then a new extreme of the correction printed (or attempted within 2 ticks at a key entry point) before the with-trend count is accepted. After the main trend's own break and new extreme, with-trend second entries are blocked until a lower-high / higher-low confirmation prints (overshoot flag: if the break was on the trend side of the channel line and the signal bar is > 1.5 median ranges from the EMA, require the confirmation).
- **Entry and stop:** trigger = 1 tick beyond the signal bar; entry variants to test: (a) stop order at trigger, (b) limit at the signal-bar close, (c) limit 1-2 ticks back from the trigger (his live practice; expect missed fills). Stop loss = 1 tick beyond the other side of the signal bar, never moved. Typical stop estimate = median of the last 30 signal-bar ranges + 1 tick (his average over 30 trades is about 8 ticks on ES).
- **Target:** his scalp is 8-10 ES ticks against an ~8-tick stop, i.e. about 1:1. Scale to MNQ by the ATR ratio: target_MNQ = (8 to 10 ES ticks in points) x (ATR_MNQ / ATR_ES) measured on the same 1-minute window; alternative: target = median signal-bar stop of the sample. Constraint: MNQ friction is about 3 points per round trip (spread + commission), so his 10-tick ES scalp (2.5 ES points, roughly 10-14 MNQ points at current ATR ratios) cannot be copied literally without losing most of the edge to friction; the 2-contract model with half at target and half trailed under each swing (runner) is the one to test, plus a measured-move target (leg 1 projected from the end of the pullback) for the runner.
- **Add-on (L6SD9duXkww):** only while the first position is open and not yet scalped out; the new entry must be (1) with trend, (2) a second entry or a lower-high / higher-low still inside the same second entry, (3) at the key entry point, (4) with the prior correction played out. Size = same as the initial. Test on and off.
- **Time and news windows:** no entries in the first N minutes after the open if the pre-market bias is unclear (test N = 0, 5, 15); no entries in the last 120 minutes before the close; no entries 15 minutes before to 15 minutes after scheduled releases (FOMC, CPI, NFP) and flatten runners before FOMC; skip Fridays before long weekends and rollover-day Fridays (flag only, test on and off); holiday rule: carry prior-day levels and lines across a holiday.
- Session rules: 2 consecutive losses -> 15-minute pause; 3 losses -> stop; 2 or 3 wins -> stop (test on and off); one position at a time; no new entries while a runner is open.
- Report format as agreed: net, max drawdown, % profitable, profit factor, green/red days, monthly, why each loser lost, common denominator of losers, why winners won. Add: win rate split by setup type, market state and key entry point score, to compare with his claims (65-70% blind, 90% with retest).

## 13. Candlestick and signal bar rules (sat588vKgJw, 45 min, plus restatements)

Principles:
- A signal bar is "the last piece of confirmation": market context -> key entry point -> price action pattern -> candlestick. "You cannot trade candlestick patterns in a vacuum"; "you cannot enter a trade based solely on a candlestick in the no man's land"; retail patterns (tweezers, gravestone doji, harami, pin bars alone) are "dangerous to novice traders" because context is ignored (sat588vKgJw, aJ7EMrUG_zk).
- Watch the bar form but judge the final picture; novices always wait for the close. "If you don't understand, that means the market is unclear, you sit on your hands."
- Three factors of a single bar: (1) size: big = momentum, excessively big = too much risk, small or neutral = lack of pressure; (2) body: closing on its high/low = momentum; (3) tails: possible rejection or reversal. A big tail below a bull body = "bullish tail" = strong buying; at a key level this is "massive momentum."
- When the signal bar matters less: context clear with "no conflicting variables" (strong trend, trendline break, two-legged pullback to the EMA: a weak neutral candle is still sellable), or multiple key entry points coincide. When it matters a lot: context weak or unconfirmed, unsure key entry point, aggressive trades (buying low, selling high, fading breakouts, early reversals, riding back to a far EMA).
- Direction rule: cannot short below a bullish bar even when the count says second entry ("I don't want to cherry-pick"); a "full-on bearish bar, two or three ticks of bearish body" disqualifies a long (Mu8XCswoe1Q); "extremely bullish signal bar" at two key entry points still means no short (RpdD-Tifcok); a one-tick bearish body with a long lower tail and buying at the close is acceptable for a long at a strong key entry point (Ajq4FELRa1s, DVNSeqUS3Lc, d0aud04jpag, Juyy-5cLfBk).

Named patterns and his usage rule:
- **Trend bar:** bullish closes on its very high "without a tail"; bearish opens at the top and closes at the low. Best signal bars.
- **Reversal bar:** significant tail at the bottom (bullish: looked bearish, buyers reversed it) or at the top (bearish). "Nice bearish reversal bar" at the EMA in a downtrend = good signal bar; a neutral body with a massive upper tail that touched the EMA and reversed = still a strong bearish reversal bar. Tail in the upper third = late buyers trapped (xYI4QLdhsAU).
- **Doji:** tails both sides, body of one or two ticks or none = indecision, "not good signal bars"; a second entry long above a bearish doji is "not what we want to see" (oYeSn3HMnkI, 7l4ruYYgp-c); exception: a tiny doji at the key entry point "may be worth taking a chance because you're risking almost nothing but it is fairly aggressive" (oYeSn3HMnkI); a "fairly doji-ish" bar with a tail at the bottom and bullish close is OK in a double trap (MJTge-KU9_o). Many dojis side by side = congestion = no trade zone.
- **Consecutive bars:** a series of consecutive bearish bars = downtrend, short only; consecutive bullish trend bars = uptrend; an equal mix with wicks both sides = consolidation. Third bearish bar with tails at both ends at a key entry point = weakening (Mu8XCswoe1Q).
- **Depletion bar:** massive bar at the end of a move followed by no continuation, wicks, opposite bars = momentum dissipated; expect a count reset and a larger correction.
- **Inside bar** (aJ7EMrUG_zk, sat588vKgJw): highs and lows including tails inside the previous bar. In the candlestick guide: "not enough strength and momentum", "I recommend avoiding inside bar entries, especially if you're a novice." In the inside-bar video: never a reason by itself to trade or not; when the setup is valid it lets you trigger 1 tick beyond a smaller bar (saved 1 tick with one inside bar, 3 ticks with a double inside bar) with a smaller stop; refuse the inside-bar trigger only when its body is strongly against the trade ("heavily bearish" inside bar in an uptrend; wait for the break above the original bullish bar). Observed: every good inside-bar entry had a body in the trade direction. He took a live long above an inside bar with a one-tick bearish body because the close showed "extremely bullish pressure" at the bigger-pattern key entry point (d0aud04jpag). Inside-bar entry keeps the stop 1 tick below the signal bar (xYI4QLdhsAU).
- **Engulfing:** the second bar's high is above and low is below the previous bar (both, otherwise not engulfing). Shows trap + momentum. Advanced traders may enter one tick beyond the previous bar without waiting for the engulfing bar to close, "only for a trap, for a failed second entry", to save ticks and enlarge the target.
- **Double bar entry:** when the first signal bar is a bad tier and the context is clear, wait for the next bar; if it is a good bar, use it as the signal bar: trigger 1 tick beyond it, or drop a limit order a few ticks back because double bars "tend to be quite big"; stop 1 tick below the first bar (the lowest point of the swing). Prefer the two candles close together "where it basically merges into one". Not for unclear context. "Kind of like two reversal bar entry" (Juyy-5cLfBk); his attempt with the limit 2-3 ticks back never filled (MJTge-KU9_o).
- **Outside bar:** named with the two-bar patterns; treated like the engulfing case.
- **Gap between bars:** price starts selling at the close with no retrace = momentum, no limit fill (xYI4QLdhsAU, vHaDDLX9lOc); "very rarely market will gap" (bDhJn3ux30s).

Tier ranking (bullish; bearish mirrors):
- Tier 1: strong bullish body, tail at the bottom, almost no upper tail, closing on the very high. Ideal, take.
- Tier 2: still strong, "minor weakness" (small upper tail or a slightly smaller body). Suitable.
- Between 2 and 3: consider if the context is good.
- Tier 3: weak bullish body, decent bearish wick, or even a slight bearish body. Only with very clear context and a strong key entry point.
- Tier 4: neutral or doji. Avoid.
- Bearish mirror: bearish bodies closing on the low with tails at the top = tier 1; small bullish bodies with tails at the bottom = weaker; neutral = skip.

## 14. Trade management in detail

- **Limit order placement** (core: bDhJn3ux30s, Mu8XCswoe1Q, zOhx-tuXR4k, LCm3VvhkQxI, xYI4QLdhsAU, Juyy-5cLfBk, MJTge-KU9_o, vHaDDLX9lOc, v5HqLD8kcRc): wait for the 1-tick trigger, then place a limit order back inside the bar: "I normally put it at the close or one or two ticks further"; "limit order two ticks back"; "2-3 ticks back due to the congestion" (never filled); "exactly to the end of this bar" on a big entry bar; "further back" when the target would be at a nearby strong support, "put your target profit at this support". Reason: "most of the time market will tick one tick below the signal bar and then trace a little bit back; since everybody's using stop orders prices will go back to their break-even point." Each tick back makes the stop 1 tick smaller and protects the runner from stop hunts ("institutions see the orders and always go back for the runners"). Cost: missed fills (gap bars, "prices didn't even take one tick back"); one tick too far back cost him a trade (xYI4QLdhsAU); greed on the limit cost him the only easy setup of a day (viVjkkB0TuE). Equivalence math: a 4-tick target on a stop order vs an 8-tick target on a limit 2 ticks back is "a seven to eight, very similar" risk-reward. He used stop orders for the first 2.5-3 years, limit orders only for about 5 years; stop orders now only when demonstrating live. Cancel every order when there is no trigger or the context changes (aEYG2Kptufk).
- **Stop:** 1 tick beyond the signal bar; typical size = average of the last 30 signal-bar stops ("about eight ticks" on ES); never moved; alt-tab for 15 minutes after placing orders.
- **Scalp target:** 8 to 10 ticks on ES (both numbers appear: "8 to 10 ticks" JGULPdDOWbw, zOhx-tuXR4k, aEYG2Kptufk, bDhJn3ux30s; "10 ticks" LCm3VvhkQxI, qo0ebM_yyZY, rmmLXeYivk8; "eight ticks" oYeSn3HMnkI, d0aud04jpag; "10 ticks when the market's more volatile"). Why not 4 ticks (the "general consensus", Brooks and others): at 4 ticks one 3-point loser erases three winners; he was "60 to 70%", "65% and not making money"; the pressure after a loser made him jump the gun; at 8-10 ticks "I can make my losing trade right back on the next trade" and the runner survives the noise ("left 11 and a half points on the table" at a 6-tick target). Two-tick scalping "gives six winning trades back with one loser" plus commissions. Transition: 6 ticks if 8 feels too long; wanting out at 4 ticks "is telling you you're still feeling nervous" (IdgvwfhWhHU). Backtest claims: 95%+ (zOhx-tuXR4k) / 98% over 100 days (bDhJn3ux30s) of high probability setups reach 8-10 ticks / 2 points. Measured-move legs can set the target ("move my target profit below the signal bar", oYeSn3HMnkI).
- **Runner trailing:** after the scalp, the runner goes to breakeven and "I trail the stop loss below each new swing" (aEYG2Kptufk, L7G19gc2C9Y); example of 22 points on one contract. Always hold a runner on fades and range extremes; do not tighten the runner into a retest (he was stopped twice doing that, GD6D3r2cwZQ: "I should have kept my runner in place"). Measured moves tell you "where you should be keeping your runners" (vHaDDLX9lOc). Runner losses are common and accepted: "of course they came back and got the runner."
- **Add-on, four rules** (L6SD9duXkww): (1) the setup is with trend, (2) it is a second entry (or a lower-high / higher-low still inside the same second entry after a few-tick trap), (3) at the key entry point, (4) the previous counter-trend correction has played out (break + new extreme). Only while the initial position is still open and not yet scalped out ("I never add on when I already scalped and I'm holding a runner"). "A second chance to enter" an identical setup. Experienced traders only; beginners "can end up losing twice their initial position." Live examples: doubled on a lower high (L6SD9duXkww); added on the higher low, holding 12 contracts (7l4ruYYgp-c); higher-low add-on after an inside-bar entry (xYI4QLdhsAU). He scales down to 1 contract when tired or done for the day.
- **When he exits early:** beginners never; advanced only after a very long congestion (about one hour) with no direction, scratch at breakeven; before news, exit runners (below); "I was done with trading, didn't want to risk giving my money back" ends the session, not the trade. On the losing side he never widens the stop.
- **News rules** (mbzdZ-JH64k, kf-gs1kfJEE, MJTge-KU9_o, Juyy-5cLfBk): stop trading 10-15 minutes before a release, resume 10-15 minutes after ("15 minutes" repeated); exit runners before FOMC ("I would even exit the runners here, exit even before the news"; slippage makes a stop "twice as big, even three times as big"); "you don't be trading during this part of the spike"; price action stays valid after the release, only volatility changes; before a big FOMC the market consolidates for hours; he skipped a textbook second entry "right around the news" and took the higher-low confirmation after it. Straddle strategies around news fail by design.
- **No trading in the last 2 hours** (Ajq4FELRa1s): "an hour or two hours before the close, at least two hours", because traders liquidate and volatility collapses; late trades he regrets (kf-gs1kfJEE "impulsive trade this late in the day"; viVjkkB0TuE "no trading this late regardless"; 7l4ruYYgp-c "don't trade late Friday mess").
- **Holiday rule** (rmmLXeYivk8): on the day after a holiday use the prior day's price action because the structure "didn't really finish yet, it is still in continuation"; otherwise he does not use the prior day's structure (only its levels).
- **Rollover day** (bDhJn3ux30s): "today is the rollover date, generally the second day of the rollover is pretty decent"; the expiring contract is slow and the new one slower; rollover plus Friday = congestion, "not worth it."
- **Position limits:** one position at a time; no new entries while a runner is on; after the scalp "no new entries at the highs"; stop after 2-3 winners; stop after 3 losers.
