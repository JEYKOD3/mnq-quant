# NOTES_D: Brooks, Trading Price Action Reversals (2012), Part IV Ch21-25
Machine-first reference for an intraday MNQ/MES engine. Source: plain-text extraction of ch21-25 incl. figure commentary. Bullets only. "P" = his stated probability. "EMA" = 20-bar EMA on the 5m chart. Ticks = Emini ticks (0.25 pt) unless noted. "MTR" = major trend reversal. "MM" = measured move.

## Ch21: Detailed day trading examples (rules extracted per figure)

### 21.1 T-Note 10k-volume bars (bear trend into reversal)
- Bear trend + bar tests EMA from below -> short (bars 3,4,5).
- Second-entry bear trend-channel-line overshoot + reversal up with a doji signal bar -> skip the entry; but if the rally from it breaks the trend line -> arm a long on the test of the low.
- Two-legged higher low with a doji signal bar after two strong bear trend bars -> minimally acceptable long; prefer a stronger signal.
- In a countertrend long inside a bear trend: a low 2 at the EMA with a bear body forms -> exit the long (consider reversing) unless the trend has clearly already reversed.
- Lower low after a trend line break + bull reversal bar + two-bar reversal + bear channel overshoot -> MTR buy.
- Spike-and-channel bear usually ends on the third push (bars 2, 6, 9); the tight channel before the final low becomes the final flag.
- Breakout above an inside bar ending the first tiny pullback after the reversal -> add to the long.
- After a 20-30 bar bear channel, expect the correction to last >= half as many bars.
- Spike-and-channel bear retraces to about the start of the channel; bulls take profits there; bears re-short there.
- Bears do not short into the close when there is not enough time left to profit.
- Four-bar bull spike after the reversal = strong evidence the always-in flipped to long.
- High 2 after a strong bull spike where the pullback is tiny relative to the spike -> buy; all three readings (complex first leg / second leg starting / deeper pullback then new high) imply higher prices.
- A countertrend short signal (second-entry EMA-gap-bar short) that forms right after an always-in flip to long -> ignore it; the flip dominates.

### 21.2 EUR/USD 5m strong open
- Eight consecutive bars without a bear trend bar -> any pullback likely tests the swing high before falling below the spike bottom.
- EMA gap bar + breakout test of the rally start + micro wedge (three pushes down) + wedge bull flag after a strong spike -> expect at least a bounce.
- Triangle (three pushes up and three down) = tight trading range; when several readings coexist, treat as breakout mode and wait for the breakout.
- Failed upside breakout (one tick below the prior high) that reverses through the range bottom as a bear outside bar = double top bear flag -> short.
- Breakout pullback: small bar above the middle of the breakout outside bar -> low-risk short; a second breakout pullback is also a short.
- Low 2 short at the EMA with a bear signal bar + second attempt to break the spike low -> short.
- Keep placing low 2 sell stops while in a bear swing even after a bull doji higher low.
- Parabolic wedge bottom: three pushes down; trend line from pushes 1-2 flatter than from pushes 2-3 (acceleration); largest bear bar and largest two-bar bear spike of a ~20-bar decline at the end -> exhaustion.
- Exhaustion spike at the end of a 20-bar decline -> expect >= two legs up lasting >= 10 bars; entry above a strong bull inside bar (ii).
- After a spike up from a wedge bottom, spike-and-wedge usually gives two more pushes up before a correction.
- ii higher-low long after a trend-channel-overshoot reversal that also forms a double bottom bull flag with the wedge-channel low -> long.
- A large bear trend bar inside the new bull leg traps bears in and weak bulls out; it does not negate the long.

### 21.3 Soybeans 5m buy climax on open
- Doji trading-range bar right after a one-bar spike up -> bad buy setup.
- Aggressive: limit short at the high of the prior bar expecting the high 1 to fail.
- Low 2 + lower high + final flag short after an opening buy climax -> short for an opening reversal / high of day.
- Bear trend-channel overshoot + double bottom with the first bar of the day -> possible low of day; enter above an ii bar with a bull close above the EMA.
- Lower-high wedge bear flag + double top bear flag + four-legged move up (low 4) -> short below it or below the next bear bar.
- A large bear trend bar right after a short entry confirms many traders shorted there.
- Breakout pullback after a trend line break -> entry.
- Huge bull reversal bar at a new low that the next bar cannot trade above -> that next bar (bear body) is a strong short signal bar and a breakout pullback short.

### 21.4 Crude 5m wedge top
- High 1 breakout pullback after a two-bar bull spike breaks out of a trading range (two-bar reversal) -> long; market is now always-in long.
- High 2 above a bull trend bar is acceptable even inside barbwire and after a second buy climax when the prior top did not show bears in control.
- Wedge top at the top of the channel after a spike, with prominent tails, overlap, pullbacks, bear bodies -> expect a test of the channel low.
- Move down that breaks the trend line AND undercuts the prior higher low -> strong bears; the next lower high is an MTR short candidate.
- Lower high after a trend line break = two-legged breakout test of the signal bar low + two-bar reversal + rejection of an EMA poke + wedge bear flag -> short.

### 21.5-21.6 Emini 1m scalps, first 90 minutes (catalog; he says most cannot trade these live)
- Long setups: high 2 after two pushes down; wedge + two-bar reversal + expanding triangle bottom; wedge second entry at a higher low; first pullback after a trend line break (high 2); high 2 in a bull trend; failed bull trend line break + failed break below the range + double bottom bull flag; high 2 with a bull flag breakout pullback two bars earlier.
- Long setups (cont.): two pushes to a one-tick new low of day on a trading range day (one-tick failed breakout + double bottom + wedge + two-bar reversal after a two-bar final flag); two-legged higher low after a trend line break + double bottom pullback; failed trend line break + double bottom one tick higher.
- Short setups: wedge + final flag; breakout pullback low 1 after a bear spike; breakout test lower high low 2; lower high low 2 below the EMA; failed trend line breakout + double top bear flag; two-legged lower low after a trend line break on a trading range day; wedge + final flag + double top bear flag.
- Short setups (cont.): wedge + failed test of the high of day (double top) lower high; low 2 at the EMA + wedge bear flag + five-tick failure for prior longs + first pullback after a bear spike that flipped always-in short; second leg up wedge bear flag + double top bear flag.
- Rule: a one-tick new extreme that immediately reverses = failed breakout + double bottom/top; fade it.
- Rule: five-tick failure (prior entry gets only 5 ticks then reverses) = signal in the other direction.
- Rule: a strong bear leg on a day that is not yet a bear day does not make it a bear day; still a trading range day.

## Ch22: Daily/weekly/monthly charts (futures-relevant extracts)
- Higher-time-frame signals are too rare for a day trader; ignore them intraday except yesterday's high/low, visible on the 5m.
- Countertrend swing on daily charts: target a 1-2% scalp; most countertrend trades become pullbacks in the larger trend.
- Daily setup with the open inside yesterday's range: enter on a stop one tick beyond the range (lower risk).
- Daily setup with a gap open: do not enter on the gap; wait for a pullback into the gap and buy the opening reversal; stop below today's low; at most one more attempt if stopped.
- Daily pullbacks rarely have classic reversal bars -> smaller size and scale in (lower before bears prove control, or higher at a higher low after resumption).
- Round numbers are magnets: expect a 5-10% overshoot beyond the number, then at least one pullback through it; bears do not short in front of a magnet; bulls buy into it and take profits just above.
- DJIA 1932-33 daily shows identical patterns: low 2 trend line break; final flag lower-low test = MTR; breakout pullback; high 2 first pullback in a spike; wedge channel -> two legs down; bear channel = bull flag.
- A strong countertrend momentum move will usually be tested after a rally back toward the prior extreme.
- If a countertrend move is strong enough to flip always-in, do not take the opposing scalp; wait to short the lower high.
- Weekly SPY: when a rally retraces most of a bear trend, the bear loses influence; the chart is a trading range; uncertainty = trading range.
- Institutions take trades with P >= 60% and therefore need at least a measured move (reward = risk) for a positive equation.
- Price often hits an MM target exactly, then pauses or reverses (partial profit taking); most targets then fail because the MM is the minimum.
- 80% of topping patterns resolve upward (most tops are bull flags).
- 5-10 bars hugging a trend line -> odds favor a break below it soon.
- At a trend channel line + MM target -> expect a two-legged sell-off.
- Dollar index equation: buy at the bottom of a range, risk $5, P 60% for $10 to the range top -> ~$4 expected per trade; double bottom bull flag raises P above 60%.
- The 40% case (new low) must trigger an exit; then the target flips to an MM down.
- After an always-in flip, expect a stall at the prior swing extreme and a larger range.
- After a 10-20 bar bull spike with large bull bars, risk of a protracted two-legged pullback is high: strong bulls only buy a big pullback; strong bears short at market and scale in higher; do not buy the top of a parabolic spike.
- Avoid high-beta/news stocks; the reduced size erases the gain.

## Ch23: Options (1 line)
- Options only on 60m/daily or when order systems may lock up; futures-relevant numbers: VIX/16 = expected ADR in % (VIX 32 -> ~2%; VIX 50 -> ~3%, unsustainable); 30+ consecutive EMA gap bars -> >95% chance of an EMA touch within 10 bars; a break above a lengthy bull channel usually reverses to the channel bottom within ~5 bars; a low 2 short at the bottom of a trading range usually fails; ~80% of attempts to end a strong trend fail; 15 consecutive bull trend bars (not seen in years) = climactic -> expect >= 10-bar two-legged correction below the EMA.

## Ch24: The Best Trades (core)

### 24.A Edge and the trader's equation
- Edge exists iff P(target before stop) * reward > P(stop before target) * risk, significantly.
- If the setup looks good -> assume P = 60% (may really be 80%).
- If especially confident -> use P = 70%.
- If uncertain -> use P = 50% and require reward >= 2x risk.
- Equidistant-move rule: in good context, P(equidistant move) = 60% for any stop size that is neither too big (> tallest bar on the chart) nor too small (2 ticks when bars are 50 ticks).
- Normal Emini stop ~8 ticks; on large-bar days use the normal stop or 2-3x it, with reward >= risk.
- Best trade = P 60-70% with reward >= risk, or P 50-60% with reward >= 2x risk.
- Very best = high-probability swing (P >= 60% and reward >= 2x): 2-3 per day, mostly always-in reversals or pullbacks in strong trends.
- Within a trading range P ~ 50%, occasionally 60%; fades at the top/bottom: P 60-80% that the market tests the opposite side.
- Scalper's dilemma: risk $1.00 to make $0.50 at P 60% -> -$0.10/trade. Risk 8 ticks to make 1 tick needs a 90% win rate to break even.
- Reward < risk requires P ~ 80%; almost nobody sustains it; even 80% / 2:1 risk-reward nets only 16 cents per $1 risked.
- Sound examples: 50% for 2:1; 60% for 1:1; 40% for 3:1.
- Strong reversal example: bull trend, sell-off well below the EMA, wedge rally to a marginal higher high with prominent bear bodies, strong bear reversal bar poking above a longer-term bear trend line -> P ~ 70% for 2-4x risk; forms several times a week per market.
- Tight trading range after a strong trend -> P(breakout in trend direction) <= 55%.
- Low-probability setups (reward >= 2x, P < 50%) must be taken as a basket; no cherry-picking.
- High-probability setups (P >= 60%) may be cherry-picked.
- Convincing always-in reversal signal -> P >= 60% on average; target >= stop gives a positive equation.
- Strong breakout follow-through P often > 70% (his preferred trade).
- Opening range: most reversals have P 30% for 2x; usually one per day with P 60%; a reasonable swing setup exists in the opening range on > 80% of days; a strong setup there has P ~ 60% for 1x even when P for 2x is ~50%.
- 5m chart: >= 90% chance of at least one trade/day with reward >= 2x and P >= 50%; 5-10 best trades/day on any major market's 5m.
- Market is in a strong trend <= 20% of the time.
- Inertia: 80% of reversal attempts in a trend fail (become flags); 80% of breakout attempts in a range fail.
- The market tries to keep P near 50%; it cannot, so 60% setups appear.

### 24.B Named best trades (stop entries unless stated)
- Opening reversal, strong setup, swing: reward >= 2x risk, P 50-60%.
- Opening reversal, strong setup, scalp: reward >= risk, P 60-70%.
- Opening reversal example with P >= 60% for several times the risk: gap up, rally a few bars, pullback to the EMA, double bottom with the first bar of the day, strong bull reversal bar -> buy above it.
- MTR (P 50-60%, reward >= 2x), required components: (1) strong break of a major trend line; (2) first reversal leg with momentum and a strong reversal bar; (3) test of the trend extreme: after a bear buy a higher low / double bottom / lower low, after a bull short a higher high / double top / lower high; (4) strong reversal signal bar. Missing any -> no trade.
- MTR outcome: most become trading ranges, usually with room for a 2x swing; those with P > 60% form a few times a week; P 50-60% ones almost daily.
- MTR management: same stop management as trend trades; expect a pullback before the target; hold if the premise is valid.
- Strong final flag reversal after a swing in a trading range or weak channel (P 50-60%, 2x).
- Final flags are usually high 1 / high 2 / low 1 / low 2 or triangles; ii patterns often become final flags.
- Consecutive buy climaxes -> at least a two-legged correction.
- Bear stairs: buy the third or fourth push down; target = test of the prior push's low. Bull stairs: sell the third or fourth push up; target = prior push's high.
- Spike-and-channel day: when the channel (or the breakout on a trending-trading-range day) reaches the MM target and the move is weakening -> fade.
- High 2 pullback to the EMA in a bull trend, buy above a bull signal bar; low 2 pullback to the EMA in a bear trend, sell below a bear signal bar.
- Scalp version of the above: P >= 60%, reward >= risk, 5-10 per day.
- "Single most reliable day trade": high 2 / wedge / triangle at the EMA in a bull trend (low 2 / wedge / triangle in a bear) on a strong trend day.
- Post-spike low 2: four-bar bear spike far below a range, small tails, moderate (non-climactic) bodies, now a low 2 pullback to the EMA with a bear reversal signal bar -> P >= 70% for reward >= risk.
- Wedge bull flag pullback in a bull trend; wedge bear flag pullback in a bear trend.
- Breakout pullback after a breakout from a bull flag (bull) / bear flag (bear).
- High 1 in a strong bull spike (not after a buy climax); low 1 in a strong bear spike (not after a sell climax).
- Short at the top of a trading range; buy at the bottom; especially second entries.
- Range fade is best when the move into the extreme was strong: e.g. strong spike to the top, then a low-momentum wedge bull flag to the bottom third -> buy the reversal or the breakout pullback.
- Not clearly trending: short a low 2 on a rally to about the prior swing high; buy a high 2 near the prior swing low.
- Range swings last 5-10+ bars, then a trend line break and an opposite swing.
- If momentum into the prior extreme was strong, wait for the second entry (usually within ~5 bars).
- In a trading range: up 5-10 bars -> shorts only; down 5-10 bars -> longs only.
- Micro double top/bottom and small final flags produce most tradable reversals; many traders require one before any reversal trade.
- Breakout test that runs breakeven stops and reverses back in the new trend direction = good risk/reward (risk to the signal bar, reward = new trend).
- Stop run followed by trend resumption = reliable scalp.
- Two failed attempts at the same thing -> the opposite usually follows.
- High-momentum reversal leg -> expect a higher low (not a lower low) on the test.
- A deep countertrend leg (> ~1 hour) usually needs a second leg before the final extreme.

### 24.C Limit/market-order best trades (experienced only; always with the always-in direction)
- Buy a bull spike in a strong breakout at market or at/below the prior bar's low (wider stop, smaller size); sell a bear spike at market or at/above the prior bar's high.
- Sell the close of bar 2, 3 or 4 of a strong bear spike; stop possibly above the spike top; P >= 60% for reward = risk.
- Very strong spike with good context: risk < half the spike height, P ~ 70% for >= 2x.
- Buy at/below a weak low 1 or low 2 signal bar in a possible new bull trend after a strong reversal up, or at the bottom of a range; short at/above a weak high 1 / high 2 at the top of a range or after a strong reversal down.
- Buy at/below the prior bar in a quiet bull flag at the EMA; short at/above the prior bar in a quiet bear flag at the EMA.
- Buy below a bull bar that breaks above a bull flag (anticipating a breakout pullback); sell above a bear bar that breaks below a bear flag.
- Swing: buy/add on a breakout test (attempt to run breakeven stops); mirror for bears.
- Buy a pullback in a strong bull at a fixed tick count equal to or slightly less than the average prior pullback (GS: prior max pullback ~$1 -> limit short $1 above the most recent low).
- When a bear needs one more bull bar to confirm an always-in flip and the breakout bar looks weak -> sell its close expecting no follow-through; mirror for bulls.
- Never use limit orders countertrend; never buy intending to scalp and then short the low 2 if the market is still always-in short.
- Surprises in trends are with-trend: "weak low 1, one more push up" is usually wrong.
- A bull reversal bar at a new low in a weak bear channel is bear scalpers covering; buying above it on a stop loses ~70% of the time with average loss > average win.

### 24.D Beginner guidelines (his list)
- Only trades with reward >= risk; starting out, reward >= 2x.
- Only trades you think will probably work; assume P >= 60% if the setup looks good; do not worry how far it might go.
- Enter only on stops.
- Always a protective stop in the market.
- Always a profit-taking limit order in the market.
- Buy only above bull bars; short only below bear bars.
- Small size: if you think 300 shares, trade 100 ("I don't care" mode); if 10 Eminis, trade 2-3; 1 contract when starting regardless of account.
- 3-5 reasonable trades/day; if in doubt, stay out.
- Simple strategies; if unclear, wait.
- Best starting choices: trends that develop in the opening range, strong trend reversals, pullbacks in strong trends.
- Same size, same stop, same target on every trade.
- Never convert swing -> scalp, scalp -> swing, or scalp -> target smaller than risk; if tempted, place brackets and walk away for an hour.
- Patience fix: limit to 3 trades/day; avoid entering on breakouts; enter on pullbacks only.
- Other than strong reversals: buy only when most recent bars are above the EMA; sell only when mostly below; if both, wait.
- No countertrend scalps; no 1-minute charts (reversals every 3-10 bars, most become flags).
- Do not trade in tight trading ranges (worst environment for stop entries; institutions enter on limits there).
- Worst setup to avoid: midday, middle of the range, flat EMA, not a clear trend day.
- Do not buy breakouts of trading ranges on stops; wait for a clear strong breakout and enter the pullback.
- If confused, assume the market is in or entering a trading range: buy low, sell high only.
- Must be able to do the math before entry; if the market is too fast, wait for a later entry.

### 24.E Scalp vs swing, sizing, scaling, management
- Emini, ADR 10-15 pts: risk ~2 pts; swing target 4 pts (2x); scalp 2-4 pts then swing the balance.
- Range traders target 2-3 pts; reversal traders swing >= 4 pts.
- OCO bracket: profit limit 2-4 pts, stop <= 2 pts, then walk away for an hour.
- Once a trend is established: scalp most with-trend entries for 2 pts risking ~2 pts; swing some if very strong.
- Stock basket (>= 5M shares/day, ADR >= $2): 1-2 trades/day netting ~1/3 of ADR on the scalp portion, risking ~half the reward.
- Stock stop: beyond the signal bar (rarely > $1, usually < $0.60) until the entry bar closes; after a trend bar in your direction, move the stop to just beyond the entry bar.
- Stock breakeven: with ADR $3, after $0.60-0.80 open profit move the stop to breakeven minus a few cents (bought $120.10, hit $120.80 -> stop $120.07).
- Stock scaling: half off at $1 on a limit; trail beyond 5m swing lows; take more at $2; let the rest run to the stop or a clear strong opposite signal.
- AAPL bear: entry $169.88, stop above the outside entry bar $170.38 (risk $0.50), placed before the bar closed; do not move to breakeven until ~$0.60-0.80 open profit; half off at $1.00 on a limit; then stop breakeven + a few cents ($170.91).
- AAPL bear: stopped at breakeven to the penny on a breakout test -> re-short the EMA/breakout test even though 41 cents worse.
- Emini bull: initial risk 14 ticks below the signal bar; after the next strong two-bar reversal tighten to 11 ticks below entry; with P 60% the minimum target = actual risk (11 ticks) -> limit at >= 12 ticks above the signal-bar high; it reached 15.
- Alternate breakeven rule: move to breakeven once halfway to the target and after several closes beyond the prior range.
- Swing part of every with-trend entry on a strong trend day; at each new setup add back the scalp contracts (or stack a full position: swing becomes 2-4x); most should just re-add the scalp portion or scale out at levels.
- Scale-in: in a strong trend / bottom-of-range long, scaling in lower (ideally larger) turns a 60% setup into ~80%+ wins.
- Wide stop + long hold converts "60% for 2 pts vs 2 pts" to "~90% for 4 pts vs 8 pts".
- Extreme-range days (2008 crash, 10-pt risk): cut size to 20% of normal; same setups.
- Hold through pullbacks as long as the premise is valid; tighten to breakeven only after the trend resumes following a test of the entry price.
- 17-tick failure: bears taking profit at 16 ticks (one tick shy of 4 pts) below the signal bar and the bar closes bullish -> exit above the bull reversal bar with the smaller profit (11 ticks).
- When a move has an increased chance of coming up short (compressed ADR, small initial risk), set the limit one tick short of the obvious target (15 instead of 16) or at 3 pts.
- Low-range day: today's range 2 pts vs recent ADR > 10 and sub-6-pt days rare -> expect the range to double or triple before the close -> take the 50% / 4-tick-risk / 16-tick-reward short at a lower high.
- Minor top short followed by a high 2 with a bull signal bar at the EMA -> exit or reverse to long; mirror for a minor bottom long followed by a low 2 with a bear signal bar at the EMA.
- After a final flag in a bull, wait for >= 10 bars and two legs to the EMA before buying again.
- Buying a low in a bear trend: scalp at least part at the EMA test / prior swing low; stop to breakeven; exit if two bear trend bars follow unless the trend line break was strong.
- Buying a pullback vs the spike: pullback entry allows a stop below the pullback low instead of below the spike bottom; less reward, less risk.
- Cautious bulls skipping a high 1: buy stop one tick above the trend high so trend resumption is not missed.
- Size vs stop: fixed dollar risk; GS example risk $2.50/share with $500 max -> 200 shares; half off at $2.50 below entry (1:1), rest breakeven until always-in flips / stop / final-hour reversal / close.

### 24.G What he avoids
- Countertrend scalps (negative equation; a friend became profitable only after stopping them).
- Tight trading ranges, barbwire, midday flat-EMA middle-of-range setups ("the absolute worst thing to do").
- Stop-entry breakouts of trading ranges.
- 1-minute / tick / volume charts for execution.
- Reversals missing any of: trend line break, momentum reversal leg, strong reversal bar, test of the extreme.
- Weak signal bars (dojis = one-bar trading ranges); buying above a trading range or selling below one.
- Trades whose math he cannot evaluate before entry.
- A sudden large trend bar after 30-60 minutes of no signals.
- Trading during reports, when upset, distracted or tired; reversal trades after a losing month.

## Ch25: Trading guidelines (all 78, condensed)
1. Knowledge is necessary but not sufficient; turning it into skill is hard work.
2. Everything is a gray fog; close is close enough; if it looks like a reliable pattern it trades like one.
3. No easy rules; zero-sum; edge is small and fleeting; must trade a positive equation more than half the time.
4. The edge can never be large; a trade cannot have high P of a big reward relative to risk.
5. Reading charts is half the job; trading is the other half and equally hard.
6. A mathematical edge always exists for both long and short; when relatively clear it is fleeting; trade then.
7. The key skill is spotting positive-equation trades: stop above, limit above, limit below, stop below.
8. The most important decision, made after every bar closes: more buyers or sellers above and below the prior bar; critical at breakouts and failed breakouts because they set the always-in direction.
9. Every stop buy above a bar meets a limit seller; nothing is certain; the edge is small.
10. Every bar, even a strong trend bar, is a signal bar for both directions; be open to the exact opposite.
11. For every setup, consider what a breakout of the opposite end of the signal bar means; a buy setup can be a great sell setup if it traps longs.
12. Assess whether a breakout trend bar will succeed, be met with profit taking and a pullback, or reverse.
13. Weigh signs of strength and weakness; a setup that failed an opportunity to be strong is weaker.
14. If you are positive the setup is good, don't take it; you are missing what the other side sees; arrogance inflates P.
15. Nothing is as it seems; most strong range breakouts fail; 60% is 60% only 90% of the time; scaling in lower or a wide stop with a long hold can turn 60% into 80-90% on larger targets.
16. The most important daily activity is talking yourself out of bad trades: on a range day do not buy after a strong bull bar or high 1 near the top, nor short after a strong bear bar or low 1 near the bottom.
17. Inertia: in a trend 80% of reversal attempts fail (flag, resumption); in a range 80% of breakout attempts fail.
18. If a pullback "going too far" makes you feel twisted inside, the trend has probably reversed.
19. Trade the trade you have, not the one you expect; take the strong sell setup even if you think it should go up.
20. Price is truth; fundamentals are useless for a day trader; the market is rarely more than 60% certain of anything.
21. Everything makes sense; learn to read fast enough to see it in real time.
22. "It's not fair" means take a break; it is all math; emulate the computers: tireless, memoryless, objective; copy what they do.
23. Price action reflects human behavior; it works on all markets, countries, time frames.
24. Always have a protective stop in the market; the danger is yourself (denial, arrogance, lack of discipline).
25. No savior (TV experts, newsletters, chat rooms, indicators) will help; do your own analysis.
26. Those who talk don't know; don't watch TV or read news.
27. If you skipped two winning Emini trades in a row you are too large; if 3 contracts feel comfortable trade 1; size so you can take any decent signal and remain comfortable after 2-3 consecutive losses; cherry-picking leads to a blown account.
28. "I don't care": small size, no TV, no 1/3-minute, tick or volume charts, no "overdone" feeling, no indicators or divergences without a trend line break; only the one chart and your rules.
29. If a great trade needs a far stop, cut size to about a quarter so dollar risk is unchanged; but spot the good setup first.
30. The market is never certain it has gone far enough but always certain when it has gone too far; most reversals require excess.
31. Reversing a position is hard; better to exit (even at a loss) and look for a new setup.
32. No reliable countertrend patterns; never trade countertrend without a strong break of a significant trend line and a reasonable always-in reversal setup; 80% of reversals fail.
33. Any reversal setup justifies partial or full profit taking; view each top as the start of a bull flag and each bottom as the start of a bear flag.
34. Too early is always worse than too late; most reversals and breakouts fail; most trends go far.
35. All patterns fail and failures often fail, creating a breakout pullback in the original direction with high probability.
36. When one side is suddenly trapped, a scalp in the opposite direction is more reliable.
37. A stop run followed by trend resumption is as reliable as traders trapped in; at least a scalper's profit.
38. If no signals for 30-60 minutes and a sudden large trend bar breaks out of a range, wait; you have lost touch and it is a trap.
39. You don't have to trade; the goal is money, not trades; don't trade to relieve boredom.
40. Simple is better: one chart, no indicators; two charts make you take only simultaneous signals and reject most good ones.
41. Decide hobby or job; as a hobby it is too expensive and addictive.
42. Start on the 5m: enter pullbacks on stop orders; bull trend -> buy above a bull bar at the EMA; bear trend -> short below a bear bar at the EMA; take some or all off on a limit near the prior trend extreme; move the stop to breakeven on the rest.
43. Start with 200-500 SPY shares (1 Emini ~ 500 SPY); switch to Emini at 1,000-1,500 SPY.
44. Buy low, sell high, except in a clear strong trend (buy a high 2 even at the high of the day; sell a low 2 in a bear); the market is in a range most of the time; if you cannot name the established trend pattern, don't buy high.
45. Profitable traders feel uncertainty and urgency, not fear and greed; certainty/urgency = strong trend -> buy at least a small position at market rather than wait.
46. Uncertain/confused = trading range -> buy low, sell high; scalp if trading much; up 5-10 bars -> shorts only; down 5-10 bars -> longs only.
47. In a range, buy low = cover shorts near the bottom and buy on a strong signal; sell high = take long profits and short on a good setup near the top.
48. Good fill, bad trade: be suspicious of a better-than-expected fill.
49. First 1-2 hours are the easiest to make money (big swings, few dojis) and to lose it; many reversals; wait for a swing setup (P < 50%, reward >= 2x); experienced traders can scalp.
50. Down in the second half of the day: follow your rules into the close; do not change style, size up or take lower-probability setups.
51. Avoid midday, middle of the range, flat EMA, tight range with prominent tails (barbwire); ask "is this one of the best of the day, the one institutions waited for?"
52. A tight trading range is the worst environment for stop entries; institutions do the opposite.
53. A tight trading range trumps everything; once you sense one forming take no trades, even for hours.
54. Every bar/series is a trend or a range; pick the always-in direction and trade only that way until it changes; around 8:30 PST decide which trend pattern the day resembles; if you take any trade you must take every with-trend trade; never countertrend unless you took all the with-trend trades.
55. Best signal bars are trend bars in your direction; dojis are one-bar ranges and terrible signal bars; buying above or selling below a trading range usually loses.
56. Most countertrend setups fail, most with-trend setups succeed; trends form great-looking countertrend and lousy-looking with-trend setups; countertrend setups in strong trends become great with-trend setups, especially on the 1m.
57. No consistent money until countertrend scalps stop; with risk = target, four losses need six winners; scalp only at 60%+ wins; reward >= risk; beginners scalp with trend only, if at all.
58. Until consistently profitable, reward >= risk; risk 2 pts -> take at least 2 pts; risking 2 to make 1 needs 80% accuracy.
59. Three variables; any positive result is a good trade (high P / 1:1, low P / huge reward, or anything between).
60. Experienced traders scale in/out to improve the equation (later entries have higher P).
61. You will not make money until you trade with-trend pullbacks.
62. You will not make money on reversals until you wait for a significant trend line break and a strong reversal bar on a test of the extreme.
63. Print the 5m Emini chart daily and annotate every setup, for years.
64. Find a style compatible with your personality; stress means you have not found it.
65. Do not take risky trades in the final hours that you would not take in the first hours.
66. Computers have speed, no emotion, no fatigue: don't trade during reports, when upset/distracted, or when tired (end of day).
67. Always look for two legs; two failed attempts at something -> the opposite will likely succeed.
68. Never cherry-pick; either swing the best 2-3 setups/day or scalp every valid setup (the latter is for unusual personalities).
69. Avoiding losers is the key; most losers: middle of the range with weak signal bars (small dojis, mid closes); entering a reversal too early (wait for the second entry); denial of a trend via 1/3-minute reversals; believing a TV expert's "bottom is in".
70. If a trade is not doing what you expected, pretend you are flat; if you would not put it on now, get out.
71. Do not scalp when you should swing or swing when you should scalp; until profitable, swing 1-3 trades/day and do not scalp (scalping needs >60% wins at 1:1).
72. Converting swings to scalps, or exiting scalps below risk, loses money; use bracket orders and walk away for an hour.
73. If you lost money last month: no reversals; if 7 of the last 10 bars are mostly above the EMA, only buy; if 7 of 10 are below, only short.
74. Beginners take only the best trades even if that means hours without a trade.
75. Discipline is the most important trait; if you cannot hold it all day, do it for the first hour and grow size.
76. Second most important trait: the ability to do nothing for hours; do not let boredom convince you it has been too long.
77. Increase position size rather than trade count or setup variety; 2 pts/day on 50 contracts is seven figures.
78. Perfected trading skills can fund your dreams.

## Numeric candidates for the engine
- Equation gate: take a trade iff P_est * reward > (1 - P_est) * risk with a margin; P_est = 0.60 for a qualifying setup, 0.70 for confluence (second entry + strong with-trend signal bar + trend context), 0.50 for an uncertain one (then require reward >= 2 * risk).
- Stop size: initial stop 1 tick beyond the signal bar; reject if risk > tallest bar in the last 20 bars or risk < 25% of the median bar range; default MES/MNQ stop ~ 8 Emini-equivalent ticks scaled by ATR; on large-bar days allow 2-3x the normal stop with target >= stop.
- Target: scalp target = risk (1:1) when P_est >= 0.60; swing target = 2 * risk when P_est in [0.50, 0.60); optional half out at 1x, remainder to 2x or trail below swing lows; set the limit 1 tick short of the obvious target when ADR is compressed.
- Breakeven rule: move the stop to breakeven (+/- 1-2 ticks) only after open profit >= 0.5 * target AND at least one with-trend trend bar has closed after entry; after a scalp exit at 1x rely on the breakeven stop; after a breakeven stop-out on a breakout test, re-enter on the next with-trend signal.
- Entry mechanics: stop entries 1 tick beyond the signal bar; long only above a bull-body signal bar, short only below a bear-body signal bar; reject doji signal bars (body < 1/3 of range) unless second entry at a trend channel line overshoot or the counter-move had many opposing bodies.
- Always-in filter: >= 7 of the last 10 closes above the EMA -> longs only; >= 7 of 10 below -> shorts only; otherwise range mode (fade only).
- Trend-day detection: after the first 60-120 minutes, if <= 2 bars have closed on the wrong side of the EMA since the spike, flag a strong trend day; take every high 2 / low 2 / wedge / triangle at the EMA; swing part of each; re-add the scalp portion on each new signal.
- Small-pullback trend day: no pullback to the EMA in 20+ bars -> limit entries at a fixed distance = the largest prior pullback from the most recent extreme; stop beyond the 4th bar of the day; first EMA touch expected to fail and the extreme to be retested.
- Range mode: up >= 5 bars inside the range -> shorts only (low 2 near the prior swing high); down >= 5 bars -> longs only (high 2 near the prior swing low); require second entries when the move into the extreme was strong; expect 60-80% chance of a test of the opposite side.
- Tight-range lockout: >= 5 consecutive bars whose ranges mostly overlap the prior 2-3 bars' union with EMA slope ~ 0 -> no stop entries until a bar closes outside the range and a pullback forms; P(breakout continuing the prior trend) <= 0.55.
- Midday filter: no trades in the middle third of the day's range when EMA slope ~ 0 and the day is not a confirmed trend day.
- Spike entry (experienced mode): strong breakout spike of 2-4 consecutive trend bars with small tails -> enter at the close of bar 2-4; stop at the far end of the spike; size reduced; P_est 0.60 at 1:1; if very strong, risk 0.5 * spike height with P_est 0.70 for 2x.
- First pullback after a spike: 4-bar spike with moderate bodies (not climactic), first low 2 / high 2 to the EMA with a with-trend signal bar -> P_est 0.70 at 1:1.
- High 1 / low 1 validity: only in a strong spike and not after a climax bar (climax = bar range > 2x the 20-bar average range after a 10-20+ bar trend); after a climax, wait >= 10 bars and two legs to the EMA before the next with-trend entry.
- MTR checklist (all required): trend line break; reversal leg with >= 2-3 trend bars; test of the extreme (HH/LH/DT or LL/HL/DB); strong reversal signal bar; then P_est 0.50-0.60 at 2x, 0.70 if also a wedge + prominent opposing bodies + trend channel line overshoot.
- MTR secondary rule: if the test leg goes far beyond the prior extreme, reset and require a new trend line break and a new test; after a violent reversal leg expect a higher low rather than a lower low.
- Stairs rule: third or fourth push in a stairs pattern -> fade with target = the prior push's extreme.
- Measured move handling: at a spike-and-channel or trending-range MM target with weakening momentum (overlap, tails, opposing bodies) stop adding and treat as a fade candidate; expect a touch to the tick, then a pause or reversal.
- Final flag: a tight range / ii / 2-bar flag after a climactic bar whose breakout fails within 2 bars -> reversal entry on the failed breakout; after consecutive climaxes expect >= 10 bars / two legs of correction.
- Failed-breakout tick rules: a 1-tick new extreme that reverses = double top/bottom fade; a 5-tick failure (move reaches only 5 ticks past entry then reverses) = opposite signal; a 17-tick failure (bull close 16-17 ticks below the signal bar with a 4-pt target) = exit on the next opposing bar.
- Breakout test: after a swing entry, a pullback that reaches the entry price within 1-2 ticks then reverses back = add / re-enter; stop below the test bar.
- Micro channel: >= 4 consecutive bars with no overlap of the prior bar's extreme; do not fade its first breakout; enter on the first pullback after the breakout (breakout pullback); in a strong trend above the EMA a counter micro channel's failed breakout becomes a with-trend breakout pullback.
- EMA gap bar: first gap bar against a strong trend = with-trend entry on the next with-trend signal; second EMA gap bar in a counter move = second-entry fade; 30+ consecutive gap bars -> expect an EMA touch within 10 bars (P > 0.95).
- Second-entry rule: if the first signal against strong momentum fails, the second signal within ~5 bars gets P_est + 0.10.
- Low-range day: if the range after 60-90 minutes < 0.3 * 20-day ADR, expect range expansion to 2-3x before the close; allow the 50% / >= 2x lower-high or higher-low entry with a tight stop (4 ticks in his example).
- Opening range: initial swing often starts with a double top/bottom; opening range ~0.5 * ADR -> expect a trending-trading-range breakout then a pullback test; opening reversals P_est 0.30 for 2x by default, 0.60 only with the strong-setup checklist (EMA pullback + double bottom with bar 1 + strong reversal bar).
- Trade budget: 3-5 entries/day in beginner mode, 5-10 in standard mode; no entries during scheduled reports; no new countertrend entries in the last hour; no reversal trades in the month after a losing month.
- Sizing: fixed dollar risk per trade; contracts = floor(max_risk / (stop_ticks * tick_value)); when the required stop is >= 2x normal, cut contracts to keep dollar risk constant (down to 20-25% of normal size on extreme-range days).
- Size progression: 1 contract until profitable for several consecutive weeks; then at most 25% of the maximum affordable size.
- VIX regime input: expected daily range % ~ VIX / 16; scale stops and targets by it.
