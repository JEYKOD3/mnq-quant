## 1. The natural laws of trading

- Consistent set of actions -> consistent results. Losing streaks trigger urges (remove stop, double down, switch strategy); giving in breaks consistency.
- You must have an edge. Analogy: coin toss, heads +$2, tails -$1. Without an edge, risk management/discipline/psychology do not save you.
- Law of large numbers: short-run results are random; results converge to expectancy only over a large sample (10 tosses can be 6/4, 1000 tosses approach 50/50). Do not abandon a strategy after a few losses.
- Take care of the downside. Example: strategy with 50% win rate, 1:2 R:R, sequence L L L L W W W W. Risking 25%/trade: -25% x4 = blown up. Risking 1%/trade: -1 x4, +2 x4 = +4%.
- You need money to make money: at 20%/yr, $1k -> $200, $100k -> $20k, $1M -> $200k. Treat 20%/yr as a "consistent" return, not 100%.
- Compounding: $100k at 20%/yr with no withdrawals = $619,173 after 10 yrs, $3,833,759 after 20, $23,737,631 after 30. Biggest money is OPM (hedge funds).
- No "best" strategy: "best" is undefined (returns, drawdown, time, capital). Define goals first, then pick.
- No strategy works all the time: market conditions change (trend/range, low/high vol), so drawdowns are inevitable. Keep profits in good times to pay for bad times.
- Daily profits require trade frequency so LLN plays out intraday (1 toss/day = random daily P&L; 1000 tosses/day = near-certain daily profit). Retail is handicapped by commissions and inability to spot enough setups by eye; HFT dominates (Virtu: 1 losing day in 1,238, 2009-2014). Expect profit per quarter/year, not per day.
- Get-rich-slow: $5k at 20%/yr for 20 yrs = $191,688; adding $5k/yr = $1,311,816. Risk small, add funds, compound.
- One line of motivation: play defense so you survive to see the good times.

## 2. What price action trading is

- Definition: understanding the imbalance between buying and selling pressure to time entries/exits. Price moves on pressure, not on count of buyers vs sellers (100 buyers x 1 share vs 1 seller x 1M shares -> price falls).
- Claimed benefits: ignore fundamental news (AMD negative earnings 2014-2016 yet +700% in 12 months; news is chosen after the fact to explain the move); price is objective, indicators can be tuned to fit bias (RSI period changes oversold into overbought); price is faster than lagging indicators; price action gives a framework for different market conditions.
- Indicators are not useless: use them to filter setups and manage trades, not to time entries.
- Price action is a framework, not a strategy. When the market changes, the strategy must change.
- Downsides he states: near-impossible to backtest accurately (hindsight bias, recording errors); forward testing takes months (short-term) to 1-2 years (long-term), so keep a job; subjectivity is unavoidable, the framework exists to minimize it.
- Less manipulation, not none: illiquid markets can still be pushed by deep pockets.

## 3. Market structure (Wyckoff / Weinstein four stages)

- Four stages cycle: accumulation -> advancing -> distribution -> declining. All stage definitions are given on the DAILY timeframe with a 200-day MA.
- Accumulation: occurs after price has fallen for 5+ months; looks like a range with obvious S/R inside a downtrend; 200 MA flattens; price swings back and forth around the 200 MA. Can still break DOWN (downtrend resumes).
- Advancing (uptrend): triggered by breakout of accumulation resistance; series of higher highs and higher lows; price above 200 MA; 200 MA starting to point up.
- Distribution: occurs after price has risen for 5+ months; range with obvious S/R inside an uptrend; 200 MA flattens; price whips around the 200 MA. Can still break UP.
- Declining (downtrend): triggered by breakdown of distribution support; lower highs and lower lows; price below 200 MA; 200 MA pointing down.
- Use: accumulation -> look for longs (at support lows, breakout of resistance, first pullback after breakout). Declining -> look for shorts near an area of value (resistance, MA).
- If structure is not obvious: stay out, trade a cleaner market. Do not force a read.

## 4. Support and resistance

- Support: horizontal AREA where potential buying pressure could push price higher. Resistance: horizontal area where selling pressure could push price lower. They are "areas of value" to buy low / sell high.
- Drawing rules: (1) zoom out to at least 300 candles; (2) draw only the most obvious levels (if you second-guess, skip it); (3) adjust the level to maximize touches.
- Drawn as lines for cleanliness but TREATED as areas. Reason: FOMO traders enter at first touch (shallow reversal), "cheapo" traders enter at the lows of the zone (deep reversal); you cannot know which group dominates, so expect a reversal anywhere inside the zone.
- Contrarian rule: the MORE times S/R is tested in a SHORT period, the WEAKER it gets. Each retest fills institutional resting orders; when they are consumed, nobody is left and the level breaks. Applies to MA, trendline, channel areas too.
- Role reversal: broken support becomes resistance, broken resistance becomes support. Three explanations: trapped losers exit at breakeven; traders who missed the breakout place limit orders at the breakout level; self-fulfilling prophecy. Corollary: stick to popular TA concepts because only widely-used tools get self-fulfilled.
- Other areas of value: moving averages (in a "healthy trend" price respects the 50-period MA), trendlines, channels.
- When S/R breaks (no certainty, only tendencies): resistance tends to break in an uptrend (HH needs it); support tends to break in a downtrend; higher lows into resistance (ascending triangle) = strength, resistance likely breaks; lower highs into support (descending triangle) = weakness, support likely breaks.
- S/R says where, not when.

## 5. Candlestick patterns

- Each candle: open, high, low, close. Bullish candle: open below close. Bearish: open above close.
- Three pattern types named: reversal, indecision, continuation. Only reversal patterns are detailed. Never trade them in isolation; context (structure + area of value) comes first.

Bullish reversals (form after a decline):
- Hammer (1 candle): little/no upper shadow; close in the top 1/4 of the range; lower shadow about 2-3x body length. Meaning: sellers pushed down, buyers reversed and closed above the open = rejection of lower prices.
- Bullish engulfing (2 candles): candle 1 closes bearish; candle 2 body completely covers candle 1 body (shadows ignored); candle 2 closes bullish. Text also says close above previous candle's high.
- Piercing (2): candle 1 bearish; candle 2 body closes beyond the halfway (50%) mark of candle 1 body; candle 2 bullish. Weaker than engulfing.
- Tweezer bottom (2): candle 1 shows rejection of lower prices; candle 2 retests candle 1 low and closes higher. Two failed attempts lower.
- Morning star (3): candle 1 bearish; candle 2 small range (indecision); candle 3 closes aggressively higher, more than 50% into candle 1.

Bearish reversals (form after an advance), mirror images:
- Shooting star (1): little/no lower shadow; close in bottom 1/4 of range; upper shadow about 2-3x body.
- Bearish engulfing (2): candle 1 bullish; candle 2 body covers candle 1 body; candle 2 bearish (close below previous low per text).
- Dark cloud cover (2): candle 1 bullish; candle 2 closes below the 50% mark of candle 1 body; bearish. Weaker than engulfing.
- Tweezer top (2): candle 1 rejects higher prices; candle 2 retests the high and closes lower.
- Evening star (3): bullish, small range, then aggressive bearish close more than 50% into candle 1.

Universal read (replaces memorization), two questions:
- Q1 Where did price close relative to the range? Close near highs = buyers in control; near lows = sellers in control, even if the candle is technically bullish.
- Q2 What is the candle size relative to earlier ones? Current candle 2x or more larger than prior candles = conviction/strength; same size = no strength.
- Purpose: candlesticks are ENTRY TRIGGERS (the "when"), nothing more.

## 6. Risk and trade management

Stop hunting:
- Institutions need liquidity; to go long with less slippage they push price below support to trigger the stop cluster below it, buy the resulting sell orders, then price rallies. Hence: do NOT put stops just below support lows / just above resistance highs; do not set stops by feeling or account balance.

Stop placement:
- Lean the stop against a "barrier" (area of value: S/R, swing high/low, trendline) plus a buffer.
- Concrete rule: stop = lows of support minus 1 ATR (long); highs of resistance plus 1 ATR (short).
- Stop must sit where the setup is invalidated (bull flag long -> stop below flag lows; anything above keeps the pattern intact).
- Why 1 ATR and not 0.5 or 2: multiple controls breathing room vs stop size; there is no "best" multiple.

Risk per trade and drawdown math:
- Loss 10% needs +11.11% to recover; 50% needs +100%; 90% needs +900%.
- Risk not more than 1% of account per trade; 20 consecutive losses = roughly 20% drawdown, survivable.

Position sizing:
- Forex: Position size = Amount risked / (stop loss in pips x value per pip). Example: $10k account, 1% = $100, stop 200 pips, $10/pip per standard lot -> 100/(200x10) = 0.05 lot (5 micro lots).
- Stocks: Position size = Amount risked / stop size per share. Example: $50k, 1% = $500, stop $2.50 -> 200 shares.
- Wider stop -> smaller size at constant % risk.

Exit methods:
- Capture a swing (swing trading): exit before opposing pressure arrives (long: exit at/just before swing highs, resistance, upper channel; the text says swing lows/support/lower channel for where selling pressure comes in, i.e. the next obstacle). Pros: higher win rate, less pain. Con: misses big moves.
- Ride a trend: only possible with a trailing stop. Expect average win 2-3x average loss, win rate 30-45%, give back 30-50% of open profit, winners often turn to losers.
  - MA trail: exit on close below 200-day MA (long-term); 100-day for medium-term; 300-day for longer-term. Objective.
  - Structure trail: exit if price closes below the previous swing low by more than 1 ATR. Subjective (swing identification).
  - ATR trail: 3 ATR trailing stop used in examples (ch. 9).
  - Previous-candle trail: exit on close beyond the previous day's high (short) / low (long), used in MAEE variation and the GBPUSD counter-trend example.
- Hybrid: scale out part at the first obstacle, trail the rest. Rule: exit NO MORE than 50% at target 1; example: exiting 90% and letting 10% run to 10R nets only 1R. Downsides: never full size in a good trend; if T1 hits then reverses you may end breakeven/small loss where a pure swing exit would have won.
- No best method; pick one you can execute consistently.

## 7. Trading formulas (strategies)

### MAEE formula: reversal / pullback trading (Market structure, Area of value, Entry trigger, Exits)
- Context: classify stage (accumulation/advancing/distribution/declining). If unclassifiable, skip the market.
- Setup: price reaches an area of value in the direction implied by structure (resistance in a declining stage, support in accumulation, etc.). Area can be S/R, trendline, channel, MA.
- Entry: trigger = reversal candlestick (hammer, engulfing...) OR any other trigger (chart pattern, "higher close", RSI crossing above 30, lower-timeframe swing break). Entry is on the next candle's open after the trigger.
- Stop: 1 ATR beyond the area of value (above resistance highs for shorts, below support/trigger-candle lows for longs).
- Target: nearest swing low/high (fixed target), or a trailing stop.
- Management: fixed target for swing capture, or trail (e.g. exit when price closes beyond previous day's high for a short).
- Book examples: GBPUSD daily declining, pullback to 1.2750, bearish engulfing, stop 1 ATR above resistance, target swing low ~1.2550. Copper 8h accumulation, hammer at 2.600 support, stop 1 ATR below hammer low, target ~2.720. AUDJPY daily declining, gap up into 76.00 resistance then bearish engulfing, target ~74.50. NZDUSD: daily declining into 0.6450 resistance; on 4h a lower high/lower low forms, entry = break of 4h swing low, stop 1 ATR above resistance, trail by previous day's high.
- Bonus tips (quality filters):
  1. Power move into the area of value: big-bodied candles with little/no pullback. The move is a "liquidity gap" (few transactions), so reversal is easy and the nearest swing is far away (better R:R).
  2. Strong price rejection: trigger candle range at least 1.5x ATR and close near the extreme (near lows for shorts).
  3. The more significant the area, the stronger the reversal: multi-year highs/lows, untested-for-a-long-time areas, areas that produced strong reversals before. Failed breakout traders' stops fuel the reversal.

### MBEE formula: breakout trading (Market structure, Buildup, Entry trigger, Exits)
- Context: accumulation (or distribution) stage, or an intact trend; also works in a plain range.
- Setup: buildup = series of narrow-range candles / tight consolidation AT the level (resistance for longs). Low volatility -> tight stop -> better R:R when volatility expands. Buildup at resistance = buyers willing to buy high or sellers absent = strength. Shapes: "box", or higher lows into resistance (ascending triangle). Variation: buildup can form AFTER the breakout (EURUSD example) rather than before.
- Entry: buy stop order above the highs of the buildup/resistance (sell stop below lows for shorts). Trade what you see, not what you think.
- Stop: 1 ATR below the lows of the buildup (or 1 ATR below previous swing low, or below the ascending trendline).
- Target: trailing stop preferred (potential transition into advancing stage); or fixed target at next S/R for swing capture.
- Management: trailing technique left to the trader (3 ATR used in examples).
- Bonus tips:
  1. Don't trade into "traffic": no S/R between entry and target. EURGBP example: short buildup with support ~0.8500 just below -> risk $1 to make $0.3, skip.
  2. 20-period MA as guide: best buildups have "digested" the move; breakout is ready when the 20 MA has caught up to the lows of the buildup (highs for shorts). Stop 1 ATR below buildup lows then also sits near the 20 MA.
  3. Tighter buildup = better (stored energy). Avoid loose buildups with obvious swing highs/lows: larger stop, and the highs of a loose buildup are lower-timeframe resistance.

### DERR formula: process (Develop plan, Execute, Record, Review)
- Plan must answer six questions: timeframe (lower TF only if ~10 hrs/day available, else daily/weekly); markets; setup conditions (e.g. 50-day high, 200-day high, highest close of 100 days, plus filters like uptrend or low vol); exit when wrong (setup-invalidation level); exit when right (fixed target for swing, trailing stop for trend); risk per trade (max 1%); units (position size formula).
- Template plan: Russell 1000 stocks, daily TF; if price above 200-day MA and makes a 200-day high, go long at next candle's open; trailing stop 20% from the highs; max loss 1% of capital.
- Execute: outcomes are breakeven, small win, small loss, big win, big loss; eliminate big losses. Execute the same setup at least 100 times before judging it.
- Record per trade: date, timeframe, setup type, market, price in, price out, stop loss, P&L in R (risk $5 make $10 = 2R). Save three charts: higher timeframe (one TF above entry: big picture, trend, structure, areas of value), entry timeframe (setup, entry, stop marked), post-trade chart (R result, plan followed?, why rules broken).
- Review after 100+ trades. Expectancy = (Win% x Avg win) - (Loss% x Avg loss) - (Commission + Slippage). Example: 50/50, $1000 avg win, $500 avg loss, $10 costs -> (0.5x1000) - (0.5x500) - 10 = $240/trade. Positive = edge.
- Review actions: find patterns behind winners (his: breakouts after a buildup; no buildup = no trade), patterns behind losers, remove large losses (95% rule-following still loses if 5% rule-breaking produces huge losses; name the fear, write prevention steps).

## 8. Advanced techniques (nine)

1. Pre-breakout technique: while the higher TF forms a buildup, drop to a lower TF and look for price rejection at the lows of the buildup (a lower-TF swing low/support). Go long on the next candle. This is MAEE on multiple timeframes; use a trailing stop so the eventual breakout is captured.
2. Trend classification by pullback depth (uptrend version; mirror for downtrend):
   - Strong trend: pullback not more than 38% retracement; price holds the 20-period MA. Hard to buy pullbacks (too fast); trade breakouts, or use the pre-breakout technique.
   - Healthy trend: pullback not more than 50%; price holds the 50-period MA; prior resistance retested as support near the 50 MA. Trade the pullback/bounce at the 50 MA.
   - Weak trend: pullback at least 62%; buying appears near the 200-period MA. Do NOT buy breakouts (stopped out on the pullback); buy pullbacks to the 200 MA or support.
3. Don't trade with the trend when price is overstretched from the mean (e.g. far above the 50 MA in a healthy trend). Stop must go below the 50 MA to be valid -> wide stop, poor R:R; a tighter stop gets hit by noise. Wait for price to return to the MA.
4. Hidden strength/weakness via leg comparison: trending move = larger bodies/ranges, with-trend; retracement move = smaller bodies/ranges, counter-trend. Trend weakening = retracement legs grow larger (and trending legs shrink). Combine with structure + area of value to spot turns. NZDCAD example: daily at resistance + descending trendline; 8h shows larger counter-trend candles; entry = break and close below 8h support.
5. Market exhaustion via ATR: 20-period daily ATR of 100 pips = average 100 pips/day over 20 days. Not a hard limit, but a boundary for how far the day can go; use with S/R + structure + candles to filter reversal trades.
6. Not all markets are equal (credit Andrea Unger): test system = buy breakout of previous day's high; hold until price hits previous day's low, then reverse short; hold short until break above previous day's high; repeat. Rising equity curve (GBPUSD) = trending market, prefer trailing stops. Falling equity curve (AUDCAD) = mean-reverting, prefer fixed targets at the previous day's high/low. Also applicable on weekly TF.
7. Higher-TF structure decides trade management: HTF strong uptrend -> hold/trail; HTF potential distribution -> exit at nearest swing high; HTF potential accumulation -> sell half at resistance, hold half for the breakout. Keep it simple at first.
8. Volatility cycles: low vol precedes high vol and vice versa. High-vol entries need wide stops (poor R:R) and get stuck when vol collapses. Pros enter in low vol: tight stop, larger size at same risk, possible 1:5+ R:R. Find low-vol entries via MBEE or pre-breakout.

## 9. Trading examples (outcomes deliberately not given)

- NZDJPY daily, breakout (MBEE): potential accumulation, higher lows into 70.00 resistance, buildup, 20 MA caught up with buildup lows. Plan: buy stop above resistance highs; stop 1 ATR below buildup lows; trail 3 ATR to ride a possible advancing stage.
- EURNZD daily, breakout: plain range (not accumulation/distribution); price broke support and formed a buildup under old support (now resistance); 20 MA caught up with buildup highs. Plan: sell stop below buildup lows; stop 1 ATR above buildup highs; trail for trend, or swing target at 1.6600 (major swing low). Demonstrates the ATR-multiple reasoning (1 vs 0.5 vs 2 ATR = breathing room, no best value).
- Bitcoin daily, breakout: accumulation, ascending triangle into $4200, 20 MA supporting price. Plan: buy stop above resistance; stop 1 ATR below previous minor swing low; trail 3 ATR. Always trail in accumulation-to-advancing transitions because new trends are born there.
- EURCAD daily, reversal (MAEE): declining stage, swing into resistance, bearish engulfing (breakout buyers trapped). Short at next candle open; stop 1 ATR above the engulfing pattern; targets: nearest swing low 1.4550 or further 1.4450, placed a few pips before the level. Exit half at T1, half at T2, keep at least 50% for T2. Classified as a weak trend -> capture a swing, not trail (steep pullbacks erode open profit).
- WTI daily, reversal: range market, power move into support, traded below 51.15 then closed near the high of the day (failed breakdown, shorts trapped). Long at next open; stop 1 ATR below the low; targets 58.00 and 63.00. Raises the unanswered management question: price at 57.5 reversing, hold / breakeven / partial exit, decide in advance.
- GBPUSD daily, counter-trend reversal: declining stage, clean move into the swing low, bullish hammer (breakdown shorts trapped). Long at next open; stop 1 ATR below candle low; target before the recent swing high ~1.2290. Because counter-trend, trail under the previous candle's low: manual exit on a close below the previous day's low. Shows MAEE works with trend, in range, and counter-trend.
- USDINR daily, stay out: range, price in the middle after a support bounce. Long would risk $1.3 (entry 71.4, stop 70.1 = 1 ATR below support) to make $0.6 (target 72 before resistance). His minimum: 1 to 0.8 R:R before the first obstacle; acceptable R:R depends on win rate.
- S&P 500 daily, stay out: strong uptrend (price stays above the 20 MA for a sustained period) but no area of value, no buildup, nearest structure at $3150 -> no logical stop. Lower TFs (15-min) may still offer trades because their structure differs.
- US T-Bond daily, stay out: healthy trend, overextended beyond the 50 MA; proper stop must go below the 50 MA -> wide stop, small size, poor R:R. Let price come to the area of value.
- Gold daily, stay out (for now): advancing after a close above resistance, no nearby structure for a stop. Two plans: (1) retest of $1350 (old resistance) with bullish rejection -> enter next open, stop 1 ATR below support; (2) buildup forms -> wait for 20 MA to catch up to buildup lows, buy stop above buildup highs, stop 1 ATR below buildup lows.

## 10. Relevance to an intraday MNQ scalper built on Brooks / Wade second-entry pullbacks

- New and testable: the trend-strength taxonomy by pullback depth (<=38% / 20 MA = strong; <=50% / 50 MA = healthy; >=62% / 200 MA = weak) with a prescribed tactic per class (breakouts in strong, pullback buys at the 50 MA in healthy, no breakouts and 200 MA pullbacks in weak). Brooks has "strong trend / weak trend / trading range" but not fixed Fib thresholds; this gives you a regime filter you can code: measure last leg retracement, pick entry type.
- New and testable: "20 MA catches up to the buildup lows" as a breakout-readiness condition. Easy to encode on 1-5 min bars: consolidation lows within X ticks of the 20 EMA before accepting a breakout stop order. Brooks' equivalent is "breakout pullback to the EMA", similar spirit but Teo gives the specific MA length.
- New and testable: trigger quality filter = signal bar range >= 1.5x ATR AND close in top/bottom 25% of the range. Brooks talks about "strong signal bar" qualitatively; this is a numeric gate you can backtest directly on second-entry signal bars.
- New and testable: "candle 2x larger than recent candles = conviction". Combine with Q1 (close location) as a two-number bar-strength score.
- New and testable: trend vs mean-reversion diagnostic (buy prev-day high / sell prev-day low stop-and-reverse). Run it on MNQ by hour of day or by ATR regime to decide trail vs fixed target. Cheap to run on your 5-year Databento set.
- Partly new: the "more retests in a short window = weaker level" rule. Brooks also says the third push through a level usually works, so this is consistent but Teo frames it as a counting rule you can parameterize (N touches within M bars -> expect break, stop fading).
- Redundant with Brooks/Wade: stop at setup invalidation; trapped-trader logic (failed breakouts fuel reversals, WTI and GBPUSD examples are textbook Brooks failed breakouts); "don't trade in the middle of the range / no man's land"; scale-out at the first obstacle; let price come to the area of value; mandatory journaling. MAEE itself is just "context, location, signal bar, exits", which is Brooks' framework with different vocabulary.
- Contradicts or diverges: Teo's bar patterns are closed-bar triggers with entry at the NEXT bar's open, while Brooks/Wade enter on a stop one tick beyond the signal bar high/low (or a limit second-entry). Teo's 1 ATR buffer beyond the structure is wide for a scalper; Brooks uses signal-bar extreme plus one or two ticks. These are mutually exclusive stop models; test both, expect Teo's to lower win-rate variance but kill R:R on MNQ.
- Contradicts in spirit: Teo says accurate backtesting of discretionary price action is near impossible and recommends forward testing; your whole project is numeric replication. Take his rule set as hypotheses to test, not as a reason to skip testing. His "100 trades minimum before judging" is far too small for intraday; treat 100 as a floor per regime bucket, not per strategy.
- Not applicable as stated: the four stages are daily-TF, 200-day MA, 5-month constructs. Intraday you would have to re-scale (e.g. 200 EMA on 5-min, 1-2 session ranges) and the book gives no guidance on that translation, so any intraday version is your invention, not his.
- Position sizing and risk: nothing new; 1% per trade, size = risk / stop distance. Your prop-firm constraint (daily loss limit, trailing drawdown) is a harder cap than anything here. Teo's trend-riding stats (30-45% win rate, 2-3x average win, give back 30-50% of open profit) are the baseline to compare a 3-ATR trailing exit against your current fixed-R exits.
- Minimum R:R of 0.8:1 to the first obstacle is a usable gate: compute distance to the nearest prior swing / opening range level / VWAP vs stop size before firing a second-entry, skip if below the threshold. Cheap filter, likely to remove a chunk of the losing pullback trades that enter right under resistance ("traffic").
