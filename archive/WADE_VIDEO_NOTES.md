## Video 1: "5 Price Action Rules EVERY Trader NEEDS To Know" (youtube.com/watch?v=TegF3yYjnng, 31 min)

**Why price action works (0:00):** markets reflect human greed and fear, so the same patterns repeat. Indicators only show the past and clutter the chart. Read raw price.

### Rule 1: Trendline rule (1:15) [R01, R02, R03, R04]
- After a trendline break (candles CLOSE outside the channel), a **new extreme** is expected (new low in a downtrend, new high in an uptrend). Only after that new extreme: correction or reversal.
- A break alone is NOT a reversal. Don't start trading the other way on the break.
- "Triple confirmation" = 3 touches validate the channel (R01).
- Same rule applies to the short-term trendlines of the corrections inside a trend (3:00).
- After correction the trend can resume (bigger pattern) or reverse (4:25).
- A double top close to the high counts as a new high (5:05).

### Rule 2: Don't counter-trend trade (5:59) [R05]
- No picking tops and bottoms, however good the signal bar looks.
- Only trade the other way after a **proven trend in the opposite direction**.
- If you can't draw a clean trendline, still follow the overall bias.
- Markets make counter-trend entries look attractive and with-trend pullbacks look sketchy. Most reversal attempts fail.
- Don't sell long traps inside an uptrend: the trend is still in play.

### Rule 3: Trading range rule (8:25) [R06, R07]
- **Most breakouts of trading ranges fail** (about 90%), at least temporarily.
- If you trade a breakout, take the **breakout pullback**, never the breakout itself.
- In a range: buy low, sell high, fade breakouts.
- **Congestion = micro trading range. Avoid trading it.** (9:30)
- Even tiny consolidations: first breakout usually fails, then a breakout pullback is the real entry (12:15, R07).

### Rule 4: High-probability setup rule (12:46) [R08, R09, R10]
- **Markets move in pairs of twos**: two-legged pullbacks.
- High-probability setups:
  1. Second entry at a key entry point, with the trend
  2. Failed second entry against the trend
  3. Failed breakouts
  4. Higher low / lower high confirmation setups
- Setups mean nothing alone: **combine with a key entry point** (trendline, support/resistance, EMA 21 as a support tool).
- Best: two key entry points together (trendline + EMA) with a two-legged pullback (15:10, R09).
- Sell when price is below the EMA, buy when above (R08, R10).
- A 2nd entry short inside an uptrend is likely to fail; that **failed 2ES is a long setup** (F2ES), and is often also a 2EL (16:00, R10). Stop goes below the signal bar.
- Ignore entries far from the EMA / trendline: no chasing (17:00).

### Rule 5: Signal bar rule (17:23) [R11]
- Longs only above bullish bars, shorts only below bearish bars. The signal bar must confirm direction and momentum.
- Stronger context = signal bar matters less, but it must still be decent. A doji is not acceptable (31:00).

### Trading ranges checklist (18:26) [R12, R13]
1. Identify structure (uptrend, downtrend, range)
2. Locate key levels (support, resistance)
3. Locate trendlines (key entry points, trendline rule)
4. Careful trading in the middle of the range
5. Stay away from congestion
6. Look for setups that fade the breakout
7. Buy low, sell high
8. Don't chase after missing a trade. Wait for the next high-probability setup
9. Look for entries at the EMA
10. Identify a good signal bar
11. 2nd entries with the trend, failed 2nd entries against it

Example (20:10-23:05): sits patiently in the middle of the range; after 20 bullish bars, takes the 2nd entry long at the EMA even with an average signal bar because context is strong; "if I miss it, I can't go long all the way up here, my scalp was already made".

### Uptrend checklist (23:14) [R14, R15]
1. Identify structure
2. No counter-trend attempts; even a medium trend must be followed
3. Locate trendlines (key entry points)
4. Draw short-term trendlines for the bearish corrections
5. Stay away from congestion
6. No entries at the top of the move. The deeper the correction, the higher the chance of success
7. **Long setups only: 2nd entries off the trendline or EMA (preferably both) and failed 2nd entries. First entries are too aggressive**
8. Don't chase (FOMO). Wait for the next setup
9. After a new high, buying is on hold until more confirmation
10. Good signal bar
- Several new extremes in a row = probably a bigger channel (spike and channel), typical of strong trends (24:00).
- Price failing to close below the EMA = still bullish bias.
- **After taking the entry he holds a runner** while the market extends; no new entries at the highs (25:08, 25:43).

### Downtrend checklist (26:47) [R16, R17]
Same as uptrend, reversed: shorts only on 2nd entries off the trendline/EMA and failed 2nd entries long; no entries at the lows; after a new low, selling is on hold.
- 2ES inside a sideways, stacked area above the EMA = skip (27:30).
- **Lower high confirmation** after a 2ES, below the EMA = better entry (28:00).
- **A micro double bottom resets the count** (29:16).
- A failed 2nd entry long in a strong downtrend off the trendline is a high-probability short (30:30).

## Video 2: "The ONLY Price Action Setup Traders NEED To Know" (youtube.com/watch?v=GrRbOItgLgc, 12:45) [01-09]

- 1:12 Pullback is the number one priority. First entries are not reliable (01).
- 1:40 High-probability setup = **second entry at a key entry point**. Key entry points: trendline, EMA 21, support/resistance key level (01).
- 2:05 The correction needs to play out: the two-legged pullback tells you where.
- 2:35 Always start the count at a new extreme. Bullish leg = series of bullish bars. Leg I then a break lower = 1ES; Leg II then a break lower = 2ES, "safe place to sell" (02, 03, 04).
- 3:40-4:25 Combine two key entry points (EMA + trendline) with the two-legged pullback (04).
- 5:00 If the first entry gets stopped, the second entry is the better one.
- 5:40 Uptrend: second entry long at trendline/EMA (05, 06).
- 8:45 NQ 5-minute example: "the goal is to predict a small market move for a scalp" (07).
- 10:00 1EL/2EL with the stop below the 2EL low (08).
- 11:40 The 4 conditions: correction played out, bullish signal bar to buy, bearish to sell, at a key entry point (09).

## Rules we can code (for the setup hunter)

1. **Structure:** trend up if price above EMA 21 and making higher highs; down if below and lower lows; range otherwise.
2. **Trendline rule:** after a close outside the trend channel, wait for a new extreme before trading the other way.
3. **Count legs from the last new extreme.** Entry only on the **2nd** entry (H2/L2), never the 1st. A micro double top/bottom resets the count.
4. **Key entry point:** the pullback must touch the EMA 21 or the trendline (both = best) or a breakout level.
5. **Signal bar:** bullish to buy, bearish to sell, real body, no doji.
6. **Entry:** stop order 1 tick beyond the signal bar. **Stop:** beyond the signal bar / pullback extreme.
7. **Skip:** congestion (overlapping bars), entries far from the EMA, the middle of a range, right after a new extreme (wait), counter-trend.
8. **Extra setups:** failed 2nd entry against the trend (F2), breakout pullback, lower high / higher low confirmation.
9. **Exit:** scalp at TP1, hold a runner while the trend extends, move the stop to breakeven.
