# Thomas Wade: rules extracted from the playlist "Trading Tutorial For Beginners" (transcripts, 2026-10-21)

## V01 "5 Price Action Rules EVERY Trader NEEDS To Know" (TegF3yYjnng, 31 min, 2.3M views)
Premise: markets reflect greed/fear so patterns repeat; indicators only show the past; read naked price. Institutions move the market; retail follows their footprints.
Rule 1 Trendline rule: after candles CLOSE outside a trendline/channel, expect a NEW EXTREME (new low in a downtrend) first; only after the new extreme, expect correction or reversal. A break alone is not a reversal. Triple touch = confirmed channel. Applies to short-term correction trendlines too. After the correction, the trend can resume (bigger pattern) or reverse. A double top close to the high counts as a new high.
Rule 2 No counter-trend trading: no tops/bottoms however good the signal bar; trade the other way only after a PROVEN trend in that direction; if no clean trendline, follow the overall bias; markets make counter-trend entries look attractive and with-trend pullbacks look sketchy; don't sell long traps inside an uptrend.
Rule 3 Trading range rule: ~90% of range breakouts fail at least temporarily; never trade the breakout, trade the breakout PULLBACK; in a range buy low, sell high, fade breakouts; congestion = micro range, stay out; watch for bullish/bearish imbalance inside the range to guess which breakout will work.
Rule 4 High-probability setup rule: markets move in pairs of twos (two-legged pullbacks). HP setups: (a) 2nd entry at a key entry point with the trend; (b) failed 2nd entry against the trend; (c) failed breakouts; (d) higher-low / lower-high confirmation setups. Setups mean nothing alone: they must sit at a KEY ENTRY POINT = trendline, support/resistance, or the 21 EMA (supportive tool only). Best = two key points together (trendline + EMA). Entries far from EMA/trendline = chasing = skip.
Rule 5 Signal bar rule: longs only above bullish bars, shorts only below bearish bars; the bar must confirm direction and momentum; stronger context = less perfect bar acceptable, but never a doji.
Range checklist: identify structure; locate support/resistance; locate short-term trendlines (trendline rule still applies inside ranges); careful in the middle; avoid congestion; fade breakouts; buy low sell high; don't chase after a miss, wait for the next HP setup; entries at the EMA; good signal bar; 2nd entries with the trend, failed 2nd entries against it.
Uptrend checklist: identify structure; no counter-trend; locate trendlines; draw short-term trendlines of the bearish corrections (to see them play out); avoid congestion; no entries at the top of the move (take pullbacks); longs only on 2nd entries off trendline/EMA (both preferred) and failed 2nd entries; no FOMO; after a new high buying is on hold until more confirmation; several new extremes in a row = probably a bigger channel (spike and channel); price failing to close below the EMA = still bullish; after the entry HOLD A RUNNER while the market extends, no new entries at the highs ("my scalp was already made").
Downtrend: mirror. Extra: a 2ES in a stacked/sideways area above the EMA = skip; prefer a lower-high confirmation after the 2ES, below the EMA; a micro double bottom resets the count; a failed 2nd entry long off the trendline in a strong downtrend is a high-probability short; signal bar must be decent even with good context (dojis rejected).
Quote: 'if I miss the second entry long I cannot go long all the way up here, my scalp was already made and I need to wait for a proper pullback'.

## V02 "The ONLY Price Action Setup Traders NEED To Know" (GrRbOItgLgc, 12:44, 2022) = TWO-LEGGED PULLBACK, EXACT COUNTING
Trend = correction phase, push phase, alternating. Job: identify when the correction is over = when the push resumes. Done "just by counting bars".
LEG DEFINITION (downtrend, looking to short): start the count at the LOWEST LOW of the move (a new low resets the count; a new high by even 1 tick resets the count in an uptrend).
- Bullish leg = series of bullish bars, or even ONE bullish bar. Bar colors inside a leg do not matter: a bearish bar inside a bullish leg does not end it as long as there is no break below.
- A leg STAYS IN PLAY until price trades ONE TICK BELOW THE LOW of the last bar of the leg (for a bullish leg) / one tick above the high (bearish leg). That break = the "trigger".
- First break below (after the lowest low) = FIRST ENTRY SHORT (1ES) = the first bullish leg is over; a bearish leg begins. If price makes a second bullish leg (new push up without taking the low) and then breaks 1 tick below a bar of that second leg = SECOND ENTRY SHORT (2ES) = the two-legged pullback is complete and the trend resumes. Mirror for longs (break 1 tick above the high of a bar in the 2nd bearish leg = 2nd entry long; "you need to go long above a bullish bar", so the signal bar is the bar whose high is broken and it must be bullish).
- First entries are ~50/50 ("sometimes they work sometimes they won't"); only second entries at key entry points produce consistency. A micro 2-legged pullback can form on 2-3 bars; a first entry can be stopped and a second entry trigger on the same bar.
- Inside bar in the second leg = a key entry point; trigger 1 tick above its high.
CONDITIONS for a 2nd entry to be high probability: (1) at a key entry point = trendline, 21 EMA, or S/R, best two together; (2) the correction has played out = the correction's own channel must make a NEW EXTREME (e.g. in a downtrend, the bullish correction must break the correction's prior high) before you count the 1st/2nd entry short; (3) signal bar bullish for longs, bearish for shorts.
STOP: one tick above the signal bar for a short / one tick below for a long; for an inside-bar entry, stop one tick below the entire swing.
TARGET: "designed to predict a small market move, a scalp", not a swing.
Opposite-direction 2nd entries in a bearish context = traps (failed 2nd entry long), used as short confirmation (lower-high confirmation).
