# JJ Simon: "Fair Pricing Theory" (Chart Fanatics podcast)

- **Source:** "STEAL The 1-Minute Strategy That Made Him $1.8M+ (Works EVERY Session)", Chart Fanatics, published Oct 4 2026, 1h42m. https://youtu.be/KHEQ5g55dQ4
- **Guest:** JJ Simon. He claims about $1.8M in prop-firm payouts over roughly 18 months, on about $400K spent on evaluations. Those figures are his claims and are not verified here.
- **Market and timeframe:** Nasdaq futures only (NQ and MNQ), 1-minute chart. The 5-minute chart is used only to read the bigger-picture bias.
- **How these notes were made:** watched the whole video, ads skipped. The notes are my own summary from the auto-captions plus the board frames listed below. They are not a transcript. Timestamps are video time.

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

Price has a "fair price". The market makes an unfair move away from it when a burst of volume arrives: a session open, or a scheduled news release. It then tends to come back. He trades two things:
1. A quick **continuation** of the opening move.
2. Repeated **reversions** back toward the fair price.

Both trades use fixed point targets and stops, chosen to suit the prop-firm rules rather than the chart. He says the entry signal matters less than the bias and the "optimal" risk per trade.

## 2. What counts as the fair price

| Situation | Fair price | When it applies |
|---|---|---|
| Normal day | The **session open price** (9:30 ET open for New York) | 3:00-5:00 |
| Red-folder news at 8:30 ET (CPI, PPI and similar), result close to forecast | The **price just before the release**. The spike is "unfair" because the news was priced in. | 12:30-14:00 |
| News day where the 9:30 open drops straight back to the pre-news price | Use the pre-news price and **ignore the open** | 62:00 |
| Unexpected news (tweets, speeches) | The fair price **moves**. The new one is the first consolidation after the jump. | 14:30-15:30 |
| A day where price consolidates elsewhere before the open | That **pre-open consolidation**. Used live on Aug 25. | 77:30 |
| After 11:00 ET | 9:30 is **no longer a target**, only a "big-picture reversion" bias | 41:00 |
| 2:00 PM session | If 2 PM opens away from 9:30, trade back toward the 9:30 price | 42:00 |

## 3. Sessions and time windows

- He trades the **first 90 minutes of each session**: New York 9:30-11:00 ET, New York afternoon from 2:00 PM, and Asia. He says it also works for London.
- 11:00 AM to 2:00 PM is a dead zone. He usually stops at 11:00 unless many accounts are still unfinished.
- The continuation trade is limited to the **first 5 minutes** after the open (live session, 87:00).

## 4. Big-picture bias filter

- Reversion of the previous 6-12 hours: take the opposite of whatever the market did overnight or that morning. It can be read on the 5-minute chart, or on 1 minute looking back 12 hours (3:50-6:00).
- The opening continuation trade must agree with this bias.

## 5. Trade 1: opening continuation (once per session)

- **Direction:** the colour of the **first 1-minute candle** of the session.
- **Trigger:** a candle closes beyond the structure of the previous candles in that direction, and the direction agrees with the big-picture bias.
- **Risk:** take profit **38 pts**, stop **25 pts**.
  - If the opening candle is longer than 25 pts, double both (76 / 50) and halve the size, so the dollars stay the same.
- **Where he takes it:** mostly on evaluations. He calls these continuation trades the weaker of the two.

## 6. Trades 2+: reversion toward the fair price (many per session)

All signals are on 1-minute candle **closes**. He watches the clock and enters as soon as the candle closes (56:00).

1. **Displacement candle.** All three conditions must hold:
   - Its body is larger than the previous candle's body.
   - It closes beyond the previous candle's wick: below its low for a short, above its high for a long.
   - The previous candle is the opposite colour. He says you can't displace a red candle with a red candle (69:30).
   - He calls this the weaker signal, used mainly on evaluations so he can trade many accounts.
2. **Break of structure.**
   - A swing point is a candle whose wick is lower than both neighbours (for a short), or higher than both (for a long).
   - The signal is a candle that **closes** beyond that swing point, in the direction of the fair price.
   - He calls this the stronger signal and reserves it for funded accounts.
3. **Live-trading extras.** A long wick that rejects the other side, or two candles in a row in the trade's direction, also count as confirmation (80:00-88:00).

A reversion trade is taken only when there are enough points left to the fair price for the chosen target. He wants at least 80% of the target distance available (79:00).

## 7. Exits and sizing

### Evaluations (Lucid / Apex style 50K: $3,000 target, $2,000 max loss)
- **Always fixed:** stop 25 pts, target 38 pts on 1 NQ, which is −$500 / +$750. The same on MNQ is 10 contracts.
- **His reasoning:**
  - The eval itself is a 1:1.5 bet (risk $2,000 to make $3,000), so he matches it per trade.
  - The 50% consistency rule punishes big winners, which is another reason to cap the target.
- **Variant:** with the stop at 50 pts and the target at 76, use 5 MNQ instead of 1 NQ (66:00).

### Funded accounts
- **The target comes first.** It is set per account from his simulation dashboard, for example $1,500, $3,210 or $6,000.
- **Contracts** = account target ÷ (points to fair price × $ per point).
  - 50 pts → 6 NQ for $6,000.
  - 100 pts → 3 NQ.
- **The stop is secondary.** Common sizes are 25, 37.5, 50 and 75 pts, and never less than 25 pts in New York.
- **Consistency rule:**
  - With a consistency rule: 1:1 to 1:1.5, sometimes 1:2.
  - Without one: let the trade run 1:3 to 1:6 toward the fair price.
  - Reasoning for consistency accounts: 1:4 trades need about 20R to pass a 20% consistency rule, while 1:1 trades need about 5R (43:00-45:00).
- **Breakeven:** sometimes moved to breakeven once price wicks the fair price, because past that point he thinks price is "random" (90:30).

### Drift trades after unexpected news
- No target, a very wide stop, and an exit when a 1-minute candle closes against the trend structure (16:30-18:30).

## 8. Discipline rules

- **3 losses in a row in a session:** stop trading reversions for that session. The market isn't reverting that day (34:30, 40:00).
- **When reversions keep losing,** move the fair price instead of trading against a strong trend (32:30).
- **Layering:** each new entry goes on a different account, rather than copy-trading one signal across several. Early entry on displacement on one account, a later break of structure on another (38:30).
- **Tilt:** if you feel tilted, trade cheap evaluations instead of a funded account close to a payout (35:30).

## 9. Prop-firm maths he uses

- **Backtest for pass rate, not for the equity curve.** He recommends simulating at least 50 evaluations (24:00, 26:30).
- **Cost of one funded account** = eval fee ÷ pass rate. Example: $100 ÷ 25% = $400.
- **Profitable if** the expected payouts per funded account are greater than that cost.
- **Risk of ruin:** P(pass) × P(payout) gives the chance an eval pays. Example: 30% × 30% ≈ 9%, so 91% pay nothing. With $1,000 (10 evals), the chance of losing it all is 0.91^10 ≈ 39%. He aims for under 5% (74:30-75:30).
- **Live accounts:**
  - He keeps the simulated funded balance just under the size of the live account the firm would give, because profits above it are lost when you move to live.
  - Once a live account qualifies for a bonus (for example $4.5K at Lucid or Tradeify), he stops trading it until the bonus arrives (46:30-51:30).

## 10. Worked examples shown on the chart

- **Tue Jul 14 (CPI).**
  - The pre-news price is the fair price.
  - A break-of-structure short gave about 167 pts toward it; he set a 175-pt target with a 25-pt stop.
  - Later entries layered in at 100-pt and 38-pt targets.
  - The 9:30 open fell straight back to the pre-news price, so he ignored the open.
  - Several 38/25 eval reversions followed until 11:00.
- **Wed Jul 15 (PPI).** Break-of-structure and displacement shorts back to the pre-news price, then 2 long losses near 11:00 (69:00-72:30).
- **Live, Tue Aug 25.**
  - Fair price was the pre-open consolidation.
  - Two funded shorts won $3,210 and $1,505, and an earlier continuation won $1,200.
  - Two long reversion attempts lost about $2K.
  - The day ended net positive.
- **Live, Fri (Aug 28).**
  - Continuation shorts for 25 pts on 2 contracts.
  - A long reversion for 50 pts.
  - A 6-NQ short for 50 pts on a $6,000-target account.
  - 4 of 5 trades won, about +$9K, then he stopped before a 10:00 AM speech.

## 12. Draft rules we could test (my reading; open points marked ?)

1. **Sessions:**
   - New York 9:30-11:00 ET.
   - New York afternoon 2:00-3:30 PM ET, aiming at the 9:30 price.
   - ? Asia: he doesn't say which open (6 PM Globex or 8 PM Tokyo) or which hours.
2. **Fair price:**
   - The 9:30 open.
   - On 8:30 red-folder days, the 8:29 close, if the 9:30 open trades back through it in the first minutes.
   - ? "Consolidation" needs an exact definition, for example the midpoint of the tightest 15-30 minute range.
3. **Continuation:**
   - Within 9:30-9:35, in the direction of the first 1-minute candle and against the prior 6-12 hours (? which window, and how to measure it).
   - Entry on a close beyond the previous candle's high or low.
   - Take profit 38 / stop 25, or 76 / 50 at half size when the first candle is longer than 25 pts.
4. **Reversion:**
   - Entry on a displacement or break of structure toward the fair price.
   - Only when the distance to fair price is at least 0.8 × the target.
   - Eval version: always 38 / 25.
   - Funded version: target = distance to fair price, rounded down to 25, 50, 75 or 100; stop 25-50.
5. **Session stop:** 3 reversion losses in a row. One position at a time when testing a single account (he spreads his across many accounts).
6. **Costs:** $2.37 per side and 1 tick of slippage, as in all our tests.

**Things to keep in mind when testing it:**
- His results come from running about 45 accounts in parallel and buying many evaluations. That spreads variance in a way one account can't.
- The 25 / 38 bracket is very tight next to MNQ's current daily range of about 440 points. When he recorded the examples (July), the daily range was smaller.
- In our earlier work, tight-stop opening trades failed on unseen data. This needs a fair test before drawing conclusions.
