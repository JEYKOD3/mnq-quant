No trade while price is inside the 9:30 range. A candle close outside arms that side; a close back inside just disarms (no day cancel). Enter on the retest with a bullish/bearish signal bar. Big range: also fade the edges from inside.

Prop firm rules

Max loss trails your best end-of-day balance and stops trailing at +$100. Consistency: no single day can be more than this % of your profit when you hit the target. Set 0 to turn off.

Exits (both strategies)

The strategy's Target becomes the runner target when TP1 is on (0 = runner holds until 15:55 or the trail). With 1 contract, TP1 cannot split, so it only triggers breakeven. Tested over 5 years (5m and 1m bars): the plain 2R target beats every TP1, breakeven, trailing, point-target and max-time combination for both strategies.

How to read this
One eval
You start on a given day and trade the strategy every day until you hit the profit target, hit the max loss, or run out of days.
Pass %
Out of every possible start day in the period, the share that reached the target within your days, without breaking a rule. 35% = 35 out of 100 evals passed.
Fail %
The share that hit the trailing max loss first. That eval is gone and you pay a reset.
Unfinished %
Neither happened in time. The account is still alive; on plans with no time limit you would keep trading it.
Random entries
The same number and size of trades, but with zero edge (pure luck). A strategy is only worth paying for if it clearly beats this.
Days to pass
For the evals that passed, how many trading days it took. The middle value is the typical case.
Evals per pass
How many evals you buy on average to get one funded account: (passes + fails) ÷ passes. Cost per pass uses the eval price for the first try and the reset price after.
Net, win rate, profit factor
Plain trading results over the whole period. Profit factor = money won ÷ money lost. Above 1 makes money; 1.2+ is solid after costs.
Does it hold up every year?
The same test split by year. A strategy you can trust beats random entries in most years, not just one.
