# Brooks, Trading Price Action Reversals (2012): Part I intro + Ch1-4
Machine-first reference. Each bullet is a rule/definition/number as stated. Format: condition -> expectation (prob if given). "Bars" = bars of the chart being traded (Brooks default 5-min Emini). MA = 20-bar EMA.

## Part I overview (Trend Reversals: A Trend Becoming an Opposite Trend)

### Definitions
- Trend reversal = change bull->bear or bear->bull. Not a TR->trend transition (that is a "breakout").
- Pullback = small TR + small trend against the larger trend; end of pullback = minor trend reversing back to major trend.
- Every trend bar is a breakout; every breakout is followed by an attempt to make it fail. Breakout stronger than reversal attempt -> reversal fails, becomes flag (breakout-pullback setup). Reversal attempt stronger than breakout -> breakout fails.
- Every trend sits in a channel bounded by trend line (TL) and trend channel line (TCL), even if not obvious.
- Always-in: if forced to hold a position at all times, the direction you would hold. Reversal = majority of traders believe always-in flipped; "almost always requires some kind of breakout".
- Reversal trade = trade where an always-in flip is likely. Countertrend scalp != reversal trade; usually bad trader's equation, forms inside channels.
- Bear trend definition used: series of lower highs and lower lows.
- Distribution (top TR) = bulls selling out / bears accumulating shorts. Accumulation (bottom TR) = bulls building longs / bears covering.

### Core probabilities and rules
- Most trend reversal attempts fail. Market has inertia. "There is really no such thing as a trend reversal pattern": all patterns in a trend are continuation patterns; a reversal is a failed continuation.
- TR in a trend -> breakout in trend direction ~80% of cases. Reversal patterns (double top, H&S, final flag, triangle) are TRs; fail as reversals >=80% of the time.
- Most trend reversal setups have <50% chance of success. Reasonable MTR setup: 40-50%; especially strong setup: 60%+.
- Strong setup -> >=60% chance of at least a two-legged move lasting >=10 bars; target usually >=2x risk.
- Most initial reversal entries: ~40-50% probability. Waiting for strong follow-through + clear always-in flip raises probability but lowers reward.
- Emini with 10-15 pt avg daily range: reasonable reversal (good context + decent signal bar) -> ~40% chance of a 4-pt swing (50-60% if very strong); chance of 2-pt stop hit before target or reversal exit -> ~30%. Worked example: 10 trades, 4 winners x 4 pts = 16 pts; 3 losses <=2 pts + 3 wins 1-3 pts ~ breakeven; net ~1.6 pt/trade.
- Countertrend scalps inside a channel using stop entries: lose on >=70%, average loser > average winner.
- Low 2 / low 3 short in strong bear trend -> hits stops below the prior higher low in ~80% of cases (hence L2/L3 shorts reliable in bear trends, H2/H3 longs reliable in bull trends). Trapped countertrend scalpers do not retry for at least a couple of bars.
- After a reversal, outcome usually a TR rather than opposite trend; TR vs new trend looks identical for dozens of bars. Even reversal into TR usually goes far enough for a swing trade. In TR, prefer scalps; in true reversal, swing most of position.
- Most tradable reversals require at least: a double top/bottom, micro double top/bottom, or final flag. Most traders will not take any reversal without one.

### Two requirements for a trend reversal (most technicians)
- 1 (absolute): countertrend move breaks a TL of the prior trend (breaks the channel).
- 2 (usual, not required): after the TL break, market returns and tests the old extreme. Rare exception: climactic reversal with protracted first move that never tests.
- Sequence: TL break -> move back toward old trend (test) -> if test fails to resume trend, test becomes breakout pullback in new trend -> at least second leg against old trend.
- Test may be lower low / double bottom / higher low (bear ending) or higher high / double top / lower high (bull ending). Higher high vs lower high tests occur about equally often.
- Bull trend ending via TL break (no TCL overshoot first): at least two legs down in ~2/3 of cases; other 1/3 reversal fails (trend resumes or TR forms). Higher high test -> expect two legs down; lower high test -> may be single leg (first leg already happened).
- Bull trend ending via TCL overshoot: breakout above channel rarely succeeds, usually fails within 1-5 bars.
- After the failed TCL breakout: minimum target = poke below the TL at channel bottom.
- After the failed TCL breakout: usually >=2-legged sideways-to-down correction; may lead to reversal or TR.
- First-leg pullback after the failed TCL breakout usually becomes a lower high.
- Second leg down extends to a measured move: leg1 = leg2, or height of bear spike, or height of a TR inside the bull channel.
- Bull trend can also end by TL break without TCL overshoot: sharp spike down or sideways drift into TR.
- "Single most important rule": never consider countertrend trades until there is a break beyond a significant TL. Also require a strong signal bar. After first countertrend surge, still look with-trend because market almost always tests the old extreme.
- Two failed attempts at the same level -> "usually tries the opposite".
- Only look for countertrend swing trades after the test of the old extreme, and only with a good setup on the reversal away from the extreme.
- Lower low test that reverses up within a few bars -> can be strong swing up. Lower low that extends "too far" below the old low -> assume new bear leg; wait for another TL break, momentum surge, and higher/lower low pullback.
- Steep/tight test with strong momentum -> trend resuming, not reversing. If a higher high goes >=5 bars above old high (Ch3) restart the process.

### Leg size / bar counts
- Traders want TL-break leg and test leg each > 2-3 bars; most prefer >=5 bars. If one leg is only ~2 bars, the other leg needs more bars. New trend rarely starts after 2-bar break + 2-bar test; if it does, expect a larger pullback within ~10 bars.
- One or two exceptionally large bars can be enough for a TL break to imply a second leg.
- After agitated reversal (failed L1/L2/wedge flag trap) following TL break + test: new trend usually lasts >=10 bars and retraces a good portion of the recent old trend.
- After a strong reversal the market turns "for at least a couple of legs and about 10 or more bars".
- Strong first leg of reversal: many bars, breaks well beyond MA, majority trend bars in new direction, closes beyond prior-trend swing points (e.g. below a prior higher low).
- If the setup is weak in a bull trend: wait ~5 bars for a second entry (lower high or higher high). If none and market sells 4-5 bars, breaks a pattern, and follows through next bar -> always-in flipped short; sell at market or pullbacks.

### Counting legs, H1/H2, L1/L2, flags
- Slow reversal from bear: first pullback = low 1 short setup; L1 should not be shorted unless strong bear spike in clear bear trend, so it likely fails. Then low 2; if trend believed reversed, also fails. Failed L2 = failed bear flag -> often strong upside breakout; that flag = final flag (even if it only broke down by ~1 tick). Sometimes one more push (wedge bear flag, L3) before the breakout.
- Aggressive bulls buy at/below the L1 and L2 signal bar lows with limit orders, expecting failure.
- Failed L1/L2 often becomes a strong outside up bar; if trapped out, enter at least a small position at market or on 1-2 tick pullback with a very wide stop (reduce size).
- Countertrend scalp plan in bear trend: hold long through L1 trigger if expecting second leg up; exit if L2 triggers; must exit on L3 (wedge bear flag) trigger; many reverse to short on L2/L3.
- H&S top in bull = triangle / triple bottom / wedge bull flag; neckline break usually fails and market breaks above right shoulder. Right shoulder = lower high = bear flag; break above it -> measured move up by height of right shoulder or of whole pattern. Mirror for H&S bottoms. Head-to-neckline leg = bull flag; right-shoulder rally = its breakout.
- Micro double top = failed H1/H2/triangle buy signal; micro double bottom = failed L1/L2/triangle sell. Both are small final-flag reversals.

### Trade mechanics in overview
- Theoretical ideal swing stop: beyond the start of the most recent spike (below most recent higher low in bull / above most recent lower high in bear).
- Bull trailing stops: after price exceeds first-leg high, stop to just below higher low; trail below each new higher low after each new higher high until market becomes two-sided (two-legged corrections). Then exit on strength (close of bull bar at/above/near trend high, or below prior bar low) and re-buy around the expected higher low.
- Fading a reversal bar in a trend (large bear bar closing on low in bull trend, esp. at MA): buy its close and try to buy below its low.
- If the next bar has a bull body: buy its close and above its high.
- Target 1 = high of the bear bar; target 2 = measured move up equal to bear bar height.
- Initial stop = about as many ticks as the bear bar is tall, or the usual stop (e.g. 2 pts Emini). Bear scalpers cover at the same spot.
- 1-min reversal trick: when a 1-min countertrend entry triggers, place with-trend stop entry where its protective stop would be -> "very high-probability with-trend scalp".
- Breakout pullback after a pullback's TL is broken = best "countertrend" trade (with-trend on higher TF); usually leads to test of major trend extreme. More momentum in the TL break -> higher probability.
- Spike phase: institutions enter during spike (at market, 1-2 tick pullback, stop above prior bar, breakout of swing high), add on pullback, size down to keep dollar risk constant.
- After TL break + strong move in bull: first pullback usually tests the bear low but may not get close; may instead test signal bar high / entry bar low / TL / swing point / TR / MA. First big pullback in strong trend usually tests the 15-min MA.
- Strong bull mindset: buy close of every bear trend bar (even large close-on-low), below prior bar low, below swing lows, at support; also buy above prior bar high, above bull bar highs, above resistance. Bear trend mirror: sell above and below bars and levels.
- Bull channel buying: limit below prior bars (institutions) or stop above H2 signal bars; marginally positive equation at channel top.
- Consecutive buy climaxes -> protracted correction with >=2 legs, >=10 bars (GS example: three consecutive buy climaxes; 9 bars up with higher H/L, 8 of 9 higher closes, little overlap = unsustainable). After 3rd buy climax: do not buy H1 or H2 (both failed).
- Measuring gap: breakout bar closing above prior bar high, next bar's low above that high -> gap; MM target = (leg start to gap middle) projected from gap middle.
- Daily chart: closes in final 30-60 min reflect higher-TF trend; selling into closes in bull = possible transition to TR/bear. Market leaders (AAPL) turn down several days before S&P tops; AAPL -3% day after +40% year = warning.
- Major reversals from bear markets often volatile: large bars, several pushes, one to several sell climaxes; end with large-range bars and a failed flag or 3-push pattern. Climactic reversals with big bars/volume more common at bottoms; tops more often from TR (double top, H&S) then bear spike breakout.
- Fig PI.5 numbers: average lower-low MTR long with no always-in flip on prior rally -> ~40% success, risk ~2 pts, reward 6-10 pts; shorting for 3rd push down in a spike-and-channel bear day -> 50-60%, risk ~2 pts, reward 4-6 pts. Weak spike -> traders scalp 2-4 pts all day. Bar 2 volume 98,000 Emini contracts noted.
- Bulls allow at most one bar against them, rarely two, when the buy signal was weak and the breakout had no follow-through.
- Buying at the bottom of a TR with a small signal bar: p >= 60% (GS example: risk 66 cents = signal bar 64 cents + 2 ticks, reward ~$4.00). After the strong entry/spike bar, move stop to 1 tick below its low (~breakeven).
- Great setups can produce small entry bars and several small bull-body bars before the market "realizes"; do not exit on smaller-TF stops when the 5-min signal is strong.

### Rules from Fig PI.3 (daily Emini) commentary
- Any move above a prior swing high is a sign of strength, even if that high was in the prior down move.
- Wedge reversal (3 pushes) usually leads to two legs down.
- Leg falling sharply below the last higher low of the bull trend = bear strength.
- Two-legged lower high test that forms a possible double top with the prior high -> expect second leg down testing the bear spike low; then bear channel or TR.
- Correction that breaks a major TL from the trend start -> market could reverse after a test of the high.
- Failed breakout above a double top = three-push top.
- Strong spike usually leads to a measured move; leg1 = leg2 common (leg 2 slightly larger than leg 1 in example).
- Once a lower high forms, draw TL and TCL for the new bear trend.
- Reversal up from a TCL breakout -> expect two legs up and a break above the bear TL.
- Any failed breakout that then fails to resume the trend becomes a breakout pullback in the new trend.
- Rally that breaks above the last small high of the bear trend, a small double top, and the bear TL = strength; failed test of that rally high (no higher high) -> another down leg likely.
- Sell climax (large bear bars) does not have to lead to a reversal; it means the market went too far too fast and needs to pause; expect 2 legs sideways-to-up.
- Second higher low after two legs up -> at least two more legs up likely; a leg covering too few bars and points does not convince traders the correction is over.
- Second failed attempt to break back into a prior TR -> market usually tries the opposite (breaks the other way).
- Double top bear flag after break of bull TL in possible bear resumption = strong short.
- Breakout pullback short entry: stop 1 tick below the pullback bar and below the prior swing low (wedge low).
- Expanding triangle (5 points) tops/bottoms noted repeatedly as reversal context.

## Chapter 1: Example of how to trade a reversal (CAT 5-min)
- Reversal trade expects a swing-size pullback or opposite trend; probability often <=50%. Constant risk + larger reward -> lower probability. Profitable because reward can be several times risk.
- Required order: strong break of TL -> reversal on test of trend extreme -> strong signal bar.
- Follow-through often a large fast spike of several trend bars; size down to keep dollar risk constant rather than skipping (risking 2-3x usual ticks is the norm).
- Every pullback begins with a minor reversal; waiting for pullback clarity usually means missing the day.
- Easy/clear setup -> usually a small fast scalp. Big moves must be unclear and hard to take.
- Friend's MTR method: 1-2 trades/day; no short without prior strong break below bull TL. Do not short a reversal bar at a new high if the prior pullback lasted 1 bar and did not reach MA. A 3-bar TL break that fails to reach MA = selling pressure but not strong enough.
- Major reversals often occur in the first hour or so.
- Second-entry short below bar 11 (two-legged test = some two-sided trading = bears showing strength; third push up on day). Wedge-shaped bull channel (3 pushes) -> expect 2 legs sideways-to-down.
- Swing trader keeps stop above signal bar (or above strong bear bar after it) and allows pullback as long as premise valid; shorts again below lower-high two-bar reversal (= breakout test of prior short).
- Always-in flipped short when five-bar bear spike completed; then tighten stop to just above start of the bear spike.
- Cover shorts when: two legs down complete, double bottom forms, and a 3rd-entry MA-gap-bar long sets up (high below MA, first pullback after strong rally).
- Rally lacking two consecutive strong bull trend bars -> bulls did not regain control; 3-leg rally = wedge bear flag -> at least one more leg down; outcome TR or reversal.
- Bar 19 setup = double top bear flag + wedge bull flag + two legs up in bear + bear reversal bar + lower high MTR. Bears' stops just above bar 13 (want double top or lower high; if neither, exit and wait).
- Trader's equation example: risk 10 cents (above bar 19 high), reward >=50 cents (test of bar 16 low), probability >=60% (bear trend or top of TR) -> 10 trades: +$3.00, -$0.40, avg +26 cents/trade.
- Fall below prior higher low after a lower high -> measured move down from the double top (height of TR). Breakout bar can be a measuring gap: top = prior swing low (bar 16), bottom = pullback high (bar 27), midpoint projects target.
- Trend trade management: take ~half off at first target only if a pause bar forms; no pause -> hold; take half at pause after sell climax bar following potential final flag (3 bars), hold rest to 1-2 min before close.
- Large bull trend bar in overdone bull (3rd consecutive buy climax, no pullback) -> expect two-legged pullback below MA; more likely exhaustion gap than measuring gap. Both bulls and bears sell: at its close, above its high, at close of next 1-2 bars (esp. if bear body), below prior bar low. Bear stop < height of climax bar in ticks. No re-buy / no profit-taking until two-legged correction completes.

## Chapter 2: Signs of strength in a reversal
Bull reversal (mirror for bear: swap high/low, bull/bear, above/below). Each is a scoreable boolean; more signs -> higher probability, swing more.
- Strong bull reversal bar: large bull body, small or no tails.
- Next 2-3 bars have bull bodies >= average size of recent bull and bear bodies.
- Spike grows to 5-10 bars without pulling back more than ~1 bar; reverses many bars, swing highs, and bear flags of the prior trend.
- >=1 spike bar with low at or 1 tick below prior bar close.
- >=1 spike bar with open above prior bar close (gap up open).
- >=1 spike bar closing on its high or 1 tick below.
- Context favors reversal: higher low or lower low test of bear low after a strong break above bear TL.
- First or second breakout bar closes above the highs of many prior bars.
- First pullback occurs only after >=3 bars.
- First pullback lasts only 1-2 bars and follows a bar that is not a strong bear reversal bar.
- First pullback does not hit breakeven stop (entry price).
- Spike breaks several resistance levels (MA, prior swing highs, TLs), each by many ticks.
- First reversal bar spends most of its time near its high; intrabar pullbacks < 1/4 of bar height.
- Sense of urgency (no pullback comes).
- Signal is a second attempt to reverse within the past few bars (second signal).
- Reversal began as reversal from TCL overshoot of old trend.
- Reverses a significant swing low (breaks below strong prior swing low then reverses up).
- H1 and H2 pullbacks have strong bull reversal signal bars.
- Trending anything: closes, highs, lows, or bodies.
- Pullbacks small and sideways.
- Prior breaks of earlier bear TLs (not the first sign of bull strength).
- Test of bear low lacks momentum: many overlapping bars, many bull trend bars.
- Test of bear low fails at MA or old bear TL.
- Breakout bar reverses many recent closes and highs (high and close above highs/closes of 5 to 20+ bars). Bars reversed by the close count more than bars reversed by the high only.
Other rules:
- No countertrend trades in a strong trend until TL break or at least climactic TCL-overshoot reversal. In a TR, trade reversals both ways.
- Stronger TL break -> reversal more likely to gain more points, last longer, have >=2 legs. Strongest breaks: strong momentum, surge well past MA, beyond prior-trend swing points.
- Required reversal strength scales with trend strength: very strong bull needs many signs; two-sided bull accepts weaker setup; rally to TR top may need no reversal bar; wedge bull flag reversing back up: can buy above a bear trend bar.
- Institutions ~95% profitable (market not zero-sum due to growth).

## Chapter 3: Major trend reversal (MTR)
### Definition
- Four requirements for MTR:
  - (1) A trend on the chart in front of you.
  - (2) Countertrend move strong enough to break the TL and usually (preferably convincingly) the MA.
  - (3) Test of the trend's extreme, then a second reversal: higher high / double top / lower high at a bull top; lower low / double bottom / higher low at a bear bottom.
  - (4) Second reversal goes far enough for consensus that the trend has reversed (e.g. strong spike of many consecutive large trend bars). Without a strong spike, consensus may take 50+ bars in a broad channel, often ending as a TR.
- MTR = two trends on screen with a reversal between. Always-in flips many times a day; an always-in flip alone is not "major".
- Virtually all MTRs begin with a TL break or TCL overshoot+reversal; all eventually break the TL.
- TL break alone does not reverse the trend; it is the first sign. Keep trading with-trend until the test. Take countertrend only if a reversal setup forms during the test; then expect >=2 legs.
- TL break not strong enough to reach the MA -> not strong enough to reverse; keep taking with-trend only.
- Bears fail to reverse bull trends 80% of the time (long-term institutions buy every TL break for this reason; they expect at least a test of the breakout point below the top).
- All tests are variations of double tops/bottoms: push 1 = original extreme, push 2 = test after TL break; exact-tick double top/bottom is rare. Overshoot (LL in new bull / HH in new bear) = final leg of old trend; undershoot (HL / LH) = pullback from first leg of new trend. Tests often two-legged.
- Higher low test = start of second leg (completed 2nd leg may end the move); lower low test = start of first leg -> expect pullback then second leg. Mirror: lower high = 2nd leg start; higher high = 1st leg start.
- Overshoot "too far and in too tight a channel" -> not reversing; restart (new TL break + new test). Specific: higher high going >=5 bars above old high -> restart.
- Momentum of the test matters: tight steep channel, no pullbacks, little overlap, far beyond old extreme -> trend resumes. Many overlapping bars, several large opposite trend bars, 2-3 clear pullbacks, wedge shape, slope less than original trend and less than the break -> lower high / slightly higher high, then another sell-off; at minimum a TR.
- If the test has too much momentum (steep channel toward extreme), wait for breakout of that channel and a breakout pullback before buying HL/LL reversal.
- Most tradable reversals begin with micro double bottom/top; a micro DB on 5-min is a full MTR on a smaller TF. Traders place limit orders at/beyond the signal bar of the expected final flag (H1/H2/triangle for tops; L1/L2/triangle for bottoms).
- V tops/bottoms without TCL overshoot+reversal are too rare to consider.
- ii pattern: often a reversal pattern but needs an earlier TL break; commonly final flag before 2-legged countertrend move; ii is "just wide enough to break a small TL" so any reversal within a few bars after is a valid entry.

### Probabilities
- Earliest MTR signals: low probability (<50%), largest reward. Waiting for clear always-in flip -> >=60%, smaller reward, larger stop; equation still positive.
- Average MTR setup: 40% profitable swing / 30% small profit / 30% small loss (stop hit) -> 70% chance of at least small profit. Best setups: >=60% swing, 30-40% stop hit.
- Average MTR leads to TR not opposite trend ~70% of the time.
- Swing profit defined as reward >= 2x risk.
- Reversal after new high: strong breakout (enough for profitable scalp before pullback) = new buying; sideways = profit taking (bulls buy lower); hard reversal = bears dominant -> expect down >=2 legs, >=10 bars.
- Pullback retracement: selling a 50% pullback in bear swing with stop above the high and target = spike bottom: reward = risk, probability >=60%. Pullback >50% -> probability ~50% -> need reward 2x risk -> short at ~67% retracement (61.8% not special; "no more likely than 60, 63, or 67").
- Bar after HL signal reversing highs and closes of many bars (closed above every close since bar 6) = strong reversal, more follow-through likely.
- 1-min reversals: smaller stop but lower probability, net losing strategy (none of six shorts in strong bull gave 4-tick profit). Use 1-min countertrend signals only to locate with-trend entries at their stops.
- On 3-min/1-min in strong 5-min trend: after >=5 consecutive bear bars, limit-short at high of prior bar expecting first reversal attempt to fail; or stop-short 1 tick below the bull signal bar / below the long entry bar (bulls tighten stops after entry bar closes).
- Spike bottoms in a bear trend usually get tested; spike tops in a bear trend need no test.

### Behavioral rules
- Strong bull trend: pullbacks small; bulls buy limit a few ticks down, at/below prior bar low, stop above prior bar high, breakout above swing highs, closes of bull and bear bars. Bears step aside.
- Trend maturing: larger bear bars, tails on tops = selling pressure; bulls stop buying above swing highs and take profits at new highs; TR forms; eventually 50-50; then bears dominant.
- Once pullbacks become relatively large, they act as TRs; new high = breakout attempt from TR, odds favor failure.
- Once bears push a pullback well below bull TL and MA -> they short above old high aggressively; bulls only buy deep pullbacks; new high = short opportunity (tipping point).
- Protracted trend's unusually strong breakout can be an exhaustive climax: near MM target or TCL, esp. 2nd or 3rd consecutive buy climax, strong traders step aside (vacuum) and 1-2 large bull bars form.
- Then bulls take profits and bears short: at bar close, above its high, next bar close (esp. if weaker), following bar close (esp. bear bodies), below prior bar low, at close and below low of a strong bear bar.
- Bulls will not buy again until >=10-bar two-legged correction, and only if the sell-off looks weak.
- Weak traders buy that bar (fear of missing / short covering) and lose; computers (no emotion) control the market.
- Breakout below swing low: strong breakout = new selling; uncertain = profit taking -> TR; strong reversal up = bulls waiting there -> big pullback in bull, not reversal.
- Pullback in strong bear: breakout above first leg's high is met by aggressive sellers; minor bull trend usually reverses after breaking first or second swing high.
- Strong TCL reversals from overextended trends: first pullback often shallow (emotional consensus).
- Strong TL break -> usually second leg after pullback; pullback often tests old TL.
- H&S top in bull: head-to-neckline leg usually breaks the whole bull TL, always breaks smaller TL under head rally. Weak head-to-neckline sell-off -> bulls and bears buy neckline test (triple bottom bull flag); break above right shoulder -> cover, MM up. Two right shoulders common.
- Fig 3.3 (AAPL daily): TL break then steep channel to higher high -> do not short first breakout of steep channel; wait for pullback to HH/LH. Flash-crash bar = breakout test; big up + big down = big confusion = TR; climax reversal (spike down then spike up) usually followed by TR.
- Fig 3.4 (GS 15-min): TL break then test in steep bear channel -> wait for breakout above channel + breakout pullback. Failed wedge -> MM down about wedge height. Test with 3 small legs and overlap = low momentum = likely HL MTR.
- Fig 3.8: context overrides pattern; double top after 6 bars up and strong sell climax may just go sideways. Bar with big lower tail after a high = first bar of micro double top; short below its low. Sell climax below prior low = 3-bar micro DB on 60-min; institutions scale in during the breakout bar expecting failure.
- 15-min MTR legs of ~10 bars on 15-min = 30 bars on 5-min; on 5-min traders call those separate trends, not MTR. Terminology is chart-relative.

## Chapter 4: Climactic reversals (spike followed by opposite spike)
### Definitions
- Climax = any unsustainable behavior; every trend bar is a climax; a trend bar or series of trend bars with relatively large ranges and little overlap. Ends at any interruption (pause bar or reversal bar).
- Most climaxes -> TR of >=1 bar, then trend often resumes. Climactic reversal = climax soon followed by sharp opposite move = failed breakout. V bottom = breakout then failed breakout.
- Climactic reversal is a two-bar reversal on some higher TF (final trend bar(s) + first opposite trend bar(s), even if separated by bars); a single reversal bar on an even higher TF. Spike up and down in one bar = big tail.
- All two-bar reversals are opposite climaxes (usually small).
- Parabola / V top / V bottom / climax share increasing (curved) slope = TCL overshoot + reversal; trade as such. Parabolic moves often 3 pushes (wedge top); third push = strong breakout running several bars to a level, then stall/sharp reversal. Higher high = two-legged rally -> reversal down = low 2 short.
- Exceptionally large bear bar in sell-off (largest body/range, ~2x other bear bars, opens near high closes near low): last longs capitulating; reversal possible within next couple bars if no follow-through; but if it breaks support -> MM down first.
- Vacuum effect: strong bears wait to short above TCL, strong bulls wait to take profits there -> absence of sellers races price up several bars; momentum programs buy until momentum slows; at the level both sell aggressively -> reversal down "at least for about 10 bars and a couple of legs".
- Crowded trade = fundamental traders' term for climactic move; they buy pullbacks only.

### Spikes and tests
- Spike up in bull then spike down: expect top of bull spike tested and exceeded; bottom of bear spike usually not tested (becomes bull flag / higher low). Mirror in bear.
- With-trend spikes (up in bull, down in bear) usually get tested and exceeded. Countertrend spikes (pullbacks) may or may not be tested; spike pullback need not be tested; spike reversal usually gets tested (traders distrust reversals).
- In bull market most spike tops get tested; in bear market most V bottoms get tested regardless of how strong the reversal looks.
- Countertrend spike = vacuum pullback; bottom is at some higher-TF support (TL, MA, TCL of bull flag); 5-min reversal alone is sufficient evidence of support.
- Reversal spike larger than trend spike -> higher reversal odds. After reversal spike: pause or TR (brief to dozens of bars) while both sides fight for channel direction; winner gets spike-and-channel trend; loser forced to cover.
- "Big up + big down = confusion = trading range", at least for a while (~10 sideways bars qualifies).
- Spike and channel: start of channel usually tested within a day or two.
- Climactic reversal in a trend usually after TCL breakout; reversal back into channel often tests opposite side (TL). Second TCL breakout that also fails -> reversal more reliable.
- Double top bear flag in TR after buy climax = added short reason; double bottom bull flag = buy setup. Mirror after sell climax.
- Reversal leg to the TR is often small vs prior trend but usually breaks the TL; can be first leg of opposite trend. First leg of reversal "almost always" breaks the TL; if not, reversal suspect -> TR/continuation more likely.
- Inside bar after breakout bar often followed by opposite trend bar -> climactic reversal setup; channel can go either way.
- Spike, pullback, channel that fails: the pullback was the opposite spike; then take every with-trend entry in new direction.
- Strong climactic reversals without significant test: only a few times a month on 5-min. Most "V" patterns are actually final flag reversals or micro double tops/bottoms.
- Small entry bar in a reversal within strong trend -> odds favor it becoming a with-trend flag.
- Prefer buying a higher low after a sell climax over the initial reversal; if a second entry fails -> old trend likely runs >=2 more legs.
- Climactic reversal short triggers after 2nd/3rd buy climax then price rallies above the high -> sometimes violent breakout up (MM up).
- Reversal down from buy climax usually from some double top (may only be visible on smaller TF); may form flag that breaks the wrong way first.
- TL break = possible reversal; TCL break = trend stronger/steeper. Most TCL breakouts fail within ~5 bars, re-enter channel, usually poke through the opposite side (TL). When TCL fails to hold, assume trend stronger; look with-trend but be ready to exit breakout pullback fast.
- TCL overshoot that reverses -> usually >=10-bar, two-legged correction; if it re-enters channel, usually pokes beyond TL, may become reversal.
- Steep TL with 1-2 bar false breakouts = reliable with-trend pattern; when it fails, many trapped -> sharp reverse move, possible reversal.
- After a TL break on strength: bear TL break -> test forms HL then >=2nd leg up, or LL then >=2-legged up move.

### Consecutive climaxes
- 2nd buy climax after only brief pause -> higher odds of significant correction/reversal. 3rd buy climax after another small pullback -> usually >=2-legged correction lasting >=10 bars. Pause = any bar that is not another big bull trend bar (doji, small bear bar); pause bar may poke slightly above 2nd climax high and still act as one-bar final flag. Third climax usually 1-2 large bars.
- Two consecutive buy climaxes = double top (even with much higher 2nd high); three = wedge top; trade the same.
- Pullback after a buy climax lasting >=10 bars and breaking the bottom of the bull channel works off the excess; a subsequent climax then needs less correction. Odds of correction fall if there is a significant correction after each climax. Series of breakouts + TRs = strong bulls buying near highs, not panic covering.
- Sell climax or pair of consecutive sell climaxes after market already fell >=10 bars -> good chance of strong reversal; rally >=10 bars, >=2 legs.
- Third sell climax usually most dramatic: large bear spike, overshoots TCLs, parabolic bend; channels often end with 3rd push; final climax often has the largest bear bar of the move; largest bear body common at end of consecutive sell climaxes.
- Bulls who exit on climax: look to re-buy after ~10 bars and two legs if setup; no buy setup after 10-20 bars -> trend likely reversed down.
- Bears after sell climax look to short again after ~10 bars if reasonable setup.
- Climactic reversals more reliable with added factors (opening reversal beyond yesterday's extreme, 3 TCL overshoots, second entry, one-bar final flag, MM completion, breakout pullback).
- "10-bar, two-legged" = correction longer and more complex than a small pullback; requires at least 10 bars and two legs.

### Entries / stops in climax context
- Short a buy climax bar: at its close, above its high, or at close of next 1-2 bars; stop ~ height of bull bar in ticks.
- If waiting for a bear reversal bar and shorting on a stop below its low: stop above signal bar high.
- Wider stop (allow one more minor high) -> reduce position size.
- Buy an exhaustive bear bar (e.g. 4-pt Emini bar after protracted move, after prior sell climax without correction): at its close, below its low, close of next bar (esp. smaller or bull body), close of following bar, above prior bar high.
- Strong bull bar after the exhaustive bear bar -> buy its close and above its high.
- Stop ~ bar height (4 pts in example); p(test of bar high before stop) >= 60%. Some risk less (~2 pts). Swing part into close.
- Bears will not short again until >=10-bar two-legged correction, and only if the rally looks weak.
- Small bar right after a large climax bar = selling abating = additional buy trigger (buy during/at its close).
- Micro double bottom / micro triple bottom (3-push down) after consecutive sell climaxes and a one-bar final flag = low-risk, high-probability long entry.
- 40-bar bull trend with little pullback then biggest bull bar of trend (small tails, close well above prior high): chance it becomes a steeper trend <30%; fade it expecting exhaustion gap and >=2-legged 10-bar correction to MA.
- Late acceleration (slope increase) = emotion = trend probably about to correct.
- Wedge reversal in bear trend: countertrend, wait for higher low after strong TL break; wedge pullback in bull: buy first signal. Scalping longs in strong bear = losing strategy. Climax without countertrend momentum -> you are wrong; trade with-trend.
- Wait for bars to close; a bull reversal bar collapsing into a bear bar in final seconds traps early longs; short below its low / 1 tick below TCL breakout low.
- Barbwire (dojis, large overlapping bars) at bottom: need second signal for long; prefer shorting small bar near high.
- Consecutive sell climaxes worked off by ~10 sideways bars each (trending TRs) prevent exhaustive climactic reversal.
- Drawing many TCLs = blindness; assume any channel lasts forever until it does not. In bear channel buy only below bars, short above prior bars.
- Steep bear channel: buying every TCL overshoot reversal loses; wait for strong breakout of channel (e.g. gap up) then buy breakout pullback / second attempt to reverse prior day's low.
- In a steep bear channel smart bears limit-short at/above the prior bar high, where eager bulls buy.
- Failed wedge (price below wedge low) -> runs about the height of the wedge as a minimum; breakout below wedge low is usually a strong bear bar (trapped longs' stops + bear entry stops there).
- TCL overshoot with no entry signal -> few trapped bulls, little reaction.
- No TCL overshoot + doji signal bar -> reversal suspect; need second entry.
- Opening gap far above a bear channel that is a 20-gap-bar / first MA gap bar short -> trend-from-open down.
- Dependable opening reversal: TCL overshoot + inside bar with good bull body + second attempt to reverse yesterday's low + final flag reversal + LL breakout pullback + largest bear body of sell-off.
- Volume: not reliable in Emini/SPY; 1-min volume divergence at lows (final low has less volume than earlier reversal attempt) is common but unnecessary; reversal bar volume lower than prior bear bars does not weaken the reversal.
- Volume exception 1: reversal up where >=1 bar has 10-20x the volume of most recent bars -> more follow-through buying.
- Volume exception 2: bull spike with 10-20x average volume and range >=5x most recent bars -> higher prices over many bars, usually at least a MM up; much less often falls below spike then MM down.
- Example: climax bar 114,000 contracts vs 5,000-10,000 on quiet bars; heavy volume at a bear low after protracted decline = buying (profit taking + new longs), not shorting.
- Retail <5% of volume at major turns. All institutional systems lose 30-70% of the time.
- Big tail on the bottom of a climax bar = buyers arriving; market usually falls only 1-2 more bars before correcting.
- Overnight 1-min: 3-pt bar is big on 1-min and qualifies as a spike. Bodies-only ii after reversal bar = indecision; false breakout of ii top -> continuation down.
- Two consecutive closes below MA fail -> trend still up (RIMM).
- After 2-day huge spike up then spike down = large buy climax -> TR (two-legged rally). Bears returning after ~10 bars for a setup (bar 20); bulls re-buy at HL MTR + triangle (bar 22) 7 bars later.

## Numeric candidates for the engine
- TL break qualifies only if countertrend leg closes beyond the 20-EMA; otherwise ignore for reversal logic.
- Reversal leg length: require >=5 bars for TL-break leg and >=5 bars for the test leg; allow one leg of 2-3 bars only if the other leg is >=5 bars or contains 1-2 bars with range >= 2x median range.
- MTR long valid if: prior bear trend, TL break leg closes above EMA, test leg forms LL/DB/HL within N bars, signal bar bull body with tails <= 25% of range; expected p(swing) ~0.40 avg, ~0.60 strong; target >= 2x risk; expect >=2 legs and >=10 bars.
- Strength score (Ch2 list): count booleans; thresholds to calibrate: swing full size when score high; examples: first pullback only after >=3 bars; first pullback <=2 bars; spike 5-10 bars with <=1 pullback bar; breakout bar close above highs of >=5 prior bars (count up to 20+).
- Test momentum filter: if test leg is tight channel (bar overlap low, no pullback) and goes >=5 bars beyond the old extreme -> cancel reversal, reset state machine.
- Test momentum filter 2: if test leg contains >=2-3 pullbacks, many overlapping bars, and slope < slope of original trend -> reversal probability up; minimum expectation = TR.
- Higher high test -> expect 2 legs down; lower high test -> may be 1 leg; after a TCL overshoot failure (fails within 1-5 bars) minimum target = poke through opposite TL.
- Two-thirds rule: TL break in bull without TCL overshoot -> >=2 legs down in ~67%; failure 33%.
- Low 2 / low 3 short in strong bear: ~80% reaches stops below the prior higher low. Use L2/L3 (H2/H3) as with-trend entries; forbid L1 shorts unless strong bear spike context.
- Countertrend scalps inside channels: assume win rate <=30%; block them.
- Climax bar detection: body/range largest of move, ~2x other trend bars; after >=10 bars of decline (or 40-bar trend without pullback): fade. Stop = bar height; p(test of bar extreme before stop) ~0.60. Steeper-trend success after such bar <30%.
- Consecutive climaxes: 2 climaxes separated by <=1-2 pause bars = double top/bottom; 3 = wedge; after 3rd expect >=10-bar, >=2-leg correction; do not take H1/H2 (L1/L2) continuation entries for ~10 bars after.
- Post-climax re-entry window: look for re-entry after ~10 bars and 2 legs; if no setup after 10-20 bars assume trend reversed.
- TCL breakout: expect failure within ~5 bars; on failure target = opposite TL; after reversal expect >=10 bars, 2 legs.
- Fade reversal bars in trend: large bear bar closing on low in bull (esp. at EMA): buy close; stop = bar height (or 2 pts ES); T1 = bar high; T2 = bar high + bar height.
- Measured move defaults: leg1 = leg2; TR height; measuring gap midpoint projection; failed wedge -> wedge height.
- Retracement rule for pullbacks in a swing: <=50% retrace -> p >= 0.60 with reward = risk; >50% -> p ~0.50, require reward >= 2x risk (enter near 67%).
- Entry/stop defaults: stop entry 1 tick beyond signal bar; protective stop 1 tick beyond signal bar opposite extreme (risk = signal bar height + 2 ticks); after strong entry/spike bar, move stop to 1 tick beyond that bar; swing stop beyond start of most recent spike; trail below each new higher low in a bull.
- Sizing: keep dollar risk constant; wide stop -> smaller size; swing at least part whenever >=2 legs expected.
- Profit-taking: scale half at first target only if a pause bar forms there; otherwise hold.
- Volume gate (optional): bar volume >= 10x recent average and range >= 5x recent -> bias follow-through in bar direction (MM); otherwise ignore volume.
- Trader's equation check: p * reward >= (1-p) * risk with p=0.40 default for initial MTR entries; require reward >= 2x risk.
- Time-of-day: major reversals frequent in first hour; last 30-60 min close direction = higher-TF bias input.
