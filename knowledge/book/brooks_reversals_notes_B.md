# Brooks, Trading Price Action Reversals (2012), Ch5-10: machine-first notes

- Scope: Ch5 Wedges and other three-push reversals, Ch6 Expanding triangles, Ch7 Final flags, Ch8 Double top/bottom pullbacks, Ch9 Failures, Ch10 Huge volume reversals on daily charts.
- Format: condition -> expectation. Probabilities are Brooks' stated numbers; "~" marks his approximate wording.
- Units: tick = Emini (ES) tick = 0.25 pt unless stated. "Scalp" in ES = 4 ticks (1 pt) when ADR ~10 pts. MA = 20-bar EMA on the 5-min chart.

## Ch5. Wedges and other three-push reversal patterns

### Definition and identification
- Wedge = a trend channel with three pushes; trend line and trend channel line often converge, but may be parallel (stairs) or divergent (expanding triangle). All three-push patterns trade the same way.
- Three-push family, treat identically: wedge, micro wedge, parabolic wedge, wedge with 4th push, wedge pullback (flag), wedge reversal, any triangle incl. expanding, triple top/bottom, double top/bottom pullback, head and shoulders, failed breakout beyond a double top/bottom.
- Mechanism: a third consecutive reversal is usually enough for reversal traders to take control from trend traders.
- Push = swing extreme. Trend extreme + two legs each reaching a greater extreme (two-legged HH / LL) = three pushes.
- Wedge top geometry: slope of trend line (lows) > slope of trend channel line (highs).
- First distinguishing sign vs parallel channel: the second pullback turns before reaching the parallel-channel trend line (urgency).
- Parabolic wedge: trend channel line across pushes 2-3 is steeper than the line across pushes 1-2 -> climactic -> if it reverses, expect ~2 legs and ~10 bars.
- Shrinking stairs: each breakout beyond the prior push is smaller (push 2 = 10 ticks above push 1, push 3 = only 7 ticks above push 2; Fig 5.2: 19 cents then 12 cents) -> waning momentum -> higher odds of a two-legged reversal.
- Opposite case: push 3 extends significantly beyond push 2 and reverses -> treat as trend channel line overshoot and reversal setup.
- Shape need not be perfect: lines may be drawn on bodies ignoring tails; push 3 may fall short of the trend channel line; still trade as a wedge if three pushes end a big move.
- Overshoot of the trend channel line on push 3 -> higher countertrend odds than a non-overshoot three-push.
- "The majority of three-push patterns reverse after overshooting a trend channel line."
- Count reset: if push 2 is unusually strong, many traders reset the count (push 2 becomes push 1) -> expect a 4th push before reversal. Stronger push-2 momentum -> higher probability of a 4th push.
- Micro wedge: 3 consecutive bars (or 3 of 4-5) each with a slightly further extreme and tails; micro trend channel line across the tails.
  - Expect only a minor correction.
  - In a strong trend, do not fade (buying above bar 3's high in a strong bear likely loses).
  - On a trading range day near the range extreme with a reversal bar on bar 3 -> reasonable scalp.
- Wedge flags (pullbacks): usually 10-20 bars; with-trend; take the first signal; often do not look like a wedge. Wedge bull flag points down.
- Wedge reversals: usually need >= 20 bars and a clear trend channel line to reverse a trend. Wedge top in a bull trend points up.
- Wedge in a trading range -> behaves like a wedge pullback (no trend to reverse) -> take the first signal.
- First-hour trend day: the multi-hour midday range often has three pushes without a wedge shape (e.g. low 3 in a slightly up-sloping bear channel) -> setup for trend resumption into the close.
- Spike-and-channel trend: the channel commonly has three pushes with a wedge shape.
- Channel with large swings (much two-sided trading) is not a strong trend -> aggressive first-entry wedge reversal is acceptable (Fig 5.6).

### Entry, stop, target
- Entry, wedge top: sell stop 1 tick below the bear reversal/signal bar at push 3. Wedge bottom: buy stop 1 tick above the bull signal bar.
- Countertrend default: wait for a second signal.
  - After a wedge top: a lower high (less often a higher-high breakout pullback short).
  - After a wedge bottom: a higher low (less often a lower low).
  - Take the first signal only if the pattern is exceptionally strong.
- If the initial breakout from the wedge is strong, entering on the breakout pullback has higher success.
- If the breakout goes straight for several bars, trade it as a breakout (enter on bar closes / at market when the spike is very strong).
- Target 1 (wedge top): test of the bottom of the wedge (= bottom of the pullback from push 1).
- Target 2: measured move down = wedge height (bottom to top) projected from the wedge bottom.
- Expectation after a valid reversal: at least two legs, ~10 bars.
- Three consecutive climaxes (three strong 1-3 bar spikes with small pullbacks) -> "60 percent or better" odds of a complex correction (e.g. two legs) lasting ~10+ bars; take the reversal only with a good signal bar, reasonable shape, adequate countertrend pressure, and not a tight channel.
- Stop management: after the first strong leg down and the subsequent lower high (shorts), move the stop to breakeven.
- Emini: after a wedge top and brief selloff, the pullback often tests the wedge top to the exact tick (perfect double top) -> second-entry short.
- SPY/stocks: pullback may exceed the wedge top by a few ticks without being a failure.
- Wedge top in a strong bull trend often corrects in two sideways legs to the MA -> high 2 buy (reliable). A wedge that corrects sideways = very strong trend. Mirror for bottoms.

### Failure rules
- Wedge fails if: entry triggers, breakout does not go far, trend resumes and price goes beyond the wedge extreme.
- Failed wedge -> often runs quickly to a measured move = wedge height projected beyond the extreme.
- A wedge is a trading range -> breakout mode -> either side's breakout targets ~1 wedge height.
- When a clear wedge fails, many traders reverse on a stop beyond the wedge extreme.
- Failed-wedge breakout is still a breakout: strong -> measured move; weak -> fails and reverses back.
- Second reversal at the new extreme = higher-high (or lower-low) breakout pullback of the original wedge break -> the new trend is usually protracted (>= 10 bars) with >= 2 legs.
- Pullback that races beyond the wedge by many ticks -> aggressive buying; no longer a top; buy quickly.
- Most failed wedge tops are steep and tight in strong bull trends. In strong trends do not look for reversals; buy the pullback from each new high.
- Wedges fail most when there is no prior trend line break / countertrend strength and the pattern is small. Three pushes alone (especially small) rarely reverse a trend.
- Tight channel with wedge shape -> do not enter the reversal; wait for the breakout and judge it.
  - Weak breakout -> enter with the channel: short the low 1 / low 2 created by the failed upside breakout.
- Tight bear channel: never buy the first breakout attempt; most fail before a scalp. Wait for at least a second entry. Need a trend line break and preferably a reversal bar.
- Overlapping bars at push 3 (e.g. 4 consecutive mostly overlapping bars) = acceptance of price, not rejection -> reversal unlikely; wait for a higher low.
- Steep bear channel wedge bottom -> wait for a higher low before going long.
- Exception: steep, tight wedges can be reliable opening reversals in the first hour or two; never fade such a wedge later in the day.
- A wedge usually makes two attempts to rally; short the wedge only if it fails (below the push-3 low) or if the second rally attempt fails.
- Three one-tick failures in a row is very rare -> the next move is likely to run (Fig 5.11: breakdown on 14,000 contracts on the 1-min chart).
- A wedge bottom in a tight bear channel without prior bull strength is not a buy setup; at most a scalp (Fig 5.10).

### Related three-push breakout setups and misc numbers
- Successive one-tick breakouts: in a range, a 1-tick breakout beyond a minor swing that reverses for ~3-20 bars, then another 1-tick (sometimes 2-tick) breakout that pulls back, then a third break beyond the second -> usually a significant breakout; target ~= pattern height (range top to bottom). Sometimes breaks out after only one 1-tick failure.
- Failed double top breakout: a breakout above a double top that reverses within a few bars = wedge top (the two tops + the breakout = three pushes). Usually falls enough for at least a scalp; sometimes breaks the opposite side for a measured move = range height.
- Trading-range fade orders: short on a stop 1 tick below the failure bar near the top; buy on a stop 1 tick above the failed-breakout bar near the bottom.
- Channel re-entry: breakout above a bull channel then a strong reversal back inside -> ~50 percent chance the bear swing breaks below the bottom of the channel.
- A breakout that is going to fail usually does so within ~5 bars of breaking the channel.
- After a failed low 2 with strong momentum, a second push up occurs in over 60 percent of cases.
- First-entry reversal short with reward >= 2x risk: ~40 percent success (positive equation). Entering on the breakout bar / closes: >= 60 percent with reward >= risk.
- Climactic reversal: ~60 percent chance of at least two legs and 10 bars.
- Fig 5.8: rally reached 14 ticks above the signal bar high with a bear close -> bulls took profits at exactly 3 pts; it stopped 2 ticks below the prior low 2 entry = missed breakeven stops by 1 tick = bears defending.
- First attempt to reverse a spike (micro channel) usually fails. V bottoms are rare; after a spike at least one more push down is highly probable.
- Most measured move targets do not cause reversals, but all reversals happen at some support/resistance level.
- Every trend bar is a breakout; most breakout attempts fail; do not be frightened by a single big trend bar.
- Breakout above the second high in a weakening trend is often sharp (short covering) but reverses quickly if there is no immediate follow-through; a strong breakout well beyond the second high -> at least two more legs up.

## Ch6. Expanding triangles

- Definition: at least 5 swings (sometimes 7, rarely 9), each extreme greater than the prior one (higher highs and lower lows). It is a trading range; most breakout attempts fail.
- Bottom (reversal in a bear trend): 3 pushes down, both pullbacks form higher highs; third low = lower low after a rally above the prior swing high -> also a breakout pullback and a lower-low major trend reversal.
- The rally from push 2 always breaks the bear trend line that contained push 2; all expanding triangles are major-trend-reversal variants.
- Entry: buy stop 1 tick above the bar at the 5th-leg lower low (Fig 6.3). Mirror for tops.
- Target 1: breakout beyond the opposite side of the triangle (top: below the pattern; bottom: a new swing high). "In most cases, that is as far as the trade goes."
- Target 2 (if the target-1 breakout succeeds): measured move ~= height of the last leg of the triangle.
- If the target-1 breakout fails and reverses -> the triangle becomes a continuation pattern (expanding triangle flag) with 7 legs; target = new extreme in the original trend; if that succeeds, measured move ~= last leg. Eventually one breakout succeeds, or it evolves into a larger range.
- After completion it usually sets up an expanding triangle in the opposite direction (reversal then continuation, or vice versa).
- After the 5th leg completes, swing part of the position; each leg is also a scalp (new swing high/low in a range).
- Shape need not be perfect; extremes need not touch the lines. Bad proportionality (one leg far wider than the others) weakens the pattern.
- Second-chance entry: if many bars pass between leg 5 and the next lower low, the triangle loses influence; treat it as a double bottom reversal.
- Trend channel line failed breakout combined with an expanding triangle -> usually a two-legged move down.
- Dojis mid-leg mark equilibrium; the balance point is often the midpoint of the move (rough target).
- In a possible new bull trend (strong momentum) wait for a lower high before shorting the triangle top; breakout-failure shorts without a prior trend line break are unwise.
- Emini gives fewer false 1-tick signals than SPY (1 ES tick ~= 2.5 SPY ticks).

## Ch7. Final flags

### Definition and characteristics
- Final flag = the last pullback (flag) before a trend reverses; every reversal follows a flag.
- Final-flag likelihood rises when:
  - the trend has run dozens of bars;
  - the flag is mostly horizontal with strong two-sided trading: several opposite trend bars, prominent tails, several reversals, bars overlapping the prior bar >= 50 percent;
  - the flag is a tight trading range after a trend (magnetic pull);
  - the flag follows a climax (consecutive climaxes).
- Size: any, including a single bar; usually at least 5-10 bars.
- One- or two-bar final flag: a pause bar after a strong spike of several unusually large trend bars (potential exhaustion climax).
  - Breakout from it often reverses after 1-2 bars -> two-legged pullback lasting >= 10 bars.
  - Tradable countertrend, not a reliable trend reversal: the trend extreme is frequently tested within 10-20 bars.
- Micro final flag: a bar with a tail or opposite body inside a strong spike -> expect one more small push (enough for a scalp), then a pullback of 5-10 bars; then usually at least one more push in the trend direction, possibly a channel to a measured move = spike height.
- Micro trend line breakout pullback = a 1-2 bar final flag in the micro trend.
- Two consecutive horizontal flags, the second smaller -> breakout from the second can lead to a wedge reversal.
- Final flags often reverse briefly then grow into a larger flag; a scalper's profit is usually available first.
- Trend length rule: original trend 50-100 bars -> the reversal more likely evolves into a large flag/range than an opposite trend.
- Trend reversal more likely if a strong countertrend leg in or before the flag broke well beyond the trend line.
- Expectation from a final flag reversal: at least a two-legged sideways-to-opposite correction, often a trend reversal; both are traded the same (profits after leg 1 or 2, add on pullbacks).
- Consecutive climaxes -> reversal of ~10 bars and ~2 legs; at a major support/resistance level can lead to strong reversals.
- The push before the flag + the flag breakout = a double top variant (HH or LH; treat the same).
- After a sell climax a horizontal bear flag may have low 1 / low 2 setups with no follow-through, then break upward = common reversal form.
- Some of the best reversals: the first leg from the reversal extends beyond the final flag, then a sideways flag above the old flag, then the new trend continues -> swing part of the position.

### Three resolutions of a final bull flag
- (1) Breakout above the old trend high, then reversal down = higher high reversal.
- (2) Weaker breakout reverses below the prior high = lower high reversal.
- (3) Little or no upside breakout: a high 2 / high 3 triggers but the entry bar reverses after 1-2 ticks, or the flag breaks down without exceeding the prior bar's high.
  - Downside breakout is usually a spike then a bear channel to a measured move.
  - If no clear breakout and the flag keeps growing into a bear channel, traders eventually accept a bear trend; there is usually at least a one-bar bear spike at its start.
- Mirror for bear flags, incl. failed low 2 / low 3 then a large bull trend bar = always-in flip to long.
- Measured move after a bull flag breaks down with no bull breakout: ~height of the flag (Fig 7.6).
- Breakout confirmation: a bull breakout of a bear flag needs a bull trend bar closing at least several ticks above the flag top; a bear breakout of a bull flag needs a bear trend bar closing several ticks below the flag bottom; otherwise the pullback premise survives.
- Two-legged move out of a flag often sets up a major reversal with at least two legs; a two-legged move in a tight channel usually becomes only the first of two larger legs.
- Entry trigger for the final flag reversal: 1 tick beyond the reversal bar after the flag breakout; price is then drawn back into the flag (magnet).

### Fading flags after a reversal (limit orders)
- After a reversal pattern (e.g. wedge bottom on a trading range day), expect the low 1 and low 2 shorts from the first bear flag to fail.
  - Buy with limit orders at the low or 1-3 ticks below the low of the low 1 / low 2 signal bar; risk ~4 ticks in the Emini.
  - Expect at least a two-legged rally lasting 10+ bars.
- A low 1 short is reliable only in the spike phase of a strong bear trend, never after a reversal pattern.
- Mirror after a top: bears short with limit orders at or just above the highs of high 1 / high 2 signal bars; the high 2 still triggers (micro buy vacuum 1-2 ticks above the signal bar) then turns down.
- After a strong 4-bar spike with an unusually large 4th bar: expect a 1-few bar pullback (high 1 / high 2) with strong buying at and below the prior bar's low; the resumption bar often becomes another climax -> then expect a >= 2-leg, ~10-bar pullback.

### Failed final flag
- Failed final flag = the reversal triggers but fails within a couple of bars -> breakout pullback setup in the original trend direction (e.g. the short entry bar becomes a strong bull reversal bar -> buy above it).
- If the resumption runs only 1-2 bars and reverses again -> second entry for the original reversal. If that also fails -> channel mode; channels last many bars.
- A flag only ~4 bars long after strong momentum that does not break a significant trend line -> reversal likely fails; expect a sideways correction then a new high (Fig 7.3).
- "When the market tries to do something twice and fails both times, it usually trends in the opposite direction."
- Opening context: 1-2 bar final flags can lead to big reversals on the open; small final flags set up opening reversals.
- Volume (daily / event bars only): a bar with ~10x the average quiet-bar volume (93,000; 105,000 contracts) supports a spike-and-climax reversal.
- Buying the close of a climax bar: risk ~bar height (or 2-3 pts); targets = bar high, MA test, measured move = bar height, new high of day.
- Major trend reversals are more often followed by trading ranges than by opposite trends.
- Monthly Dow (Fig 7.11): 1-2 bar final flags after climaxes led to reversals (4 cases), pullbacks (4), ordinary flags (3), failed final flags (2); expected reversal duration ~10 bars with >= 2 legs.

## Ch8. Double top and bottom pullbacks

- Double bottom pullback (long, reversal pattern): sell-off -> double bottom -> rally -> higher-low pullback testing just above the double bottom low -> buy.
- Second bottom is often slightly below the first; this is preferable (also a head and shoulders bottom).
- If the second bottom does not at least reach the first -> risk of only a two-legged sideways-to-up correction -> scalp, not swing. Fig 8.3: second low 5 cents above the first in a strong bear trend "usually negates the pattern".
- Equivalences: three-push bottom where push 3 lacked sellers for a new low; breakout pullback (the rally from bottom 2 is a breakout, even if only 2 bars); two failed attempts to exceed the extreme (often a low 2) -> two failed attempts usually lead to a reversal.
- Where push 3 ends does not matter: above the double bottom (double bottom pullback / triangle), at it (triple bottom), or below it and reversing (failed breakout = final flag reversal). All are three-push bottoms; look for longs.
- Reliability: good shape + strong bull reversal bars at all three bottoms (especially the last) -> "one of the most reliable buy setups".
- Entry: buy stop 1 tick above the signal bar at the pullback low (Fig 8.1: outside up bar going 1 tick above the prior bar's high; also at the close of / above the high of the following bull trend bar).
- An inside bar after a small double bottom counts as the pullback and completes the setup.
- Double top pullback (short): double top in a bull trend -> selloff -> pullback that reaches close to the high -> sell stop 1 tick below the low of the pullback-high bar (Fig 8.4 bar 9). Any three-push top shape qualifies if the signal bar is strong and selling pressure exists.
- Double top bear flag in a bear trend is always a low 2 short (two failed reversal attempts; bulls step aside for a couple of bars -> fast move down).
- Context filter: in a strong bear trend without a prior strong trend line break, bottom picking is poor; the first pullback almost always sets up a with-trend entry; tight bear channels last longer than seems reasonable.
- Any reversal without a prior rally more likely yields a trading range -> longs are scalps only.
- Exception: first ~90 minutes (opening reversals) with big tails on the bottoms can justify the long.
- Weak double bottom -> expect it to fail -> short the low 2 at the MA instead.
- Both a double top and a double bottom in the same range -> usually a move back to the middle forming a triangle -> breakout mode; the range usually breaks out in the direction of the prior trend.
- Range fade with a good setup: ~60 percent success for reward >= risk.
- Strong breakout of a tight range: probability of reward >= risk often 70 percent or more -> trade with it; if it fails, trade the opposite direction.
- Protective stops below an entry bar are often tested; two moves down to the same level = double bottom even if the first is not a swing low.
- Fig 8.4: double top pullback short at bar 9 fell only 5 ticks -> reverse to long on a stop above the two-bar reversal at the MA.

## Ch9. Failures

### Definitions and base rates
- Failure = any trade that does not reach its goal (smaller profit or, more commonly, a loss). Falling short of a larger expected move is still a failure even if a scalp was available.
- Common good setups succeed ~60 percent -> fail ~40 percent.
- A failed bull setup = bull weakness / bear strength; a failed bear setup the reverse.
- A failure usually sets up a trade in the opposite direction (trapped traders exit and will not re-enter soon -> one-sided market); sometimes a second signal in the original direction.
- Failures that set up opposite-direction trades:
  - failed high 1 / high 2 / low 1 / low 2;
  - failed breakout of anything (swing high/low, trend line, trend channel line);
  - failure to break through a magnet (measured move target, prior swing, trend line, trend channel line);
  - failed final flag (-> breakout pullback);
  - failed wedge;
  - failed signal bar or entry bar;
  - failed one-tick breakout;
  - failed reversal (failed double top/bottom, failed head and shoulders);
  - failure to reach a profit target: 5-, 9-, 17-tick failures in Eminis; 11, 51, 99, 101 cent failures in stocks.
- Failures that signal a strong trend (with-trend setups):
  - failure to fall below the prior bar's low in a strong bull trend (or to rally above the prior bar's high in a strong bear trend);
  - failure of a pullback to reach the MA;
  - failure to reach a magnet -> often a pullback then another attempt at the magnet.
- Single most reliable failure: a flag in a trend (countertrend reversal setup goes sideways to the MA and sets up a high 2 with a bull signal bar -> expect at least a test of the trend extreme).
- Any reversal pattern, including major ones, can fail and resume the trend. Most reversal attempts look great and most fail.

### Stop runs and trapped traders
- Traders place protective stops 1 tick beyond the signal bar; after a strong entry bar closes, 1 tick beyond the entry bar.
- When a bar reverses beyond those levels, the move is usually a strong trend bar and usually enough for a scalp.
- Rule: place your entry stop exactly where trapped traders' protective stops are (1 tick beyond the entry or signal bar) -> expect at least a scalp and usually a two-legged move.
- If a trade already made a scalper's profit and then takes out the original stops, there is far less fuel (traders already exited; remaining stops are at breakeven).
- Corollary: if the extreme of a signal/entry bar is tested but not exceeded (stops missed by 1 tick), the trade is often profitable; the test forms a double bottom bull flag (signal/entry bar low + test bar).
- Breakout test: price returns to the original entry price; may miss the breakeven stop by 1 tick or exceed it by a couple of ticks; if it reverses back in the trend direction -> reliable with-trend setup (e.g. a bar turns up 2 ticks above the signal bar high = missed breakeven stops by 1 tick -> buy above that bar).
- Huge trend bar with tiny/no tail: stops sit 1 tick beyond it; the market commonly works against it over the next 5-10 bars, runs those stops, then reverses back in the trend bar's direction.

### One-tick failed breakouts
- A 1-tick failed breakout (1-2 ticks in Emini; 1-10 cents in stocks; 5-10 ticks in a $200 stock) is a reliable sign the market is going the other way -> look for opposite-direction setups.
- Mechanism: breakout traders enter on stops 1 tick beyond the prior bar; the other side fades at that tick with more volume; price drops 1-2 ticks, then 3-5 ticks as trapped traders exit; stopped-out traders stand aside -> one-sided market.
- Two-bar reversal that completely overlaps the prior two bars with the entry at the top of a small range = usual bull trap.
- A 5-min 1-tick failure in a range is often also a 1-tick failure on the 15/60-min chart while that bar forms, but the higher time frame bar usually extends beyond 1 tick before it closes (the 5-min pulls back many bars then breaks out again).
- Most breakout attempts fail, especially in a trading range; 1- and 2-tick false breakouts are common in the 5-min Emini.
- Trading range day: expect breakouts to fail; fade them, preferably second entries; breakouts with prominent tails and overlapping bars are weak and likely to fail.
- Strong trend: buy on a stop above the range top instead (expect another leg).
- Most reliable 1-tick failure: a failed high 2 in what is mistaken for a bull pullback after the bull trend is over (bear channel after a two-bar reversal from a higher high, 5 bear bars down, no trend line break).
- A high 2 alone is not a buy signal; it is a buy at the top of a bull trend or at the bottom of a trading range, not near the top of a range.
- Failed-breakout short procedure (trading-range day, modest momentum): price above a prior swing high -> sell stop 1 tick below the low of the prior bar; if unfilled at the bar close, move to 1 tick below the bar that just closed; repeat until momentum is too strong; then wait for a pullback that breaks at least a minor trend line and short the next new swing high.
- Mirror for lower-low buys: buy stop 1 tick above the prior bar's high; keep lowering; restart if the drop is too far/fast (wait for a small rally breaking a minor trend line).
- On trend days fade new extremes only after a minor trend line break and with a strong reversal bar.
- Outside bar entry: traditional entry is a stop 1 tick beyond either extreme, but an outside bar is a one-bar trading range and most such entries fail; rarely use it (risk to the far side is large).
- Two-bar reversal containing a large bar: safest entry is beyond the extreme of the larger bar (price often exceeds the small bar but not both).
- Barbwire (3+ sideways bars, at least one doji) -> too much uncertainty; wait.
- Trapped shorts off a doji entry bar exit 1 tick above the entry bar; buying there -> good for a scalp and likely two legs up.

### Profit-target failures (tick rules)
- 4-tick scalp requires a 6-tick move beyond the signal bar (1 tick stop entry + 4 ticks profit + usually 1 tick to fill the limit).
- Five-tick failure: a move of exactly 5 ticks that reverses -> trend traders have lost control; pullback or reversal imminent. Common on 1- and 3-min charts too. After a run of successful scalps, a five-tick failure -> shorts exit at breakeven and above the reversal bar (Fig 9.13).
- Nine-tick failure: 2-pt target = limit 9 ticks above the signal bar high; needs 10 ticks to fill. If it hits 9 and reverses: rely on the initial stop or a trailing stop 1 tick below the most recent bar with a decent bull body; never let it become a loss (risking 8+ ticks to make 1). Lower the limit a few ticks if it sits at obvious resistance.
- 13-tick failure = failed 3-pt target (Fig 9.4 bar 9).
- 17-tick failure: 4-pt target = 17 ticks above the signal bar high; needs 18 to fill. If price hits 17 and the next bar trades below that bar -> at least a downward scalp; longs lower targets a couple of ticks and trail stops to 1 tick below the prior bar's low; exit at market on a weak or bear close.
- 16-tick rule: once 16 ticks above the signal bar high, ~80 percent chance of reaching 17 or 18 -> do not exit at 16 unless price trades below the prior bar's low (especially if that bar is a short signal).
- 21-tick failure = failed 5-pt target (needs 22 to fill) (Fig 9.4).
- Reversal 1 tick shy of common targets (2, 3, 4 pts) = weakness.
- SPY/QQQ: common target 10 ticks, usually needs a 12-tick move beyond the signal bar; a move reaching only 8-11 ticks then reversing usually yields at least a profitable scalp the other way (trapped scalpers exit on reversal of the signal/entry bar).
- Stocks: a usually-reliable $1.00 scalp that falls short twice (93 cents, then a low 2) -> good scalp opposite; reduce target to ~50 cents in a sideways market (61 cent drop followed).
- Unfilled limit at a target followed by a few-tick pullback -> many exit at market, fueling the correction; often a sign of an attempted reversal.
- Swing alternative: traders not exiting at 4 pts rely on a breakeven stop, then trail to 1 tick below the most recent higher low once a new high prints.
- A failure that is good for only a scalp can still be part of a major reversal.

### Trend lines, spikes, context
- First spike through a trend line in a strong trend (e.g. -8 percent earnings day to the daily MA) = "sale": buy the close of the spike or the next open; the first attempt almost always is followed by a new high.
- One strong bear trend bar does not make a reversal; most fail and lead to a new trend high; weak follow-through (4 overlapping bars with prominent tails) confirms failure.
- Bear spike with no follow-through in a strong bull trend = vacuum; buy the bottom (SPY "on sale" by 1-2 percent).
- Head and shoulders bottoms: most fail; shape alone is not enough; require prior countertrend strength.
- After a double bottom bull flag long fails: move stop to breakeven; the stop run may set up a breakout pullback long, but if selling continues, the one-bar breakout pullback after the break is a great short.
- Failed double bottom bull flag: the low 2 pullback after a strong bear spike leads to a break below the double bottom and a two-legged move; short entries: below the low 2 bar, 1-2 ticks below the double bottom low, or on the close of the breakout bar / the follow-through bar.
- After a strong move, a trading range is usually a continuation pattern; first-hour double top/bottom flags are mostly tradable but scalp part and move stop to breakeven.
- A seven-bar selloff with no bullish strength -> wait for a second entry even at a higher low; a breakout pullback second entry is preferred.
- Two legs down in a bull trend or sideways market, especially two pushes below a flat MA -> always a good long. Two legs up above a flat MA into a double top bear flag -> short.
- Tight channel: a high 3 is more reliable than a high 2 (channels often reverse after the third push). A 1-tick break below a double bottom bull flag that traps longs out and immediately reverses = especially good long.
- Eager-trader tells (strong trend signals): a bar turning 1 tick short of a prior low/high (limit orders 1-2 ticks early); a pullback stopping 2 ticks below the MA instead of 1; a low 1 after a bottom that fails to make a new low.
- Higher-high fades on a two-sided day: shorts fell 26 cents and 18 cents (one scratched at a 4 cent loss); a double top to the penny with a truncated three-push = two down legs likely.
- Measured move magnet: a test within 3 ticks is not complete (most traders need within 1 tick) -> expect a pullback and a more convincing test. A test >= 4 ticks beyond the target -> the market is ignoring it and heading to a higher target.
- 60-min one-tick trap: a 5-min reversal 2 bars after a 1-tick break of the prior 60-min high usually reverses back above the breakout before the 60-min bar closes (here it closed 3 ticks above).
- Clear bear trend day: trade only with the trend; sell low 2 setups, especially at the MA; swing part. QQQ trades reaching only 8-11 ticks exit around breakeven.

## Ch10. Huge volume reversals on daily charts

- Daily stock in a steep bear trend + a day with volume 5-10x the recent average -> possible capitulation and tradable bottom.
- The huge-volume day is very often a gap-down day; a strong bull close raises the odds of a profitable long.
- Expectation after a climactic selloff: at least a two-legged move to at least the MA, lasting days to weeks (not necessarily a trend reversal).
- Entry: with a strong bull trend bar on the volume day, buy the close; cautious entry = above the high of that signal bar.
- Next-day options: buy the open; buy a new intraday high after a test down; buy the 5-min reversal up after a failed gap-close attempt.
- Fig 10.1 (LEH): volume 3x the prior day and ~10x the prior month's average, gap down below a bear trend channel line, strong rally into the close -> gapped up next day; such strength is "almost always" followed by at least two legs up and a penetration of the MA.
- Fig 10.2 (BSC): volume ~15x a typical day but only ~1.5x the prior day, minimal bull tail, close well below the trend channel line, no reversal from the channel break -> lost another 80 percent on the next open.
- Rule: climactic volume without bullish price action is no reason to fade a strong bear trend; require a bull reversal bar / strong bull close and a failed break of the trend channel line.
- Intraday: a 1-min Emini bar of ~25,000 contracts in a strong bear trend is unlikely to end the trend; it usually signals a pullback soon, typically after 1-2 lower lows on less volume (volume divergence).
- Rule: rarely if ever use volume on intraday charts; its predictive value on the 5-min chart is unreliable.
- Part II preamble numbers: avoid 1-2 pt Emini scalps (10-20 cent stock scalps) until consistently profitable; the 3-min chart has a lower win rate than the 5-min; focus on the best 2-5 entries per day, usually second entries at new swing highs/lows on nontrending days (~80 percent of days) and spike-and-pullback entries on trend days.

## Numeric candidates for the engine

- THREE_PUSH detector: 3 swing extremes in the trend direction, each beyond the prior; reversal candidate requires >= 20 bars from push 1 to push 3 (flag-type wedge: 10-20 bars). Entry stop 1 tick beyond the push-3 signal bar; target 1 = pullback extreme after push 1 (wedge start); target 2 = wedge height projected from target 1.
- SHRINKING_STAIRS: ext2 = push2 - push1, ext3 = push3 - push2; ext3 < ext2 (e.g. 7 vs 10 ticks) -> raise reversal score; ext3 >> ext2 then reversal -> trend channel overshoot reversal.
- PARABOLIC: slope(push2->push3) > slope(push1->push2) -> climactic; on reversal expect 2 legs / ~10 bars (p ~0.60).
- COUNT_RESET: if the push-2 leg's momentum (body sum, slope) exceeds the push-1 leg by a threshold -> expect a 4th push; defer the reversal entry.
- WEDGE_FAIL: after entry triggers, price trades >= 1 tick beyond the wedge extreme -> flip bias; target = wedge height projected beyond the extreme; optional reverse-on-stop at extreme + 1 tick. A failing breakout usually fails within 5 bars.
- TIGHT_CHANNEL filter: no countertrend entry when the channel is tight (small pullbacks, overlapping bars, no prior trend line break); require a second entry (higher low after a bottom / lower high after a top). Exception window: first 60-120 minutes of the session.
- ONE_TICK_SEQ breakout: two successive 1-tick (<= 2-tick) breakouts beyond a minor swing, each pulling back 3-20 bars, then a third break beyond the second -> enter with the breakout; target = range height.
- EXPANDING_TRIANGLE: >= 5 alternating swings with HH and LL each exceeding the prior; entry 1 tick beyond the 5th-leg bar; target 1 = opposite side of the triangle; target 2 = height of the last leg; if the target-1 breakout fails -> flip to continuation with 7 legs.
- CHANNEL_REENTRY: breakout beyond a channel line then a strong close back inside within <= 5 bars -> p ~0.50 of reaching the opposite channel line.
- FINAL_FLAG candidate: trend >= ~24 bars, flag 5-10+ bars mostly horizontal, >= 50 percent bar overlap, several opposite-color bodies / prominent tails; breakout then reversal bar -> entry 1 tick beyond the reversal bar; expect >= 2 legs, ~10 bars; if trend length is 50-100 bars, model the outcome as a range, not an opposite trend.
- FLAG_BREAK_CONFIRM: accept a flag breakout only when a trend bar closes several ticks (>= 2-3) beyond the flag boundary; otherwise keep the pullback premise.
- FAILED_FINAL_FLAG: reversal entry bar reversed within <= 2 bars -> breakout pullback in the trend direction; if that resumption reverses within 1-2 bars -> second entry for the reversal; a second failure -> channel mode, stop fading.
- POST_REVERSAL_LIMIT_FADE: after a confirmed reversal pattern, limit buys at the low to 3 ticks below the low of low-1 / low-2 signal bars, stop 4 ticks (Emini); mirror for shorts.
- DOUBLE_BOTTOM_PULLBACK: bottom2 <= bottom1 (slightly lower preferred); rally; higher low above bottom2; entry 1 tick above the pullback signal bar; if bottom2 > bottom1 in a strong trend -> downgrade to scalp or skip. Mirror for double top pullback: sell stop 1 tick below the pullback-high bar.
- RANGE_FADE vs BREAKOUT: range fade with a good setup p ~0.60 for R >= 1; strong breakout of a tight range p ~0.70 for R >= 1; failed breakout -> trade opposite.
- STOP_RUN_ENTRY: entry stop at 1 tick beyond the signal/entry bar of the opposing setup (where protective stops sit); expect at least a scalp, usually 2 legs. If the level is tested but missed by 1 tick -> with-trend double bottom/top flag entry.
- BREAKOUT_TEST: pullback to within +2 ticks (long) of the original signal bar high that turns back -> with-trend entry above that bar.
- TARGET_FAILURE: a 4-tick scalp needs a 6-tick excursion; max excursion exactly 5 ticks then reversal = five-tick failure -> exit / fade. Nine-tick (2 pt), 13-tick (3 pt), 17-tick (4 pt), 21-tick (5 pt): if max excursion == target ticks and the next bar trades beyond the opposite side of the peak bar -> expect at least a scalp the other way; never let a 9-tick+ excursion become a loss.
- SIXTEEN_TICK_HOLD: excursion reaches 16 ticks -> p ~0.80 of reaching 17-18; hold unless price trades below the prior bar's low.
- MEASURED_MOVE_TEST: a test ending >= 3 ticks short of a measured move target is incomplete -> expect a pullback then a retest to within 1 tick; a move >= 4 ticks beyond the target -> target ignored, project the next target.
- EAGERNESS_TELL: a pullback turning 1 tick short of a prior swing, or stopping 2 ticks short of the MA (instead of 1) = urgency of the trend side; score as a with-trend signal, not a failed test.
- SECOND_PUSH_AFTER_FAILED_LOW2: after a failed low 2 with strong momentum, p > 0.60 of a second push up before any reversal.
- HUGE_BAR_STOP_RUN: after a large trend bar with no tail, expect a stop run 1 tick beyond it within the next 5-10 bars, then resumption; treat the run as a with-trend entry if it reverses back within a bar.
- NO_INTRADAY_VOLUME: do not use volume as a 5-min feature; daily only (5-10x average volume + bull close -> 2-leg bounce to the MA).
