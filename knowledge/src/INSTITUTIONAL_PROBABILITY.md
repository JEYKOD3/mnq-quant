# Institutional Probability and Statistics for Intraday Index Futures

Scope: raw, usable facts for a rules-based MNQ/MES intraday system on 1-min and 5-min Databento data.
Each claim is tagged: **[DOC]** documented with numbers and a source, **[DESC]** described by a credible source but without hard numbers, **[FOLK]** retail folklore or vendor claim with no reproducible sample. Compiled 2026-10-21.

## 1. Day-type classification

### 1a. Market Profile / auction theory (Dalton, "Mind Over Markets") [DESC]
Source: [LuxAlgo day-type taxonomy](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/day-type-taxonomy.md), [marketcalls day types](https://www.marketcalls.in/market-profile/market-profile-different-types-of-profile-days.html)

- Initial balance (IB) = first hour of RTH (09:30 to 10:30 ET). Day types are defined by what price does relative to the IB.
- **Normal day**: wide IB, whole session contained inside it, two-sided rotation. No extension.
- **Normal variation day**: IB extended on one side, extension commonly about 1x the IB width beyond the IB (so the day's range is roughly 2x IB).
- **Trend day**: narrow IB, steady one-directional extension, close near the extreme, little rotation. "One-timeframing": each 30-min period holds above the prior period's low (uptrend) or below the prior period's high.
- **Double-distribution trend day**: quiet IB, then a gap-like move to a second value area where price builds a new distribution. Dalton describes it; no frequency figures exist.
- **Neutral day**: extends both sides of the IB. "Neutral extreme" if it closes at one extreme.
- **Non-trend day**: tiny range, no conviction, often pre-event.
- Dalton's rules of thumb: conviction opens (open-drive, open-test-drive) lean toward trend/normal-variation; open-auction inside prior range leans rotational. Day type is only certain at the close; early classification gets revised.
- No published frequency table exists in the Dalton material. "Trend days are a minority of sessions in liquid markets" is the only stated frequency [DESC].

### 1b. Measured IB statistics (what replaces the folklore)
- **ES IB vs day range** [DOC]: ES, 1,000 sessions ending Nov 2010: IB range mean 10.00 pts, median 8.75; day session range mean 20.25, median 16.75. So median day range is about 1.9x to 2x IB. ES opened above prior day's range 18.4% of days and below it 14.7% (1,340 days). [Linnsoft ES homework stats](https://www.linnsoft.com/videos/homework-average-range-volume-and-other-tendencies)
- **IB break rate** [DOC]: NQ/ES 2014 to 2026, about 3,000 break-days per instrument: IB high or low is broken on roughly 96 to 98% of sessions. [Tradingstats IB retest study](https://tradingstats.net/initial-balance-retest-statistics/)
- **IB extension reached, conditional on a break (NQ)** [DOC]: 1.1x IB reached 91%, 1.2x 80%, 1.3x 71%, 1.4x 62%, 1.5x 54%. ES is "a touch higher" at each level. (Same source.)
- **Retest of the broken IB level (NQ / ES)** [DOC]: after reaching 1.1x: 87% / 90% return to the level; 1.2x: 75% / 81%; 1.3x: 63% / 70%; 1.4x: 53% / 61%; 1.5x: 45% / 53%. Median delay from extension to first return: 5 min at 1.1x, 55 min at 1.5x. About 90% of first returns hold within 10% of IB range of the level. (Same source.)
- **After reaching the extension, what happens (NQ)** [DOC]: at 1.1x: ran away 13%, retest then continued 71%, retest then reversed 16%. At 1.3x: 37% / 37% / 26%. At 1.5x: 55% / 18% / 27%. Crossover where reversal-after-retest first exceeds continuation-after-retest: ~1.40x on NQ, ~1.50x on ES. (Same source.)
- **NQ IB extension in % of price (all sessions, 2013 to 2025, >3,000 sessions)** [DOC]: above IB high: median +0.31%, 75th pct +0.60%, 90th +1.00%. Below IB low: median -0.38%, 75th -0.79%, 90th -1.37%. Downside extensions are fatter. [TradingView IB Statistical Mapping](https://www.tradingview.com/script/2iuvi66N-Initial-Balance-Statistical-Mapping/)
- **Conditional IB patterns (NQ)** [DOC, vendor script]: "Bearish IB + high made first + close below IB mid" broke the low first 73.9% (n=1,127). "Bullish IB + low first + close above mid" closed bullish 76.7% (n=1,281). Midpoint retest after a break 40 to 65%. (Same source.)
- **Narrow IB implies extension** [FOLK]: "IB below 20th percentile of recent IBs gives 70 to 80% IB extension" is widely repeated and attributed to "Market Profile research" with no sample. [NexusFi ORB article](https://nexusfi.com/a/strategies/opening-range-breakout). Given the unconditional break rate is already ~97%, the claim is content-free unless it is about extension size, which nobody measured publicly. Test it yourself.

### 1c. Trend-day frequency and early signs
- **Frequency** [FOLK]: "15 to 25% of days", "2 to 4 per month", "range days are 40 to 50% of sessions" are all repeated (NexusFi, Brooks, Raschke commentary) with no published sample. No ES/NQ study with a stated definition and count was found. The one measurable proxy: 54% of NQ IB-break days reach 1.5x IB (Tradingstats), and the median day is ~2x IB (Linnsoft), so "day range > 2.5x IB and close in the top/bottom 15%" will likely land in the 15 to 25% zone, but this has to be computed on your data.
- **Definitions used by practitioners** [DESC]: close in the top or bottom quarter of the range, range above the 10-day average daily range, price never revisits the open after the first 30 to 60 min, price stays on one side of VWAP after the IB, "rotational factor" stays above 6 (Market Profile). [NexusFi trend-day thread](https://nexusfi.com/traders-hideout/18751-definition-trending-day.html)
- **Raschke / Crabel early signs** [DESC, mostly from the books, numbers unverified]: trend days tend to follow NR7 or inside days (volatility contraction), open with a gap or an open-drive outside the prior day's value area, make a shallow first pullback (does not retrace to the open), show early breadth skew (Steenbarger: advancers minus decliners > 2,000 issues in the first hour), and first-hour volume above the recent first-hour average. Crabel's ORB research (Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities, 1989 series) found that breakouts after NR4/NR7/inside days have the highest follow-through; his "stretch" = 10-day average of the smaller of (open minus low, high minus open). [Crabel ORB Part 4](https://store.traders.com/-v07-c02-orb-pdf.html), [LuxAlgo NR4/NR7](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/nr4-nr7-narrow-range-bars.md)
- **One measured early sign** [DOC, SPY, 6 months to Feb 2026]: first 15-min candle green, day closes green ~70%; red, day closes red ~71%. Small sample, one regime. [TradeThatSwing stats](https://tradethatswing.com/high-probability-stock-market-statistics/)
- **Overnight direction agreeing with the day session** [DOC]: ES, 1,340 days to 2010: day session agreed with overnight direction only 29 more times than it disagreed, i.e. essentially a coin flip. (Linnsoft, above.)

## 2. Base rates

### 2a. Opening range breakout (ORB)
- **Double-break rates, SPY, 6 months to Feb 2026** [DOC, small sample]: 5-min OR: both sides broken ~71%, only 14% of days break one side and never touch the other. 15-min OR: double break ~56%. 30-min: ~42%. 45-min: ~31%. 60-min IB: after a 5-min close outside, only that side breaks ~78%; no break at all ~5%. [TradeThatSwing stats](https://tradethatswing.com/high-probability-stock-market-statistics/). Interpretation: the 5-min ORB is mostly a stop-hunt range; one-sided continuation only becomes the base case at 30 to 60 min.
- **Edgeful 5-min ES ORB backtest** [DOC, vendor, 6 months]: 115 trades, 72.17% win rate, profit factor 1.62, target = 50% of range, stop = opposite side of range (reward 0.5R), skip if range > 0.55% of open, Tuesdays off. Breakeven win rate at 0.5R is 67%, so the net edge is thin and the sample is short. [Edgeful 5-min ORB](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/5-minute-opening-range-breakout-es-strategy). Note that the 71% double-break stat above says the opposite-side stop gets hit on most days; the two results only reconcile because the 50% target is reached first.
- **Tradingstats.net** does not publish an ORB study per se; its relevant studies are IB retest (above), high-of-day hold, and price extension after breakout. Homepage claims ("94% overnight range break", "79% actionable win rate") are vendor marketing [FOLK]. [tradingstats.net](https://tradingstats.net)
- **Academic ORB (stocks, not futures)** [DOC]: Zarattini, Barbon, Aziz 2023, 5-min ORB on "stocks in play" (top relative volume), 2016 to 2023, 7,000+ stocks: top-20 portfolio +1,600% net, Sharpe 2.81, alpha 36%/yr. The edge is the stock selection (relative volume at the open), not the ORB mechanics. [Concretum summary](https://concretumgroup.com/a-profitable-day-trading-strategy-for-the-u-s-equity-market/). Earlier paper "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?" (TQQQ 5-min ORB with ATR stop, 10R target, EOD exit) is on SSRN; a Substack reviewer notes the result depends heavily on the 10R target and the 2016 to 2023 bull regime. [Retail Traders Repository review](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/trading-research-paper-can-day-trading-really-be-profitable)
- **NR7 filtered ORB on ES hits 1x OR target 60 to 65%** [FOLK]: NexusFi, no sample.

### 2b. Breakout extension distribution (how far a break runs)
- **NQ/ES 2014 to 2026, 1-min data, bar-vs-prior-bar breaks** [DOC]: on 1H bars, next bar breaks prior high ~49%, prior low ~44%, stays inside ~17%. Median extension beyond the broken level ≈ 39 to 40% of the prior bar's range; 75th pct ≈ 75%; 85th ≈ 100 to 105%; 99th ≈ 3x. Breaks that stall short of the median run (~40%) fail back inside the range 65 to 71% of the time; breaks that clear the median hold ~80%. Same ~45-point gap on every timeframe and both instruments. [Tradingstats price extension](https://tradingstats.net/price-extension-after-breakout/). Usable: a break that extends less than 0.4x the prior range is a fade candidate; past 0.4x it is a continuation candidate.

### 2c. Gap fill
- **ES, same-day fill by gap size** [DOC-ish, vendor backtest, "5-year ES/NQ 1-min data", dates unstated]: 0.25 to 0.5% gaps fill same day 84% (48% in first hour, avg 45 min); 0.5 to 1.0%: 71% (38% first hour, 78 min); >1.0%: 55% (22% first hour, 2.4 h). NQ: ~55% fill within 30 min, ~75% by close (no size split). [NexusFi fill-the-gap](https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/fill-the-gap)
- **Session mix, ES/NQ/YM/RTY 2014 to 2026, 12,390 sessions** [DOC]: ~60% open inside prior range, 25% gap up, 15% gap down. In-range opens touch prior close 71 to 73%. Gap-down opens revert less often (no figure). (Same source.)
- **SPY, 6 months to Feb 2026** [DOC, small]: gaps fill 59 to 69% overall; fill odds drop sharply above 0.4%. Open inside prior day range tags prior high or low 86%; open above prior high returns to it 71%; same below the low. [TradeThatSwing](https://tradethatswing.com/high-probability-stock-market-statistics/)
- **Edgeful half-gap vs full-gap** [DESC]: half-gap fill rate materially higher than full-gap; define "fill" precisely before testing. [Edgeful half gaps](https://www.edgeful.com/blog/posts/half-gaps-vs-full-gaps)
- Bulkowski gap-type fill rates (common ~80% same session, breakaway ~60% over days) are for stocks [DOC for stocks, FOLK for futures].

### 2d. Time of day's high / low
- **Theory** [DOC]: under an intraday random walk, the daily high and low are more likely near the open or close than mid-session (arcsine-law shape). For S&P 500 futures the random-walk timing cannot be rejected; Hang Seng futures show excess highs/lows in the first 15 min after each open. [HKBU, daily high/low time in two index futures](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/using-daily-highlow-time-to-test-for-intraday-random-walk-in-two-/). So "the first hour sets the high or low often" is mostly mechanics, not edge.
- **Stocks, 55 names, 60,300 one-minute samples, 2003 to 2009** [DOC]: high set in first minute 10%, first 30 min 40%, first hour 49%; last hour 19%, last 30 min 14%. Low: first hour 46%, last hour 20%. [ThePatternSite](https://thepatternsite.com/IntradayHighLow.html). Implication: roughly half of days have one extreme inside the first hour; about 1 in 5 has an extreme in the last hour.
- **Futures, NQ/ES/YM/RTY 2019 to 2026, ~14,300 running extremes per market, session anchored 18:00 ET** [DOC]: an extreme fades (50% retrace toward the open before session end) ~58 to 59% unconditionally; highs ~55%, lows ~61%. By depth: shallow pokes (~0.25 ATR beyond the open) fade ~85%; beyond ~1.0 ATR fade only ~19 to 20%. Overnight and early-morning extremes fade freely; late cash-session extremes rarely fade. Once unfaded for a few hours, reverts ~25%. Fixed-target win rate vs stop width on NQ: 0.10 ATR stop ~37% win at 1.6:1; 1.0 ATR stop ~91% win at 0.17:1; both roughly breakeven before costs (random-entry baseline). [Tradingstats high-of-day hold](https://tradingstats.net/high-of-the-day-hold/)

### 2e. Last-hour continuation (intraday momentum literature)
- **Gao, Han, Li, Zhou (2018, JFE), SPY 1999 to 2012** [DOC]: first half-hour return (prior close to 10:00) predicts last half-hour return (15:30 to 16:00). R² ~2% full sample, 4.3% in the 2007 to 2009 crisis, 4.8% when first-half-hour volatility is high. OOS R² 1.8% (2.7% adding the 12th half hour). Sign-timing strategy: ~6.3% annualized gross vs -0.5% for always holding the last half hour. Stronger on high-volatility, high-volume, recession, and macro-release days (CPI, GDP, FOMC). QQQ similar but weaker. [AlphaArchitect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/), [CXO review](https://cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/first-and-last-half-hours-of-trading-linked). CXO critique: frictions ignored; the effect is driven by extreme first-hour days; average last-half-hour move after normal days is too small to capture net.
- **Replication, QuantConnect SPY/IWM/IYR 2015 to 2020** [DOC]: strategy Sharpe -0.63 vs benchmark 0.58 over the full window; only outperformed in the Feb to Mar 2020 crash (Sharpe 1.45 vs -1.47). [QuantConnect intraday ETF momentum](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/15348/intraday-etf-momentum/). Lesson: in calm bull regimes the effect is not tradeable net of costs.
- **Index futures, Baltussen, Da, Lammers, Martens (2021, JFE), 1974 to 2020, 60+ futures** [DOC]: the best predictor of the last 30 min is the rest-of-day return (prior close to 15:30), not the first half hour. Equity index futures pooled: beta 4.18 (t=7.29), adj R² 2.45%, OOS R² 2.88%; significant OOS in 14 of 17 equity contracts. Timing strategy: 6.86%/yr, Sharpe 1.73, success rate 55% (vs always-long Sharpe 0.11). Post-2000 beta 3.98 (t=6.65), so the effect persists. First-half-hour alone: beta 4.86 (t=6.52) but OOS R² negative; Sharpe 1.07. The predictability stops at the 16:00 cash close. Next 3 days partially reverse. S&P futures still show positive net Sharpe at a one-tick cost. [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf), [AlphaArchitect](https://alphaarchitect.com/hot-topic-does-gamma-hedging-actually-affect-stock-prices/)
- **Gamma conditioning of the same effect** [DOC]: S&P 500, 1996 to 2020, days with negative dealer net gamma (n=2,930): rest-of-day beta 6.63 (t=4.78), R² 3.58%. Positive gamma days (n=3,158): beta 0.82 (t=1.03), nothing. Interaction NGE x rROD = -123 (t=-3.42). Last-hour momentum is essentially a negative-gamma phenomenon. (Same paper.)
- **International cash indices, 16 markets, 2000 to 2017** [DOC]: pooled slope 2.68 (t=7.53), significant in 12 of 16; stronger in crises (3.71 vs 2.09) and recessions (4.04 vs 2.57); stronger when liquidity is low and when information arrives gradually. Sign strategy Sharpe 0.02 (Canada) to 1.24 (UK). [Li, Sakkas, Urquhart](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/5273196)
- **Afternoon reversal** [DOC]: the Baltussen paper's "different sign" case (first half hour and rest-of-day disagree) shows the first-half-hour coefficient flipping negative (-3.71, t=-2.76): when the morning move has already been reversed by 15:30, the morning direction is a contrarian signal for the last 30 min. No clean "afternoon reversal frequency" stat exists in the literature; everything else is folklore.

## 3. Execution, VWAP, dealer gamma, 0DTE

### 3a. VWAP / TWAP / POV
- Institutions benchmark large orders to VWAP (arrival-price / implementation shortfall is the other main benchmark). VWAP algos slice the parent order along the historical intraday volume curve (U-shaped: heavy at open and close), POV algos target a fixed share of printed volume (typically 5 to 20%). [Kearns, Implementation Shortfall survey](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/impshort.pdf), [VWAP dynamical volume approach](https://ourarchive.otago.ac.nz/bitstream/handle/10523/1540/ImprovingVWAPStrategiesADynamicalVolumeApproach2006.pdf?sequence=3) [DESC]
- **Why VWAP pullbacks get bought on trend days** [DESC, mechanism not a measured stat]: a VWAP-benchmarked buyer who is "behind schedule" and above VWAP is incentivised to buy more aggressively when price returns to or below VWAP (cheaper than benchmark), and to slow down when price is well above it. Aggregated across many such algos the natural result is support near session VWAP on a day with net institutional buying, and resistance on a net-selling day. It is also why price "stretches then returns": POV/VWAP participation is passive and cannot chase. No public study quantifies touch-and-hold rates on ES/NQ; vendor scripts exist but publish no sample. Treat "price returns to VWAP X% of the time" as [FOLK] until measured.
- Testable consequence: on days classified as trend (section 1c), the first touch of session VWAP after 10:30 ET should hold with a higher-than-random rate, and the distance-from-VWAP in ATR units should be a usable mean-reversion feature on range days only.

### 3b. Dealer gamma regimes
- **Mechanism** [DOC]: dealers long gamma (customers net short options) delta-hedge by selling rallies and buying dips, dampening moves and producing negative intraday autocorrelation (mean reversion, pinning). Dealers short gamma hedge in the direction of the move, producing positive autocorrelation and higher realised volatility. [BSIC dealer gamma review](https://bsic.it/how-dealers-gamma-impacts-underlying-stocks/)
- **Barbon and Buraschi (2021)** [DOC]: one-sigma rise in gamma imbalance lowers absolute daily return by 5 to 25 bp for single stocks and >20 bp (about 20% of a standard deviation) for indices; lowers return autocorrelation at 5, 10, 20, 30, 60-min horizons by 1.5 to 2%, strongest at 5 and 60 min; one-sigma fall in gamma raises the daily high-low range by ~16 bp. (Same review.)
- **Ni, Pearson, Poteshman, White (2018)** [DOC]: hedge rebalancing explains ~9.5% of daily absolute returns 1990 to 2001 and ~13.4% 2002 to 2012; raises the probability of a >3% daily move by 13% and of a >5% move by 40% in the later sample. (Same review.)
- **Beckmeyer and Moerke (2021)** [DOC]: end-of-day delta hedging produces ~18 bp of momentum or reversal per 1% move in the underlying in the last 30 min. (Same review.)
- **Baltussen et al. (above)** [DOC]: last-30-min momentum is present only on negative-gamma days.
- Practical [DESC]: public gamma proxies (SpotGamma, vendor "gamma flip" levels) are estimates built on a customer-vs-dealer assumption; the exact gamma number is not observable. Spot above the vendor "gamma flip" = positive regime (fade extremes, expect pinning near large strikes); below = negative regime (trend continuation, wider ranges, momentum into the close). [SpotGamma 0DTE explainer](https://spotgamma.com/0dte-options-explained/)

### 3c. 0DTE since 2022
- **Share of SPX volume** [DOC]: ~20% in 2020, >50% by 2024, approaching 63% in Feb 2026 per Cboe. Every-business-day SPX expiry started in 2022. [SpotGamma](https://spotgamma.com/0dte-options-explained/), [LuxAlgo 0DTE flow](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/0dte-flow-effects.md)
- **Academic finding (Brogaard et al.-style study summarised by Quantpedia)** [DOC]: on 0DTE expiry days S&P realised volatility is ~60 annualised bp lower on average; market makers are net long gamma through the expiry day (mostly from older positions rolling into 0DTE status); higher net gamma predicts lower volatility over the next 10 min and stronger order-flow reversal in E-mini futures, fading after ~1 hour; 1 bp more hedging demand ≈ 4 bp less realised vol. [Quantpedia 0DTE](https://quantpedia.com/do-sp500-0dtes-options-increase-market-volatility/). Net: 0DTE on average dampens, it does not amplify. The "0DTE causes crashes" narrative is [FOLK] on the average day; the risk is tail days when dealers are short gamma into big strikes.
- **Time-of-day effects** [DESC]: hedging flow intensifies in the last 2 hours (14:00 to 15:30 ET), pinning concentrates at strikes with heavy same-day volume into 16:00, activity clusters around CPI/FOMC. SpotGamma's retail rules (confirm a gamma flip by 10 to 15 min hold, avoid first 15 to 30 min and last 15 min) are heuristics, not stats [FOLK].
- Cboe's own research: average net impact of 0DTE flow is modest and flow is generally balanced [DESC].

## 4. Statistical process used by quant desks

- **Expectancy in R** [DOC]: E = p·R − (1−p), where p = win rate and R = avg win / avg loss. Breakeven p* = 1/(1+R): 0.5R needs 67%, 1R needs 50%, 2R needs 33%, 3R needs 25%. Profit factor PF = p·R/(1−p). Random-entry win rate ≈ stop/(stop+target). [Tradingstats win rate / PF / expectancy](https://tradingstats.net/win-rate-profit-factor-expectancy/)
- **Net of costs** [DOC]: PF_net ≈ PF_gross·(1−f)/(1+f) with f = friction as a share of the average trade's gross size. A gross PF 1.4 becomes ~1.15 at f=10% and <1.0 at f=20%. In the Tradingstats random-entry grid on NQ, all 36 configurations were negative after 1 point round trip, and the best cell needed costs under ~0.8 pts to survive. "Always compute PF net." (Same source.)
- **Sample size** [DOC]: 95% CI on a win rate: 70% after 20 trades is 48 to 85%; 60% after 100 is 50 to 69%; 70% after 100 is 60 to 78%. Rule of thumb: <50 trades is anecdote, ~100 starts to mean something, 300 to 500+ before planning around it. Track expectancy on a rolling 50 to 100 trade window. (Same source.) General formula: SE(p) = sqrt(p(1−p)/n); to distinguish 55% from 50% at 95% confidence with 80% power needs n ≈ 780 trades.
- **Deflated Sharpe / minimum track record (Bailey and López de Prado)** [DOC]: reported backtest Sharpe must be deflated for the number of trials tried and for skew/kurtosis; the Minimum Track Record Length is the number of observations needed for the observed Sharpe to be significantly above a benchmark Sharpe given the return distribution. With skewed, fat-tailed intraday returns MinTRL grows fast. [Deflated Sharpe Ratio, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551), [Probability of Backtest Overfitting](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253). Practical: log every variant you test; the more variants, the higher the bar.
- **Carver ("Systematic Trading") practice** [DOC]: realistic live Sharpe for a retail systematic trader is around 1.0 after costs; backtested Sharpe of 2 to 3 is almost always overfitting; multiply bootstrap OOS Sharpe by 0.75 for an honest estimate; size risk at half Kelly, never full Kelly; set a single annualised volatility target (he caps his own at 37%) and size each position by its recent volatility so each trade carries the same expected risk; keep costs at or below one-third of the conservative pre-cost Sharpe; limit the number of rules tested; always use rolling or expanding out-of-sample fitting; fit per instrument only when differences are statistically significant; skewed rules show flattering Sharpe. [CXO notes on Systematic Trading](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/a-few-notes-on-systematic-trading)
- **Why size by volatility** [DOC]: a fixed-contract or fixed-stop-in-points size means risk per trade swings with the ATR regime (NQ ATR has ranged roughly 3x across 2022 to 2026); vol-scaling keeps dollar risk per trade and portfolio vol stable so the Kelly fraction stays constant and drawdown statistics are comparable across regimes. Half Kelly gives ~75% of full-Kelly growth with about half the variance. [Kelly explainer](https://ryanoconnellfinance.com/kelly-criterion/)
- **Regime conditioning** [DOC via papers above]: the published intraday effects are conditional: last-hour momentum only on negative-gamma, high-vol, high-volume, news days; ORB only on high relative-volume names; IB extension odds vary with IB width. Unconditional backtests average a strong regime with a dead one and look mediocre; conditional ones have fewer trades and need the sample-size math above.
- **Walk-forward** [DOC]: Baltussen used an expanding OOS window with at least 500 initial observations; Tradingstats trains on prior years and grades the held-out year (AUC ~0.82, stable 2021 to 2026). Minimum practice: anchor the first fit on at least 2 years, step by quarters, report only the stitched OOS equity.
- **Declaring a strategy dead** [DESC, consensus not a single source]: compare live results to the backtest's own distribution, not to zero. Common desk rules: (a) live drawdown exceeds the worst backtest drawdown (or 1.5x it) once; (b) rolling 100-trade expectancy falls below the 5th percentile of bootstrapped backtest windows; (c) the conditioning variable that justified the edge has structurally changed (e.g. the gamma regime, costs, contract liquidity); (d) a time stop: a strategy with Sharpe S needs about (2/S)² years to be distinguishable from zero at ~2 sigma, so a Sharpe-1 strategy that is flat for 2 years is not yet "dead" statistically, but one that is down 1.5x max backtest DD is. [Medium, when to stop trading a strategy](https://medium.com/@ankit_quant/when-to-stop-trading-a-strategy-28d104bb20b6), [R-bloggers hypothesis-driven dev](https://r-bloggers.com/?p=105905)

## 5. Microstructure for scalping micro futures

- **Contract facts** [DOC]: MNQ tick 0.25 index points = $0.50; MES tick 0.25 = $1.25; NQ tick = $5.00; ES tick = $12.50. CME exchange fee for micros ~$0.32 to $0.37 per side (changes yearly), NFA $0.02 per side; broker commission from ~$0.25 (discount) to $2+ per side. [NexusFi exchange fees](https://nexusfi.com/a/exchanges/futures-exchange-fees), [Optimus commission breakdown](https://optimusfutures.com/blog/commissions-trade-futures)
- **Spread** [DOC]: ES and NQ are one tick wide essentially all of RTH; MNQ/MES track them and are one tick wide most of the time but with thinner books, so a market order can occasionally take 2 ticks. Round-trip spread cost for a market-in/market-out scalp: 1 to 2 ticks.
- **Stop-order slippage** [DESC]: a stop becomes a market order; on MNQ in normal RTH expect 0 to 2 ticks beyond the stop price, more during 08:30/10:00 ET releases and the first minute after the open. Tradingstats and most published backtests assume 1 tick per side. Model 1 tick per side as baseline, 2 ticks during news windows, and run a sensitivity at 3.
- **Your cost line (MNQ at $2.37 per side)** [DOC, arithmetic]: commission round trip $4.74 = 9.5 ticks = 2.37 points. Add ~1 tick spread and ~1 tick stop slippage: total friction ≈ $5.75 to $6.25 ≈ 11.5 to 12.5 ticks ≈ 3 NQ points per round trip. On MES at the same commission: 3.8 ticks of commission ≈ 0.95 ES points, plus 1 to 2 ticks ≈ 1.3 to 1.5 ES points total.
- **Why 1:1 scalps need well over 55%** [DOC, arithmetic]: with target T, stop S (points) and friction c, breakeven p* = (S + c)/(T + S). At T = S this is 0.5 + c/(2S). With c = 3 pts on MNQ: S = 10 pts needs 65%; S = 15 needs 60%; S = 20 needs 57.5%; S = 30 needs 55%; S = 50 needs 53%. So a 1:1 MNQ scalp only gets near the "55%" folklore when the stop is at least 30 points. At 2:1 (T = 2S): S = 10 needs 43%; S = 20 needs 38%.
- **Minimum target** [DOC, arithmetic]: friction of 3 pts is 10% of a 30-pt average win and 30% of a 10-pt win. Using PF_net ≈ PF_gross·(1−f)/(1+f), a gross PF 1.5 strategy with 10-pt average trades (f=30%) nets 0.81 (loser); with 30-pt trades (f=10%) nets 1.23. Practical floor on MNQ at this commission: average trade size ≥ 25 to 30 points (f ≤ 10 to 12%), i.e. targets below ~20 points are structurally hard to make profitable.
- **MES vs MNQ** [DOC]: MES point = $5, MNQ point = $2; per-dollar-of-risk commission on MES is about 2.5x lower when stops are expressed in points, but NQ moves roughly 2 to 3x more points per day, so friction as a share of ATR is comparable. Decide by ATR-relative friction: f = friction / (expected win in points), not by instrument name.

## What links all of this

1. **Almost every published intraday edge is conditional, not unconditional.** Last-hour momentum lives on negative-gamma, high-vol, news days; ORB works on high-relative-volume names; IB extension odds depend on IB width and the first extension's depth. Classify the regime first, then apply the rule.
2. **The first hour is mechanically special, not magically special.** Random walks put extremes near the open; the 5-min OR is broken both ways ~70% of the time; only the 30 to 60 min range gives one-sided continuation as the base case. The ORB "edge" lives in filters (NR7, relative volume, range vs ATR), not in the breakout itself.
3. **Depth and timing of a move determine its fade odds.** Shallow pokes (<0.3 ATR, <0.4x prior range) fail 65 to 85% of the time; deep ones (>1 ATR, >0.4x prior range) hold ~80%. Early extremes fade; late extremes hold. This single curve covers ORB fails, IB retests, gap fills, and stop hunts.
4. **Institutional execution is passive and benchmark-driven.** VWAP/POV algos cannot chase, so price stretches and returns; they buy pullbacks to their benchmark. Dealer hedging adds a second passive flow whose sign (gamma) sets whether the day mean-reverts or trends.
5. **Costs decide more than signals at scalp scale.** Random entries on NQ are negative at 1 pt round trip; a strategy's gross PF is irrelevant until friction is <10 to 12% of the average win. On MNQ at $2.37/side that means ≥25 to 30 point average trades.
6. **Win rate is a function of stop/target geometry before it is a function of skill.** Random-entry win rate ≈ stop/(stop+target). Judge a setup by expectancy in R and by win rate minus the random baseline for the same geometry, never by win rate alone.
7. **Sample size is the real constraint.** 100 trades gives a ±10% CI on win rate; conditional strategies produce 1 to 4 signals per day; rolling 100-trade expectancy with bootstrapped backtest bands is how desks monitor and retire.
8. **Backtest Sharpe above ~1.5 to 2 on intraday futures is a warning, not a result.** Deflate for trials, apply 0.75 to OOS, size at half Kelly on a volatility target.
9. **Effects decay at known boundaries.** The last-30-min momentum stops at the 16:00 cash close and reverses over the next 3 days; gamma effects fade after ~1 hour; 0DTE pinning disappears at expiry. Build time stops into every rule.

## Concrete rules to test on our MNQ/MES data

Measure for every rule: n, win rate, expectancy in R net of 3 pts MNQ (1.4 pts MES) friction, PF_net, max consecutive losses, result by year, and the random-entry baseline with the same stop/target geometry.

1. **Day-type labeller (run first, no trades).** For each RTH session compute IB = range 09:30 to 10:30 ET; day range; close location = (close − low)/range; ATR14 of daily ranges; IB percentile vs prior 20 sessions; gap = open − prior 16:00 close in ATR units; time of day's high and low; did price revisit the open after 10:30. Label: Trend = range ≥ 2x IB and close location ≥ 0.80 or ≤ 0.20 and no revisit of the open after 10:30. Range = range ≤ 1.3x IB. Report the monthly frequency of each label on NQ and ES 2022 to 2026 (this is the number nobody publishes).
2. **Early trend signal test (decision by 10:30).** Features at 10:30: IB percentile, gap size in ATR, first pullback depth as % of the move since open, VWAP slope over the last 30 min, first-hour volume vs 20-day first-hour average, 15-min opening candle colour. Measure P(Trend label) for each feature decile and for combos (IB < 30th pct AND gap > 0.5 ATR AND first pullback < 40%). Target: a filter with P(Trend) ≥ 40% vs the unconditional base rate.
3. **IB extension ladder (replicate Tradingstats).** On the first 1-min wick through IB high/low after 10:30: measure reach rates of 1.1x to 1.5x IB, retest rates, and continued-vs-reversed after retest. Rule to test: enter on the first retest of the broken IB level that holds within 10% of IB range, stop 25% of IB range beyond the level, target 1.3x IB. Expect ~70% continuation after retest at 1.1x on NQ.
4. **Shallow-break fade.** Any 1-min break of a 60-min bar's high/low that extends < 0.4x the prior bar's range and closes back inside within 5 min: fade toward the midpoint of the prior bar, stop 0.6x beyond the break. Published base rate: 65 to 71% fail back inside. Measure P(midpoint reached before stop).
5. **Deep-break continuation.** Same setup but extension ≥ 0.4x prior range and a 5-min close outside: enter on the first pullback to the level, stop 0.3x below it, target +1.0x prior range. Published base rate: ~80% hold.
6. **Opening-range sizing test.** Compare 5, 15, 30, 60-min ORB on NQ: double-break rate, one-sided continuation rate, and net expectancy with stop = opposite side, targets 0.5R, 1R, 2R. Expect 5-min double-break ~70% and 60-min ~22%. Only keep the timeframe where one-sided continuation > 60% and target ≥ 25 pts.
7. **NR7 / inside-day filter on the ORB.** Restrict rule 6 to days where the prior daily range is the narrowest of 7 or an inside day. Measure the change in 1x-OR target hit rate (folklore says 60 to 65%).
8. **Gap fill ladder.** Gaps bucketed 0.25 to 0.5%, 0.5 to 1%, >1% of prior close: P(fill same day), P(fill by 10:30), P(half-gap fill), median time to fill, separately for gap up and gap down and for open-inside vs open-outside prior day range. Rule: fade gaps < 0.5% that open inside the prior day's range, target prior close, stop 0.5x gap beyond the open; skip gaps > 1% (55% fill, 2.4 h average).
9. **Extreme-fade by depth (replicate high-of-day hold).** Session anchored 18:00 ET; new extreme = beyond prior extreme by ≥ 0.08 ATR with ≥ 0.15 ATR excursion from the open; fade = 50% retrace before session end. Measure fade rate by excursion depth (expect ~85% at 0.25 ATR, ~20% at 1.5 ATR) and by hour. Rule: fade extremes < 0.35 ATR from the open before 11:00; never fade extremes > 1 ATR after 14:00.
10. **Last-30-min momentum, gamma- and vol-conditioned.** Signal = sign(15:30 price − prior 16:00 close). Enter 15:30, exit 15:59 MOC. Measure win rate and R by: (a) all days (expect ~55%, Sharpe ~1.7 gross on index futures pooled); (b) days where |rest-of-day return| > 0.75% or ATR14 is in its top tercile; (c) vendor gamma-flip proxy if available (below flip = trade, above = skip); (d) FOMC/CPI days. Also test the contrarian version: when the first-half-hour sign disagrees with the rest-of-day sign, trade the rest-of-day sign (coefficient on first half hour flips negative).
11. **VWAP first-touch hold on labelled trend days.** On days with the 10:30 early-trend filter from rule 2 true: first touch of session VWAP after 10:30, enter in trend direction on a 1-min close back on the trend side of VWAP, stop 0.25 ATR beyond VWAP, target 1.0 ATR. Measure hold rate vs the same setup on Range-labelled days (expect a large gap; if there is none, the VWAP story is folklore on our data).
12. **Cost floor sweep.** For every rule above, re-run with friction at 2, 3, 4, 5 pts on MNQ. Any rule whose PF_net crosses 1.0 between 2 and 4 pts is not robust. Report average win in points; discard anything averaging < 25 pts on MNQ.
13. **Walk-forward and kill rule.** Fit parameters on 2019 to 2021, step quarterly through 2026, report only stitched OOS. Bootstrap the OOS trade list to get the distribution of 100-trade expectancy and max drawdown; set the live kill rule at the 5th percentile of 100-trade expectancy or 1.5x the worst bootstrapped drawdown, whichever hits first.
