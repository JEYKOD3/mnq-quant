Recurring vocabulary (consolidated; sections reference it):
- Key entry point (KEP): trendline / channel line, support or resistance (key level), or the 21-bar EMA. "Two key entry points" = two of these coinciding.
- High probability setup (HPS): two-legged pullback = second entry with the trend at a KEP; failed second entry against the trend (trap); higher-low / lower-high confirmation setup; failed breakout (fade); double top/bottom; double trap; reversal pattern (break + new extreme, push through EMA that holds, two failed attempts in the old direction).
- Trendline rule: after a break of a trendline/channel, expect a new extreme (retest of the extreme). Only after the new extreme forms does he expect "correction or straight up reversal." Applies to short-term correction trendlines too.
- Trading range rule: buy low, sell high, fade the breakouts; "most breakouts of trading ranges will fail" ("90% of the time").
- Entry trigger: 1 tick beyond the signal bar. Stop: 1 tick beyond the other side of the signal bar. Never moved. Longs only above bullish signal bars; shorts only below bearish signal bars.
- Counting: reset the count at each new extreme (new low in a downtrend, new high in an uptrend); first entry = first break of a bar's low (short) / high (long) after the extreme; pullback (a bar gets a break on the opposite side); second entry = next break in trend direction. Double tops / double bottoms also reset the count.

  - Step 3: since trend is down, "I'm looking for breaks below bars' lows." While the correction works higher and no bar gets a break below its low, there is no entry. First bearish bar that gets a break below its own low = first entry short, "first attempt by the market to resume the trend working lower."

It's creating second attempt."

  - Technical vs practical entry: a first entry short can technically trigger below a bullish bar, "but as a trader since the market is down, I want to sell below bearish bar. I want to see confirmation that sellers are in the market."
